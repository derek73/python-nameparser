# H11 — a one-letter title against the initial it spells

**When:** a one-letter word the title vocabulary holds stands in a name, as the Burmese honorific U does under a Burmese choice in "U Thein Sein".

**Then:** a one-letter word written with its period is an initial and not a title, where the letter has a capital, whatever title the same letter spells; written bare, it is the title.

- **A single letter with a capital between full stops inside a word is an initial too**, so a word cut at its full stops into initials is no title for holding the letter.
  ```
  "Grant, U.S."  [my]         →  given="U."
  "U.Thein Sein"  [my]        →  given="U."
  ```

**Because:** a one-letter title is spelled with the same letter as an initial, and the full stop is what tells them apart. An initial is an abbreviation and is written with one; an honorific written as one letter is a whole word and is not. The Burmese U is never written with a full stop, while U. is the initial of Ulysses or Ursula.

```
"U. S. Grant"  [my]         →  given="U."
"John U. Smith"  [my]       →  middle="U."
"Smith, U."  [my]           →  given="U."
"U Thein Sein"  [my]        →  title="U"  · boundary
```

**Except:**

- **A letter of a script without capitals keeps its title reading written with its period**, the full stop being how that title is written, as the Bengali ড. (doctor) is.
  ```
  "ড. রহিম উদ্দিন"            →  title="ড."
  ```

**Accepted:**

- A Korean name opening with the surname U, written bare, reads the surname as the title under a Burmese choice, as any name opening with the bare letter does there.
  ```
  "U Min Ho"  [my]            →  title="U"
  ```

---
**Signal:** shape: a one-letter word's full stop, and whether its letter has a capital
