# H11 — a one-letter title against the initial it spells

**When:** a one-letter word the title vocabulary holds stands in a name, as the Burmese honorific U does under a Burmese choice in "U Thein Sein".

**Then:** a one-letter word written with its period is an initial and not a title, where the letter has a capital, whatever title the same letter spells and wherever the word stands; written bare, it is the title.

- **A single letter with a capital cut from a word at its full stops is an initial too**, so a word of initials, or a name glued to an initial at its full stop, is no title for holding the letter.
  ```
  "Grant, U.S."  [my]         →  given="U."
  "U.Thein Sein"  [my]        →  given="U."
  "Dr.U. Smith"  [my]         →  given="U."
  ```

**Because:** a one-letter title is spelled with the same letter as an initial, and the full stop is what tells them apart. An initial is an abbreviation and is written with one; an honorific written as one letter is a whole word and is written bare as a rule, as the Burmese U is in the names #1231 measured, while U. is the initial of Ulysses or Ursula. A letter of a script without capitals is outside the rule: the full stop is how such a title is written, as the Bengali ড. (doctor) is.

```
"U. S. Grant"  [my]         →  given="U."
"John U. Smith"  [my]       →  middle="U."
"Smith, U."  [my]           →  given="U."
"U. Smith, Ed"  [my]        →  suffix="Ed"
"Vice U. Smith"  [my]       →  given="Vice"
"John (U.) Smith"  [my]     →  middle="U."
"U Thein Sein"  [my]        →  title="U"  · boundary
"ড. রহিম উদ্দিন"            →  title="ড."  · boundary
```

**Accepted:**

- A Korean name opening with the surname U, written bare, reads the surname as the title under a Burmese choice, as any name opening with the bare letter does there.
  ```
  "U Min Ho"  [my]            →  title="U"
  ```
- A Burmese U written with a full stop reads as the given name under a Burmese choice, the writing being an initial's.
  ```
  "U. Thein Aung"  [my]       →  given="U."
  ```

---
**Signal:** shape: a one-letter word written with its full stop, in a script with capitals
