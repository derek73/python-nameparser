# S1 — a bracketed credential is not a nickname

**When:** a clause enclosed in brackets or quotes stands in a name, as in "Andrew Perkins (PhD)".

**Then:** a clause wholly of words the vocabulary reads as no name is set apart from the name: it is no nickname and no maiden name, and the words around it read as they would beside a nickname.

- **Each word takes the role its vocabulary gives it**: a title is the title, a credential or a generation the suffix, one joined by a slash, a hyphen or a period (S3) too, every chunk counting, and a word that is both is the title in front of the name's first word, or in a name with no word of its own, and the suffix anywhere else. The vocabulary is asked of each word on its own, so a credential whose word is also a name (S4) is no word of such a clause unless its writing (S13) or a credential of its clause settles it (below), and neither is a title that is also a given name (H9).
  ```
  "John Smith (PhD, MBA)"     →  suffix="PhD, MBA"
  "John Smith (Dr)"           →  title="Dr"
  "John (Dr.) Smith"          →  title="Dr."
  "(Lt) John Smith"           →  title="Lt"
  "(Lt)"                      →  title="Lt"
  "John (Sr.) Smith"          →  suffix="Sr."
  "John Smith (Mr, PhD)"      →  title="Mr"
  "John Smith (MD/PhD)"       →  suffix="MD/PhD"
  "John Smith (MD-PhD)"       →  suffix="MD-PhD"
  "Smith (M.D./Ph.D.), John"  →  suffix="M.D./Ph.D."
  "John Smith (King)"         →  nickname="King"  · boundary
  "John Smith (MD/Bob)"       →  nickname="MD/Bob"  · boundary
  "John Smith (MA/MBA)"       →  nickname="MA/MBA"  · boundary
  ```
- **The words around it read as they would beside a nickname**, so a clause between name words ends where its brackets do and takes neither neighbour, and a lone name word beside it is the family name (N3).
  ```
  "John Smith (PhD) Withers"  →  family="Withers"
  "John Smith (PhD) Withers"  →  suffix="PhD"
  "W. Russell (Jr.) Withers"  →  suffix="Jr."
  "Jack (PhD)"                →  family="Jack"
  ```
- **A comma inside the clause parts its words as a comma between them would**, though in a nickname it is kept as written (N1), and the comma is no part of the word it is written against, with a space beside it or none, where it stands between two words: a comma standing alone is a word of its own, and one written before another comma or a character tokenize drops (T1) ends no word. A comma is any character C's Background counts as one, each reading here as the ASCII one does.
  ```
  "John Smith (USN, Ret.)"    →  suffix="USN, Ret."
  "John Smith (Dr., PhD)"     →  title="Dr."
  "John Smith (Dr.፣ PhD)"     →  title="Dr."
  "(PhD، Sr) John Smith"      →  title="Sr"
  "John Smith (MD ,PhD)"      →  suffix="MD, PhD"
  "John Smith (Jr.,) III"     →  suffix="Jr., III"
  "John Smith (MD,) (PhD)"    →  suffix="MD, PhD"
  "John Smith (MD,PhD)"       →  suffix="MD, PhD"
  "John Smith (Dr.，PhD)"     →  title="Dr."
  "Jane (Janey,Jo) Smith"     →  nickname="Janey,Jo"  · boundary
  ```
- **A member of the ambiguous class (S4) is a word of such a clause where its writing settles it after the name**, as S13 settles a member standing alone, wherever in the clause it stands. A member its writing leaves unsettled is one too where a credential of the same clause settles it: the brackets hold one kind of thing, and a credential that is no name, or a member its periods settle, says which. A joined word settles one where a chunk of it would. A generation, a numeral, an honorific, a word that is a title too and a member its capitals settle say nothing of the kind: `Ed Jr` is a name a person goes by, and a joined chunk is never settled by its case (S3).
  ```
  "John Smith (Ph.D., MBA)"   →  suffix="Ph.D., MBA"
  "John Smith (MBA, PhD)"     →  suffix="MBA, PhD"
  "John Smith (PhD, M.A)"     →  suffix="PhD, M.A"
  "JOHN SMITH (PHD, MBA)"     →  suffix="PHD, MBA"
  "John Smith (Ma, PhD)"      →  suffix="Ma, PhD"
  "John Smith (Ma PhD)"       →  suffix="Ma PhD"
  "John Smith (Ma, Esq)"      →  suffix="Ma, Esq"
  "John Smith (M.A, Ma)"      →  suffix="M.A, Ma"
  "John Smith (Ma, Ed)"       →  nickname="Ma, Ed"  · boundary
  "John Smith (MA, Ed)"       →  nickname="MA, Ed"  · boundary
  "Edward Smith (Ed Jr)"      →  nickname="Ed Jr"  · boundary
  "John Smith (Ma, Ms)"       →  nickname="Ma, Ms"  · boundary
  "John Smith (Ed, Jr/Sr)"    →  nickname="Ed, Jr/Sr"  · boundary
  "John Smith (Dr, Ma)"       →  nickname="Dr, Ma"  · boundary
  "John (Ma, PhD) Smith"      →  nickname="Ma, PhD"  · boundary
  ```

**Because:** brackets and quotes set off more than nicknames. A writer brackets a credential, a generation or a title to keep it apart from the name, and the words the vocabulary reads as no name say which one the brackets hold. Where every word is one of them, the brackets have drawn the clause's edges, so the name around it reads as it does beside any clause it holds apart.

```
"Andrew Perkins (PhD)"      →  suffix="PhD"
"Andrew Perkins «PhD»"      →  suffix="PhD"
"Andrew Perkins (Andy)"     →  nickname="Andy"  · boundary
```

**Except:**

- **A clause of nothing but title modifiers and styles** is no title: a modifier needs a head (H9), and a bracketed one more often qualifies a credential.
  ```
  "John Smith (Hon)"          →  nickname="Hon"
  "John Smith (Hon, Vice)"    →  nickname="Hon, Vice"
  "Andrew Wilson, MRCP (UK)"  →  nickname="UK"
  ```
- **A lone title of address** keeps its pair's reading: a title addressing the given name, or a salutation that may end the given part (H8), may be the name the person goes by.
  ```
  "John Smith (Sir)"          →  nickname="Sir"
  "John Smith (Mr)"           →  nickname="Mr"
  "สุดา ใจดี (นาย)"            →  nickname="นาย"
  ```
- **A clause of titles alone straight behind a credential** keeps its pair's reading: it qualifies that credential, a specialty or a college, more often than it titles the name.
  ```
  "Alex Green, FRCS (SN)"     →  nickname="SN"
  "John Smith, MD (Sr)"       →  suffix="MD Sr"  · boundary
  ```
- **A credential written as initials before a name word of its comma part** keeps its pair's reading: one or two letters with or without a period, or single letters each with its period, there are more likely the initials the person goes by, S13's reason for a member inside the name. A credential written as initials before a name word stays the nickname, and so does any word of its clause; a word that is a title too counts only written with a period after each letter, or in capitals in a name of mixed case, capitals in a name written in one case saying nothing (S6), and a generation word not at all; a credential S3 reads through chunks joined by a slash, a hyphen or a period counts where any of its chunks would, as its words written apart do, and one the vocabulary lists whole is one word, as is each run of its chunks the vocabulary lists whole (S3).
  ```
  "Justin (JP) Stearns"       →  nickname="JP"
  "Justin (R.N.) Stearns"     →  nickname="R.N."
  "Justin (MD.) Stearns"      →  nickname="MD."
  "Justin (JP, PhD) Stearns"  →  nickname="JP, PhD"
  "John (,JP) Smith"          →  nickname=",JP"
  "Justin (JP/MD) Stearns"    →  nickname="JP/MD"
  "Justin (JP.MD) Stearns"    →  nickname="JP.MD"
  "John (MD-PhD) Smith"       →  nickname="MD-PhD"
  "Robert (RA) Allen"         →  nickname="RA"
  "ROBERT (RA) ALLEN"         →  suffix="RA"  · boundary
  "John (Sr) Smith"           →  suffix="Sr"  · boundary
  "John (Jr/Sr) Smith"        →  suffix="Jr/Sr"  · boundary
  "John (NP-C) Smith"         →  suffix="NP-C"  · boundary
  "Jane (PhD/NP-C) Smith"     →  suffix="PhD/NP-C"  · boundary
  "Smith (MD), John"          →  suffix="MD"  · boundary
  ```
- **A clause holding a word the vocabulary reads as a name, or does not know**, is no such clause, and reads as its pair reads it, a nickname in a nickname pair. A member of the ambiguous class alone is S13's.
  ```
  "John Smith (PhD, Bob)"     →  nickname="PhD, Bob"
  "John Smith (Ma)"           →  nickname="Ma"
  "John Smith (Ma)"           →  ambiguities=("suffix-or-nickname",)
  ```
- **A clause that is not such a clause and ends in an ASCII period** loses its brackets and reads exactly as if written bare, the escape v1 made for any period-final content.
  ```
  "John Smith (Xyz.)"         →  family="Xyz."
  "John Smith (M.A.)"         →  suffix="M.A."
  ```

**Precedence:**

- **A clause in a pair configured for maiden names** ("Andrew Perkins (PhD)" under a maiden pair): this rule takes it first, and the clause is no maiden name (M1).
- **A bracketed clause led by a maiden marker** ("Jane Smith (née Jr.)"): the marker is no word of such a clause, so this rule leaves it to M3, and a period ending it drops the brackets as the period escape does; M3's own Precedence bullet records that reading.
- **A member of the ambiguous class alone after the name whose writing settles it** ("John Smith (MA)"): S13 reads it as the suffix, by the same set-apart reading.

**Accepted:**

- A lone title of address written short with its period stays in the name as written bare, its clause no nickname for the period ending it, and where the bare word ending the name reads as a name word, so does it ('สุดา ใจดี ศ.' reads family 'ศ.').
  ```
  "สุดา ใจดี (ศ.)"             →  family="ศ."
  "สุดา ใจดี (ดร.)"            →  title="ดร."
  ```
- A clause a credential settles a member of, ending in an ASCII period, takes the period escape, as one holding a member its writing settles already did: the escape is decided before it is known where the name ends, which the member's reading needs, so the clause reads as if written bare, the member then read by position.
  ```
  "John Smith (Ma, Ph.D.)"    →  family="Ma"
  "John Smith (M.A, Ph.D.)"   →  family="M.A"
  ```
- A clause between name words reads otherwise than the same words bare: the bare generation joins the middle name (S8) and the bare credential starts the run to the end of the name, and a lone name word beside a bare credential is the given name.
  ```
  "W. Russell Jr. Withers"    →  middle="Russell Jr."
  "Jack PhD"                  →  given="Jack"
  ```
- A title word that is a name or a tag in a writer's data reads as the title: the vocabulary is asked, and a short title is not refused for being short.
  ```
  "CASHION, JOHN (ST)"        →  title="ST"
  "John Smith (Lady)"         →  title="Lady"
  ```
- A title-only clause behind a credential keeps its pair's reading even where it holds a title of the name, and loses the period escape it took before.
  ```
  "Louise Dye, PhD (Professor)"  →  nickname="Professor"
  "John Smith, MD (Dr.)"      →  nickname="Dr."
  ```
- A clause set apart from a name with no word of its own leaves the name empty, where the same word bare is made the name (H4), and a title set apart before a comma reads as the title in front of the one name word after it (H1).
  ```
  "(MPH)"                     →  suffix="MPH"
  "MPH"                       →  given="MPH"
  "(Dr), John"                →  family="John"
  ```
- A credential joined by a slash or a hyphen is one word, so it reads otherwise than its words written apart where one of them reads by a rule of its own: a member of the ambiguous class whose position S13 does not settle, and a word that is a title too where it would be the title.
  ```
  "(M.A./PhD)"                →  suffix="M.A./PhD"
  "(M.A., PhD)"               →  nickname="M.A., PhD"
  "(PhD/Sr) John Smith"       →  suffix="PhD/Sr"
  "(PhD, Sr) John Smith"      →  title="Sr"
  ```
- A comma standing alone in a clause, as its own word, is a word that no vocabulary reads, so the clause keeps its pair's reading, where the same comma written against a word parts the words; glued to the word behind it, it opens that word and reads as it.
  ```
  "John Smith (MD , PhD)"     →  nickname="MD , PhD"
  "John Smith (, PhD)"        →  nickname=", PhD"
  "John Smith (,PhD)"         →  suffix="PhD"
  ```
- A qualifier written after a title in front of the name is the suffix it is in the vocabulary.
  ```
  "Col. (Ret.) Smith"         →  suffix="Ret."
  ```

---
**Signal:** shape: a bracketed or quoted clause every word of which the vocabulary reads as no name + neighbor: a credential in front, a name word behind, a credential in the clause settling a member + edge full stop: the period escape
