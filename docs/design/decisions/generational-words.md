# generational-words — junior and senior in other languages (2026-10-14, #661)

Closes #661. 31 spelled-out generational words join SUFFIX_WORDS, and one predicate moves with them (the run-start bullet below), so `Antonio Pavlović mlađi` reads family Pavlović, suffix mlađi, where 1.4.0 and 2.4.2 read middle Pavlović, family mlađi (checked against both wheels). The class is decided once — a word meaning junior/senior (or the Younger/the Elder) written spaced AFTER the name — and each word was then checked two ways. First, collisions: a suffix claim acts on the trailing word, so the risk is a borne name there, and `tools/name_usage.py` found none (US Census, SSA, Wikidata; nuorempi's one Wikidata bearer of each kind is noise, as the issue said). Second, usage, because the issue's list came partly from ilib's machine-translated test data: each word was found in the trailing spaced position in real titles on its own language's Wikipedia (2026-10-13, e.g. "Hans Holbein mlađi", "Плиний Младший", "Pieter Brueghel vanhempi", "बलबीर सिंह सीनियर"). The per-word list, grouped by language, is the #661 block in nameparser/config/suffixes.py. Feminine forms ship beside the masculine wherever the language has them (mlađa, младшая, молодша, jaunesnioji); lt vyresnioji is the grammatical feminine with no titled bearer found, and ships on grammar. ru/uk and cs/sk were not in the issue and were added on the same evidence (Derek, 2026-10-14).

- **CLDR is corroboration, not a source.** Its person-name samples write mlađi/млађи (hr, sr — on a woman's name too), младши/младша (bg), ifj. (hu) and जूनियर (hi), but its `generation` field also holds translations of the field LABEL rather than of the word: isizukulwane (zu), जेनेरेसन (ne), نسل (ps) and jẹnereṣọn (pcm) all mean "generation". A word is not added because CLDR has it. The wider roster (fils, yngri, الابن and the rest) is open at [#679](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/679), to be vetted word by word the same way.
- **Excluded, each with its reason.** kıdemli (tr): a rank word (Kıdemli onbaşı); Turkish writes Jr. or a parenthesis, (oğul). sóisearach/sinsearach (ga): rank words too; Irish writes Óg and Mór, which are also inside given names (Seán Óg Ó hAilpín), so they are a separate decision and not taken here. ifj./id. (hu): written BEFORE the name (ifj. Kovács János, which H2's shape rule already reads as a title), and a bare `id` would make any trailing ID a suffix. млада (bg feminine of млади): Mlada is a borne given name, and under a family-first order the given name is the trailing word. ml./мл./st.: the abbreviations stay under the #269 deferral in suffixes.py, st. colliding with Saint besides.
- **Spaced only, and what that leaves.** bg, ru and uk write a modern Jr. hyphen-glued (Теодор Рузвелт-младши, Дональд Трамп-молодший), which no whole-token set reaches; for those three the gain is mainly the historical the Elder/the Younger names, written title case (Младший), which the lowercase fold matches.
- **The #602 run start reads the letters' script, not ASCII (Derek chose the Latin-script test, 2026-10-14). SUPERSEDED the same day by #679 (decisions/S2): no script is refused now, so the split this bullet accepts is gone.** rules/S2 lets "an unambiguous Latin suffix word of two or more letters" start the run, and `_pieces.starts_a_credential_run` had implemented Latin as `letters.isascii()`, exact while no accented Latin word was suffix vocabulary. This entry adds the first ones (mlađi, mlađa, mladší, starší), so without a change `Pieter Bruegel stariji Jones` would read suffix 'stariji Jones' while `Antonio Pavlović mlađi Jones` kept family Jones -- one language, one construction, split by the `đ`. The test now asks a non-ASCII word whether every letter's Unicode name is a LATIN one; the ASCII test stays first, so an ASCII word pays no frame for it. The same script test is what refuses the non-Latin honorifics (`Taro Tanaka さん Jones` keeps family Jones), and it refuses this entry's Cyrillic and Devanagari generational words with them, though the reason S2 gives for the refusal -- an honorific carries no credential mark -- is not true of them. So Serbian splits by script: `Ana Horvat stariji Jones` reads suffix 'stariji Jones' and `Ана Хорват старији Џонс` family Џонс, and after a comma C1 asks the same predicate, so `Ана Хорват, старији Џонс` reads given 'старији'. ACCEPTED for this entry (Derek, 2026-10-14) and stated at rules/S2's Accepted clause: the vocabulary does not mark which suffix words are honorifics, a script test is the only line available, and only a name word written after the generational word reaches it. Settling it is open at [#679](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/679), where the non-Latin generational words multiply. The first draft of this entry credited the ASCII test to S2's honorific exclusion instead and proposed deleting it, on the evidence that deleting it moved no test and no corpus name. The design-docs review found the attribution wrong and the evidence empty: nothing pinned the test, and deleting it reads `Taro Tanaka さん Jones` as suffix 'さん Jones' and `Гай Плиний Старший Иванов` as suffix 'Старший Иванов'. Both are rules/S2 boundary examples now. #678 was filed on the first draft and corrected.
- **Measurement (2026-10-14).** Six corpus names move, all in corpus_cldr.jsonl and all the CLDR hr/sr renderings carrying mlađi/млађи, each gaining suffix mlađi (or млађи) from a name field; cldr_agreement records 14 changes, every one toward CLDR's fields. The bg and hi renderings do not move because those locales' formats drop the generation field. The run-start change moves no name the corpus held before this entry; its one mover is S2's own `Antonio Pavlović mlađi Jones`. Of the 161 rows of tools/differential/cldr_fields.jsonl whose `generation` value `cldr_agreement.occurs()` in the text, none writing a non-ASCII generation word has a name word after it in the same part (two write given names after `II` across a comma, which ends the run's part). Recompute: `uv run python tools/differential/snapshot.py --since <the commit before this entry>`.

- **2026-10-14 #679 — the rest of CLDR's roster, vetted word by word (Derek).** 76 more words join SUFFIX_WORDS (counted against master's set, none removed): the #679 block in suffixes.py, 30-odd languages. Vetting as above, run by three research passes over each language's Wikipedia (langlinks of the Jr./Sr./the Younger/the Elder articles, then the language's own search) and `tools/name_usage.py`. CLDR's `generation` field proved unreliable in about a third of languages, beyond the field-label translations: an adjective written BEFORE the name (ta இளைய, kn ಕಿರಿ, si බාල, az Kiçik, uz Kichik, tk kiçi, ba кесе, ky Кенже, kk кіші), an abbreviation nobody writes after a name (mr ज्यु., pa ਜੂ., kok धा.), a word that means "second" (chr ᏔᎵᏁ, hi द्वितीय, zh 二世 — regnal, and glued), or a word no Wikipedia uses where another is standard. Where the language's Wikipedia writes a different word after names, that word ships instead of CLDR's: lo ຈູເນຍ for ຮຸ່ນລູກ, ti ጁንየር (and am ጁኒየር) for ማንጁስ, or ଜୁନିଅର/ଜୁନିୟର୍ for ଜେଆର୍, ta இளையவர் and ஜூனியர், si ජූනියර්, mk помладиот for јуниор. EXCLUDED, each with its reason: he סניור, which Israelis bear as a surname (אלי סניור and בוריס סניור, found on he Wikipedia), and ja シニア, whose only evidence is weaker: `tools/name_usage.py` gives both spellings the same 96 Wikidata bearers as Latin Senior, with the same citizenships, because Wikidata's Senior surname item carries he and ja labels, so the count measures the English surname and not Japanese writing; left out anyway (Derek, 2026-10-14), beside its Hebrew twin — English `senior` is no suffix either, Senior being a surname (3,864 US Census); hy Ավագ (an Armenian given name, in lists that write the surname first); km ជូនៀ (a given name; ជូនៀរ ships); Ajala (a Yoruba surname); kk Джуниор and mk јуниор (a given name or a club name there); mn Жр (the initials ЖР); be мал. (also "figure"); lt jaun.; the single-letter abbreviations mr क. and as ক., which are the initial shape; ne/kok जेआर (rare; also JR); fa پدر/پسر ("father"/"son" without the article that marks ar الابن and he הבן as never a name); and the CLDR forms that are label translations or unattested (cv, chr, ak, blo, kok-Latin, kk-Arabic). PACK-SHAPED, NOT SHIPPED: fr fils/père and the Latin abbreviations ml./st. (cs, sk, sl, hr) are real and standard, but their collisions are with OTHER languages written in the same script — `Marie Fils` (767 US Census bearers of Fils) and `Jiří Dienstbier ml.` beside ML initials and St. as Saint — which is the case a locale pack exists for (the #604 `nic` precedent). No fr or cs pack exists; proposing them is open at [#684](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/684). The Cyrillic мл. does ship: ru, bg and sr all write it after names and nothing collides with it in Cyrillic, so a pack would protect nobody; ст. is barely attested after a name and stays out. Neither existing pack takes anything: tr and az put the junior word BEFORE the name or in a parenthesis, and Turkish baba collides inside Turkish. Measured 2026-10-14: 16 corpus names move, all CLDR renderings in is, ar, fa, gu, kn, ml, ru and cv (cv's rendering through мл.) gaining their generation word as the suffix out of a name field; cldr_agreement's default view records 4 suffix fields newly agreeing (cv, fa, is, ru) and no regression, and 40 still-wrong fields changing across the 16 renderings: 12 suffixes now the generation word alone where CLDR also holds the comma-led credential (`yngri` for `yngri þingmaður`), and 28 family, family_base and given_names fields that lost the word but still disagree. No rendering newly agrees on every role. The Burmese sample glues the Burmese comma ၊ (U+104A) to ဂျူနီယာ and is not reached, the tokenizer not splitting on it.
