# S3 — credentials written in joined chunks

## Standing

- A dotted token loses its chunk claim only where every chunk the vocabulary matches is a single ASCII character (`i`, `v`, `2`): `John Smith R.A.I.` then reads suffix 'R.A.I.' by shape, with a report, and `Jack X.Y.I.` keeps family 'X.Y.I.', while `Msc.Ed.`, `JD.CPA`, `Lt.Gov.` and `J.씨` keep their chunk readings; retiring every chunk match was declined — 2026-09-15.
- A digit-dotted token (`1.4.2`) loses the chunk claim and is no member of the shape class either, so it reads as a name word, silently (`John Smith 1.4.2` → family '1.4.2') — 2026-09-18.
- An ambiguous acronym written with its periods, one after each letter or one after each of two or more letter chunks, counts as unambiguous, and a single trailing period does not (`Jack M.A.` → suffix, `Jack Ma.` → family); the clause stood in rules/S2 until it moved here — 2026-10-19.
- Paired initials of a script with capitals (two single letters each followed by a period, the second optional) are no word of the ambiguous class at the trailing slots and read as the name word they are written as, reported, as the switch off reads them: `Ram Bahadur B.K.` → middle 'Bahadur', family 'B.K.'; `Bahadur, Ram B.K.` → middle 'B.K.'; `Ram Bahadur B.K., PhD` → family 'B.K.', suffix 'PhD'. At the first slot after a comma they stay C5's (`John Smith, X.Y. P.Q.` → suffix), behind an unambiguous suffix word they join the run (`Ram Bahadur PhD B.K.` → suffix 'PhD B.K.') while behind an ambiguous or by-shape credential with no unambiguous word in front of it they end the name and take it in (`Jane Doe MA R.T.` → middle 'Doe MA', family 'R.T.'), and three dotted letters, longer chunks and a caseless script's pair keep the position reading (`John Smith X.Y.Z.`, `John Smith B.Tech.`, `สมชาย ใจดี พ.บ.` → suffix) — 2026-10-24 #864.
- A word joined by a hyphen or a slash reads as a suffix where every chunk counts as a suffix as written, listed and not a bare member of the ambiguous class, a chunk's own periods counting as in a word of its own: `John Smith, MD/PhD` → suffix 'MD/PhD', `John Smith MD-PhD` → suffix 'MD-PhD', `John Smith, M.D./Ph.D.` → suffix; one chunk that does not count leaves the word as it was (`John Smith MD-Jones` → family, `John Smith, MA/PhD` → given 'MA/PhD', family 'John Smith'), a word of single-ASCII-character chunks is none (`Jack I-V` → family 'I-V'), a hyphen-joined word written as a hyphenated name is (first chunk in Title case, the rest in Title case or lower case) is a name word (`Kim, Ae-Ra`, `Shin Ae-ra` keep the given and family), while capitals keep the joined reading (`KIM, AE-RA` → suffix), and a joiner standing alone or glued to one side, a dash other than the hyphen-minus and a joined split credential make no joined word — 2026-10-25 #752.

Open: none in this file.

- 2026-10-25 #752 — JOINED CHUNKS: A WORD JOINED BY A HYPHEN OR A SLASH IS A CREDENTIAL WHERE EVERY CHUNK IS ONE (phase 2c, item 5 of decisions/vocabulary-model's 2026-10-23 #752 entry, agreed by Derek on 2026-10-23). The test is asked where a word's suffix reading as written is asked, so classify's tag, the comma's decision and every predicate testing a word's text read the joined word alike. A chunk is read as a word of its own, so a chunk written with its periods counts (`M.D./Ph.D.`, and `M.A./Ph.D.`, the ambiguous member written so). - 2026-10-25 #752 (Derek) — A HYPHENATED NAME'S WRITING IS A FORM CUE. SUPERSEDES the review entry's (ii), which accepted `Kim, Ae-Ra` reading suffix 'Ae-Ra'. Derek: "refuse a hyphen join whose every chunk is Title case and then mark AE-RA as the accepted". A word joined by a hyphen whose first chunk is in Title case (one capital, then lower case, its periods set aside) and whose every other chunk is in Title case or lower case is a name word whatever its chunks are listed as, the design's cue that Title case leans to the name (the 2026-10-23 #752 entry's form-cue table, decisions/vocabulary-model). Its review (2026-10-25) narrowed and stated three edges: a word holding a slash anywhere takes no cue (`Kim, Ae-Ra/Md` keeps the joined reading); a one-letter later chunk in lower case is a name's tail like any other (`Md-v`); and a word holding an interior period is read by its periods first, so the cue never sees it (`Kim, Ae.-Ra.` → suffix by the chunk rule, an unwritten shape). The lower-case tail is this change's widening of Derek's wording, because the Revised Romanization writes a given name's second syllable in lower case (`Shin Ae-ra`), the spelling the code review's case was in; `Ae-Ra` and `Ae-ra` both keep the name. Left to the every-chunk test: a chunk with a capital past its first letter (`PhD-Md` → suffix), a chunk in capitals (`MD-PhD`), a word opening in lower case (`md-phd`, the lower-case writing of a credential) and a slash, which joins no name; no local corpus holds a slash-joined credential in Title case, so the slash's exemption is decided by what the cue is for rather than by a count.
