# S3 — credentials written in joined chunks

## Standing

- A dotted token loses its chunk claim only where every chunk the vocabulary matches is a single ASCII character (`i`, `v`, `2`): `John Smith R.A.I.` then reads suffix 'R.A.I.' by shape, with a report, and `Jack X.Y.I.` keeps family 'X.Y.I.', while `Msc.Ed.`, `JD.CPA`, `Lt.Gov.` and `J.씨` keep their chunk readings; retiring every chunk match was declined — 2026-09-15.
- A digit-dotted token (`1.4.2`) loses the chunk claim and is no member of the shape class either, so it reads as a name word, silently (`John Smith 1.4.2` → family '1.4.2') — 2026-09-18.
- An ambiguous acronym written with its periods, one after each letter or one after each of two or more letter chunks, counts as unambiguous, and a single trailing period does not (`Jack M.A.` → suffix, `Jack Ma.` → family); the clause stood in rules/S2 until it moved here — 2026-10-19.
- Paired initials of a script with capitals (two single letters each followed by a period, the second optional) are no word of the ambiguous class at the trailing slots and read as the name word they are written as, reported, as the switch off reads them: `Ram Bahadur B.K.` → middle 'Bahadur', family 'B.K.'; `Bahadur, Ram B.K.` → middle 'B.K.'; `Ram Bahadur B.K., PhD` → family 'B.K.', suffix 'PhD'. At the first slot after a comma they stay C5's (`John Smith, X.Y. P.Q.` → suffix), behind an unambiguous suffix word they join the run (`Ram Bahadur PhD B.K.` → suffix 'PhD B.K.') while behind an ambiguous or by-shape credential with no unambiguous word in front of it they end the name and take it in (`Jane Doe Ma R.T.` → middle 'Doe Ma', family 'R.T.'; a member its capitals settle starts the run instead, `Jane Doe MA R.T.` → suffix 'MA R.T.', 2026-10-25 #785), and three dotted letters, longer chunks and a caseless script's pair keep the position reading (`John Smith X.Y.Z.`, `John Smith B.Tech.`, `สมชาย ใจดี พ.บ.` → suffix) — 2026-10-24 #864.
- A word joined by a hyphen or a slash reads as a suffix where every chunk counts as a suffix as written, listed and not a bare member of the ambiguous class (save one another chunk settles, 2026-11-07 #1138), a chunk's own periods counting as in a word of its own: `John Smith, MD/PhD` → suffix 'MD/PhD', `John Smith MD-PhD` → suffix 'MD-PhD', `John Smith, M.D./Ph.D.` → suffix; one chunk that does not count leaves the word as it was (`John Smith MD-Jones` → family, `John Smith, MA/Mba` → given 'MA/Mba', family 'John Smith'), a word of single-ASCII-character chunks is none (`Jack I-V` → family 'I-V'), a hyphen-joined word written as a hyphenated name is (first chunk in Title case, the rest in Title case or lower case) is a name word (`Kim, Ae-Ra`, `Shin Ae-ra` keep the given and family), while capitals keep the joined reading (`KIM, AE-RA` → suffix), and a joiner standing alone or glued to one side, a dash other than the hyphen-minus and a joined split credential make no joined word — 2026-10-25 #752.
- A run of chunks the vocabulary lists whole, its slash or hyphen included, is one credential: a joined word reads as a suffix where it can be cut at some of its joiners into chunks that each count, such a run counting as one, the cut with the fewest chunks taken, so `Jane Smith PhD/NP-C` → suffix 'PhD/NP-C', `Jane Smith CCC/SLP/L` → suffix (`CCC`, `SLP/L`), `Jane Smith MS-OTR/L` and `Jane Smith APRN-FNP-C` → suffix; a word with no such cut still refuses (`Jane Smith CPNP-PC/AC` → family; `MA/CCC-SLP`, refused here too, reads since 2026-11-07 #1138), and a word written as a hyphenated name is read by its writing before any cut (`Kim, Ae-Ra`) — 2026-11-06 #1103.
- A member of the ambiguous class is a chunk of a joined word too, however it is written, where another chunk settles it, a credential that is no name (a generation or a numeral not one) or a member its periods settle: `John Smith MA/PhD`, `M.A/PhD`, `MA/CCC-SLP` and `MA-CCC-SLP` → suffix, where each was the family; members alone (`MA/Mba`), a generation beside a member (`MA/Jr`) and a name word (`MA/Jones`) still refuse it. A joined word holding a member is a member itself in brackets, so S13 settles it by position: `John Smith (MA/PhD)` → suffix, `John (MA/PhD) Smith` → nickname, reported, as `(MA, PhD)` reads in each place — 2026-11-07 #1138.
