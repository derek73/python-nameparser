# O7 — two given names

## Standing

- A compound given name is an opt-in `Policy` switch, `two_given_names`, off by default, which a pack's `[rules]` may turn on (`two_given_names = true`): given-first it is the same string shape as a name with a middle name, and `middle` keeps its meaning — 2026-10-30 #704.
- The given name takes ONE unit, the middle unit beside it (after it, or before it under FAMILY_FIRST_GIVEN_LAST), counted as rules/O6 counts a name word, so a particle chain the parse left in the middle moves whole; a given name already holding two words keeps what it has — 2026-10-30 #704.
- The switch reads after `two_family_names` and before `middle_as_family`, and is refused beside neither — 2026-10-30 #704.
- A particle chain running to the end of the name stays the family under the switch (`Maria do Carmo Silva`); reading it as a given name is not this rule's — 2026-10-30 #704.
- The switch reports nothing — 2026-10-30 #704.

## Log

- 2026-10-30 #704 — THE COMPOUND GIVEN NAME IS O6'S MIRROR: A SWITCH THAT GIVES THE GIVEN NAME THE MIDDLE UNIT BESIDE IT. Carries out sub-phase 4g of the 2026-10-28 #754 output-model entry in decisions/vocabulary-model (its item 7: `middle` keeps its meaning, a compound given name is a rule a caller or pack turns on). Field name `two_given_names`, after `two_family_names`, whose shape it mirrors (mechanisms.md#DECLARED-SPAN).
  What the rule settles, and why each way:
  - **One unit, not the whole given part.** The given name takes the next unit and later middles stay middles (`Ana María José Pérez` → middle `José`), as O6 takes one word: a given name of two names is the convention named in #704 (`María José`, `Juan Carlos`, CLDR's `Cornelia Margarita` beside `given2` `Sofia Aletta`), and one unit keeps the field CLDR calls `given2`. Weighed against: every given-part word into `given`, which empties `middle` for every name under the switch and reads CLDR's `given2` into `given`.
  - **The count.** A unit is counted as rules/O6 counts one, so `María del Carmen de la Vega` gives given `María del Carmen`: P2 stopped the first chain where the second particle opened its own. A given name counting two words already (a hyphen, a P5 pair) keeps what it has; what is taken must count as one and hold a letter, and the given name must hold a letter too (the PSC run found forename boxes holding a house number or a lone mark, `11 Elizabeth Mcguinness`, `. Ajay Singh`). An initial is taken, being a given name written short, the reverse of O6, which refuses one because it writes a given name.
  - **A patronymic is no given name.** Under an ending rule (rules/R9), a word the rule reads as a patronymic, and a Turkic marker's father, stay in the middle (`Ivan Ivanovich Ivanov` under the East Slavic rule keeps middle `Ivanovich`); with no rule on, a patronymic is a name word and joins, as O6 accepts.
  - **Orders and commas.** A family comma lays the part after it out given-first, so the switch reads it (`Pérez, Juan Carlos`), an empty head (C15) included; no comma, the switch stands down where the name's script set its order (W4), as O6 does. Under FAMILY_FIRST_GIVEN_LAST the unit beside the given name is the one before it, so the two stay in written order.
  - **Precedence.** O6 reads first: turning both on says the family holds two names, and O6 takes its word before this rule takes from what is left. Measured in ORCID's records from Spain, Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Peru and Ecuador whose two boxes hold three words with no particle or hyphen, the family box holds two of them in 73% (Ecuador) to 93% (Peru) of the records (Spain 6,586 of 7,433), and where they hold four, two and two in 96% to 99.9% (Spain 2,484 of 2,582; 2026-10-30, the fetch of 2026-10-27), so O6-first reads `Gabriel García Márquez` and `María José García López` as those writers split them. Argentina (2+1 in 7,184 of 8,457 three-word records), the United States and the United Kingdom go the other way, which is why neither switch is a default. `middle_as_family` (O3) reads after, as P5's join reads before it: the given name is decided first, and the fold takes the middles left.
  - **The particle chain ending the name is out of scope.** The RFC's measured gap for `pt` is `Maria do Carmo Silva` (given `Maria`, family `do Carmo Silva`), where P2 chains every word after the particle into the family. Splitting that chain needs to know which particle phrases complete a given name, and the shape is `João da Silva Santos`'s, whose family it is. Measured in ORCID's records from Spain, Portugal, Brazil and 19 Latin American countries with a family box filled (2026-10-30): 5,506 given boxes hold a particle inside, against 5,625 family boxes that open with one; the given boxes' objects are mostly Marian titles (`Carmen` 1,283, `Jesús` 454 and `Jesus` 245, `los Ángeles` 564 in two spellings, `Pilar` 324, `Rosario` 260, `la Caridad` 230), several of which open family boxes too (`Jesus` 65, `Rosario` 35, `Valle` 47, `Conceição` 24, against 19 in given boxes). So the split is a question of vocabulary, which word completes a given name after which particle, and is left Open below rather than guessed at by shape.
