# O6 — two family names

## Standing

- Two family names are an opt-in `Policy` switch, `two_family_names`, off by default: the convention is Spain's and most of Latin America's but not Argentina's, and given-first it is the same string shape as a name with a middle name, so no default can read it — 2026-10-24 #754.
- `locales.ES` turns the switch on, carrying the Spanish given-name list beside it, one pack for Spain and Latin America alike, Argentina included; `locales.PT` leaves it off until a Portuguese given-name list exists, decided in chat — 2026-10-30 #753, phase 3g.
- `locales.CS` and `locales.SK` turn the switch on with no given-name list, a net gain in every real population measured (CLDR's fictional renderings lose), two given names the recorded cost; `locales.SL` and `locales.HR` leave it off, decided in chat — 2026-10-30 #753, phase 3f amended.
- Both family names stay in `family` through 2.x; a second-surname field is the RFC's Phase 4 output model (docs/design/vocabulary-model-rfc.md, "The rules a pack can choose"), and a pack may later switch this rule on — 2026-10-24 #754; landed as the `surname2` view, `family` keeping both, 2026-10-29 #754, sub-phase 4d.
- The family's words are counted as P1's fold counts them, a connective join and a hyphen counting as their words, so a family already holding two keeps what it has; the word taken must count as one by the same count and be no initial written alone — 2026-10-24 #754.
- A name its script (W4) reads in an order other than the declared `name_order` is left alone; where the two agree it is read like any other — 2026-10-24 #754.
- Under a family-first order the first two name words are the family, the remainder laid out as the positions after a family — 2026-10-24 #754.
- The switch runs before `middle_as_family` and is not refused beside it: given-first, the two together read as `middle_as_family` alone — 2026-10-24 #754.
- The switch reports nothing — 2026-10-24 #754.
- The switch reports no alternative either, so the reading it did not take is not surfaced: the caller declared the convention — 2026-10-27 #752 (Derek).
- A listed given name (`Lexicon.given_names`) standing alone is not taken, under every order, and the name then reads as with the switch off; a listed word behind a particle is taken as before, and with the list empty the switch reads as before; no other rule reads the list, the default carries none, and the veto reports nothing — 2026-10-30 #959.
- The veto stays a clause of this rule though it reads a second signal, the taken word looked up in a list, and the rule is a split candidate — 2026-10-30 #959, decided.
- The Spanish list, which `locales.ES` carries as its lexicon fragment since sub-phase 3g (a caller may pass a list of its own), is generated, not per-word vocabulary: INE's 2025 census tables, pooled over every name borne in Spain, cut at a measured rank (1,000 words, 90% given use) by `tools/es_given_names.py`; a change to it is a cutoff change — 2026-10-30 #959.

Open: hyphenated surnames block the switch (`Andrés Sánchez-Cid Torres` keeps `Sánchez-Cid` a middle name), of the 466 Spanish records the issue's held-out per-country list still read wrong, 271 are this shape (measured on the issue, 2026-10-30) — #959. No rule example shows both switches on over a given name a particle completes (`María del Carmen de la Vega`, given `María del Carmen` under O7 alone and family `del Carmen de la Vega` with O6 on too) — #959. Why the Spanish list does less in Latin America, and whether a list per region or another selection rule would serve it better than the one list `locales.ES` carries (the 2026-10-30 #959 entry's Latin America bullet; the 2026-10-30 #753 phase 3g entry measures the pack against the default there) — #959. A name written with ñ dropped (`Begona`, `Inigo`) matches no entry, the key keeping the tilde; whether to fold ñ for data that strips it — #959.

- 2026-10-24 #754 — TWO FAMILY NAMES ARE AN OPT-IN SWITCH THAT TAKES THE NAME WORD BESIDE THE FAMILY. The PSC register's fielded names (`local_agreement.py psc-individuals`) put two-word surnames read as middle plus family as the largest disagreement there, 22,168 names, 72% not Iberian (the ranking on #754). Given-first, `Ana García López` is the same shape as a name with a middle name, so the default cannot change; the convention is not one country's, so it is a `Policy` field rather than `locales.ES`. Portuguese order, the mother's name first, is out of scope.
  What the rule settles, and why each way:
  - **The count.** A particle run counts with the word it attaches to (P1's reach, so `Juan Pérez de la Vega` gives family `Pérez de la Vega`); a P2 chain counts the words it chained, so `Ana María de la Vega Rodríguez` keeps family `de la Vega Rodríguez`; a connective join counts its words, so `José María Ortega y Gasset` keeps family `Ortega y Gasset`; titles, suffixes and nicknames count as nothing. The rest of the name is split as assign read it, so a P2 chain is never split, and the word taken is counted the same way and must count as one, so a hyphenated given name (`Ana María-José López`) or a P5 pair (`Ana abdul Rahman López`) stays where it is.
  - **A hyphen joins two words**, and so do the Unicode hyphens, the figure and en dashes, the minus sign and the small and fullwidth hyphen-minus, but not an em dash, which is punctuation between words (widened by the #875 review, PR #878; no PSC record holds any of the characters added there, so the figures below stand). Counting `García-López` as one word would take the word before it. Measured over the 34,195 scored PSC records whose name holds an ASCII hyphen, with the switch on: 31,920 agree counting the hyphen's words against 19,649 counting the token (the default reads 32,958), Spanish filers 189 against 182 of 252, British 20,437 against 10,700 of 21,797.
  - **Not an initial, a mark or a script's order.** The PR review (#754) found the first draft taking a middle initial (`Ana M. García` → family `M. García`), a spaced dash (`García - López` → family `- López`) and the second syllable of a spaced Han or Hangul name read family-first by W4 (`王 小 明` → family `王 小`); each is refused now. An initial writes a given name; W4's order is the script's, not the caller's declaration, and no script it keys writes two family names as separate words. The second review found the refusals narrower than the first fix's prose: only an initial written alone is refused (`Ana J.R. García` and an abbreviation such as `Mª` are taken, accepted rather than widened, the run-together shape being a credential's as often as an initial's), the parse cannot tell a script order from a declaration that agrees with it (`王 小 明` under a declared FAMILY_FIRST moves, accepted), and the mark test asked `isalnum`, now `isalpha`, so a lone number is no family name either.
  - **Family-first.** The declared order says where the family stands, so the first two name words are the family and the rest is laid out as P1's family-first fold lays out its own: `García Márquez Gabriel` gives given `Gabriel`. A particle P6 attached at the far end stays family where it stands.
  - **Precedence.** `middle_as_family` folds every middle, which includes the word this rule takes, so the switch runs first and the fold takes what is left; refusing the pair would cost a working configuration for no wrong reading (AGENTS.md, "Invariants guard harm, not no-ops"). A patronymic rotation that fired decided the family already, and the rule finds no middle standing right before that family, so it stands down; where none fired (natural order, or any family-first order, where the rotations stand down) a patronymic is a name word like any other and may be taken — accepted, rules/O6. Against P1's family-first reach of one word the switch wins, the caller having said the family holds two. A family comma decides by itself, an empty-head one included.
  - **No report.** The caller declared the convention, as a declared order is followed without a report; the fork is real, but reported on most names of three words or more it would teach the caller to ignore the field. No existing kind names it (`ORDER` is about field order); whether the reading not taken is reported belongs to #752, which derives ambiguity from clashing readings.

- 2026-10-30 #959 — A LISTED GIVEN NAME IS NOT TAKEN: an amendment to rules/O6's Except bullet "What is taken must be one name word a family name could be", not a new rule. In `José Luis Pérez` the word the switch takes is a given name, and that shape is most of what the switch gets wrong (the 2026-10-27 entry: the records it breaks nearly all hold a one-word family box). A Spanish reader settles it by vocabulary: given names are a concentrated set and few words are common in both roles, so a word only a given name is says the name holds one family name. The rule reads `Lexicon.given_names`, empty by default, which a caller fills, and which sub-phase 3g's `es` pack is to fill with the list shipped here; with the list empty the switch reads exactly as before (the snapshot gains the rule's example texts and moves no reading).
  What it settles, and why each way:
  - **Where the list lives: generated, as locales.ZH's surnames are.** `nameparser/locales/_es_given_names.py` holds the list in a block `tools/es_given_names.py` writes, for the `es` pack (sub-phase 3g of #753) to carry as a lexicon fragment, `Lexicon(given_names=GIVEN_NAMES)`. Weighed against: `given` readings in an `es` language file, which per-word TOML would carry a thousand words judged one at a time, where what is decided is a cutoff; the evidence for the set is the tool's docstring and this entry, and a change to it is decided as a cutoff change, as ZH's top 300 is (decisions/W1). A generated list does not pass through the TOML build, so its check 11 does not reach it; that check lets a `given` reading stand beside a title or particle reading and derives the marker the pair means (`prince`, a title with a given reading, compiles to `titles_ambiguous`); what it refuses is the phase 1 flags (`may_be_name`, `may_be_given = true`), a particle reading that does not state `may_be_given = false` exactly where its word has no `given` reading, an agnomen reading on a word with no name reading, and a name reading beside any other suffix-word kind (its full statement is in `tools/vocab/build.py`'s docstring). The list is held to something stricter, a test keeping it disjoint from every word the default lists as a particle, a title (ambiguous ones too, for both), a suffix or a conjunction (tests/v2/test_es_given_names.py, with its recorded negative control), since a word whose own reading another rule weighs is no word this veto should decide, which is what the tool leaves out.
  - **The source: INE.** The Instituto Nacional de Estadística publishes every given name borne by at least 20 residents of Spain, by sex, and every surname borne as a first surname by at least 20, with its counts as first and second surname, both from the census of 1 January 2025. Its legal notice licenses the site's statistics as "la Creative Commons Reconocimiento 4.0 (CC BY 4.0)" (https://www.ine.es/aviso_legal, read 2026-10-30), asking the reuser to cite "Elaboración propia con datos extraídos del sitio web del INE: www.ine.es" and the date of the data; the shipped module's docstring gives both, with the licence's URI and the files' last update (23 April 2026, the server's `Last-Modified` on both files). A word's given uses are the bearers of the names holding it; its surname uses, the bearers of the surnames holding it, less those holding it behind a particle (`de Miguel`), which the switch never takes as the bare word (with them counted, `Miguel` is 89% given and falls out; without, 92%). A word is a candidate where at least 90% of its uses are given, #959's share; INE writes names without acute accents, so the parse matches a word with its accents and diaeresis dropped and the tilde on n and the cedilla on c kept (`given_name_key`), and a caller's entry also as written. Words common in both roles stay out (`Martín` 10% given, `Vicente` 63%, `Santiago` 57%, `Cruz` 18%). Weighed against: a list built from ORCID's boxes, which #959 measured on a held-out half; INE is licensed for redistribution, counts a whole population rather than researchers, and is not the population scored, so no held-out split is needed.
  - **One pooled list, Spain's.** INE counts Spain's residents, Latin American-born ones among them, and #959 found a list pooled over six countries within half a point of per-country lists everywhere but Argentina and Peru. Peru is where the pooled list costs most: there it fixes about as many records as it breaks at every cutoff, the breaks being first surnames that are given names in Spain (`Jorge` 12, `Carlos` 11, `Pablo` 6 at the 1,000 cut), where #959's per-country list, learned from Peru's own boxes, broke 58 of the 9,735 records of its held-out half, and its list pooled over six countries 102 of the same; this list breaks 189 of all 19,471.
  - **The cutoff: 1,000 words** (84.3% of the given-word uses in INE's table). Measured per country with O6 on, the list cut at 100 to all 16,559 candidates; agreement in each of the six double-surname countries is within 0.3 points of the 2,000-word list at 1,000 (Venezuela the 0.3), and a band past it buys at most a tenth of a net fix per added word in those six (1,000 → 2,000: 134 fixed, 35 broken; 2,000 → 5,000: 128 fixed, 36 broken, Chile breaking as many as it fixes), where the first hundred words bought sixteen. Argentina gains 1.6 points more by 2,000 and 2.4 by all, which 3g weighs if it turns the switch on there.
  - **Family-first, symmetric.** Under a family-first order the switch takes the unit after the family, and a listed one stays where it is: `Pérez José Luis` keeps family `Pérez`. No population the corpora hold writes Spanish names family-first with the fields stated, so it is measured on ORCID's boxes rendered family box first, which is a rendering, not anyone's writing. The cost moves with the order: a listed word that is a second surname, written after the first, becomes the given name under FAMILY_FIRST (`Flores Jorge Ana` gives given `Jorge`, middle `Ana`) and a middle name under FAMILY_FIRST_GIVEN_LAST, where given-first it is a first surname left as a middle name; on the rendering the list breaks 38 (Chile) to 205 (Peru) records a country, the family-first line below (found by the #961 review).
