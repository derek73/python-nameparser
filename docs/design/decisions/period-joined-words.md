# period-joined-words — a word joined at its full stop: the vocabulary reads it whole first, and it divides only into a name

## Standing

- A word with a letter after an interior full stop keeps the whole-word readings the vocabulary already gives it, and these come before any division: a suffix spelled with every full stop removed (`M.Sc.`, `Pharm.D.`, `Ph.D.`), and every chunk of one kind (`Lt.Gov.` titles, `Msc.Ed.` credentials). Whether a title is also looked up without its stops is #490's question, and is not decided here — 2026-11-09 #1255.
- Otherwise, a word may divide after a full stop only where two things hold. The chunk before the stop must be one the parser reads on its own (a single letter, a transliteration digraph rules/H2 names since #1136, a listed title, a listed given name written short, or a saint particle), and what follows must be a name. A divided word is to read as it does with a space there: `Dr.John Smith` as `Dr. John Smith`, `J.Smith` as `J. Smith`, `Mark Jhon Sta.Ana` as `Mark Jhon Sta. Ana`, `Lt.Gov.John` as `Lt.Gov. John`. That target is decided. What is not yet decided is the test that tells a name after the stop from a field or a title written without its own stop (`Dr.Med`, `A.Professor`); the first Open line covers it. So nothing divides yet, and the division ships after #998's credential batch, once that test is measured — 2026-11-09 #1255.
- Two kinds of word do not divide, and keep today's reading. The first has a lowercase letter after the stop (`G.ppe`, `Dr.philol.`, `Dott.ssa`, `Fr.co`). The second is a degree written with its field and closed by its own full stop (`Dipl.Ing.`, `Dr.Med.`, `Lt.Gen.`). What tells that field from a glued name closed by a stop (`Mrs.Smith.`) is part of the first Open line. Which other first chunks the parser reads on its own, and so which more words divide, is left to the Open line for #1230 — 2026-11-09 #1255.
- A transliteration digraph is a chunk the parser reads on its own, the ones rules/H2 names and only those (`Yu`, `Ya`, `Ye`, `Yo`, `Zh`, `Kh`, `Shch`, `Dzh`), so `Yu.A.Chibrikova` is to divide as `Yu. A. Chibrikova` reads. `Ts`, `Ch` and `Sh` are not: rules/H2 reads each as a title by shape, since each is also a title written short, so `Ts.Sandag-Ochir` does not divide and keeps today's reading; what it should read is #1230's question for an unlisted first chunk rules/H2 reads as a title, and #1235's for a Mongolian `Ts.` — 2026-11-09 #1136.
- A run of single letters joined by full stops stays one word wherever the division acts (`E.T.`, `U.S.`, `L.C.P.`, `B.K.`). Three questions about it stay open, and the division does not answer them: whether `E.T. Smith` reads given 'E.', middle 'T.' once roles are assigned (#490), which vocabulary reads such a run's spelling without stops at the front of a name (#490, #979), and what reads it at the end (rules/S3, #864) — 2026-11-09 #1255.

Open: [#1255](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/1255), the test that tells a name after the stop from what is no name. Shape alone has failed twice (the Log's two review paragraphs), and the test has to keep these rows whole or read them right. They are a field or title modifier written without its own stop (`Ole Simonsen, Dr.Med`, `Jessica Farebrother, Dr.Sc`, `Maha Youssef, A.Professor`, `Tiange Liu, MA.Sc`; each reverses the comma when divided, measured 2026-11-09); a saint phrase opening a name (`Sta.Maria Rossi` and `St.Clair Smith` divided read title 'Sta.' and 'St.', where rules/P10 reads a saint phrase as a place or a family); a glued name closed by a stop (`J.Smith.`, `Mrs.Smith.`, `Dr.John.Smith`); and a name opening with an inner capital, an apostrophe or a Greek capital (`Dr.McDonald`, `Dr.O'Brien`, `Δ.Παπαδόπουλος`).
Open: [#1255](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/1255), the writings where the case of the letter after the stop gives no signal: a name written in one case (`dr.john smith`, the FEC's `J.MCDONALD`), an all-capitals chunk (`MD.MS`), and a script without case (`डा.राम शर्मा` reads title 'डा.राम', where `डा. राम शर्मा` reads title 'डा.', given 'राम').
Open: [#998](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/998), with the credential batch listed, how many unlisted credentials written without a final stop the division would read as name words (`John Smith B.Tech` → middle 'Smith B.', family 'Tech'), against how many glued initials it gives their surname back (`Ahmed Ali M.Hassan`).
Open: [#1230](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/1230), a title glued to a second abbreviation (`Dr.J. Smith`), and what an unlisted first chunk H2 would read as a title reads (`Gen.Ademola`).
Open: [#1255](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/1255)'s comment, a given name written short and glued to the next given name (`Gio.Batta`, `Alex.John`). An unlisted stub is not divided, because its spaced twin reads it as a title by rules/H2 (`Gio. Batta Rossi` → title 'Gio.'). The Italian contraction `Fr.co` keeps its title reading through its listed first chunk.
Open: [#1235](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/1235), which field a Mongolian glued initial takes under the default order. This is a pack question, the same one the spaced `Ц. Сандаг-Очир` already raises.

## Log

- 2026-11-09 #1136 — A TRANSLITERATION DIGRAPH IS A CHUNK READ ON ITS OWN, THE ONES rules/H2 NAMES AND ONLY THOSE. This file's Open line for #1136 asked whether a digraph is an initial, a yes adding it to the chunks read on its own. decisions/H2's entry of this date answered it for the spaced word: the digraphs that are no title in any corpus read (`Yu`, `Ya`, `Ye`, `Yo`, `Zh`, `Kh`, `Shch`, `Dzh`) are no title by shape, while `Ts`, `Ch` and `Sh`, each also a title written short, still are. The glued word follows its spaced twin, as this file's 2026-11-09 #1255 entry decides every divided word does, so the division takes the same set: `Yu.A.Chibrikova` is to read as `Yu. A. Chibrikova`. The Open line's own example, `Ts.Sandag-Ochir`, is no such word, since `Ts. Sandag-Ochir` reads title 'Ts.': it does not divide, and what it should read is left to the Open lines for #1230 and #1235.
- 2026-11-09 #1255 — A WORD JOINED AT ITS FULL STOP IS READ WHOLE BY THE VOCABULARY FIRST, AND DIVIDES ONLY WHERE A CHUNK THE PARSER READS ON ITS OWN MEETS A NAME; THE TEST FOR A NAME IS LEFT OPEN. #1255 asked whether a word glued to the next at its full stop should divide there (`Dr.John`, `Ц.Сандаг-Очир`, `J.Smith`, `Sta.Ana`, `Ma.Teresa`, with #1230, #1235 and #1071 as its cases). #490 and #979 ask about a similar-looking word: whether a dotted run (`U.S.`, `L.C.P.`) reads by its spelling without stops. Decided together, because the parser already gives such a word three readings and these issues ask for a fourth. (i) The whole word with every stop removed is looked up as a suffix, which is how `M.D.` matches `md`. No title lookup is made that way, which is #490's miss. (ii) The word is cut at its stops and each chunk is looked up (rules/S3; rules/H5's `Lt.Gov.`). Any title chunk makes the word a title, which is why `Dr.John` reads title 'Dr.John'. (iii) The word stays a name word (`J.R.R.`, `B.K.` #864, `St.John` #838). The fourth reading, missing until now, divides the word into the words it would be with a space after the stop.
  THE SPACED TWIN SORTS THEM, measured on master b23f5fcc. Where every chunk is a single letter, the twin reads worse: `John Smith E. T.` → family 'T.' where `John Smith E.T.` keeps family 'E.T.', and `U. S. Grant` → given 'U.', middle 'S.'. Where a chunk the parser reads on its own meets a name, the twin reads better in each of #1255's rows (what counts as a name is the open test; the rows the reviews found it must refuse are in the second-review paragraph below):
  - `Dr. John Smith` → title 'Dr.', given 'John' (glued: title 'Dr.John').
  - `J. Smith` → given 'J.', family 'Smith' (glued: given 'J.Smith', reported).
  - `Ahmed Ali M. Hassan` → family 'Hassan' (glued: suffix 'M.Hassan').
  - `Mark Jhon Sta. Ana` → family 'Sta. Ana' (glued: suffix 'Sta.Ana').
  - `Ma. Teresa Cruz` → given 'Ma.', middle 'Teresa'.
  - `Ц. Сандаг-Очир` divides the Mongolian name as its spaced writing does, given 'Ц.', family 'Сандаг-Очир' (glued: one given word, reported). Which field is right under the default order is #1235's question.
  Where a lowercase letter follows the stop, the twin reads wrong (`Fr. co Rossi` → given 'co'), because the word is one word contracted (Italian `G.ppe` for Giuseppe, `Ant.o` for Antonio), as #1255's comment found.
  WHY THE CASE OF THE NEXT LETTER IS NOT ENOUGH. The first draft of this entry divided before any capital that opened a chunk written in Title case. The design-docs review measured two classes that rule breaks:
  - A degree written with its field in Title case and closed by a full stop (`Dipl.Ing.`, `Dr.Med.`, `Dr.Ing.`) is a title today. rules/S3 and rules/H5 hold `John Smith Dipl.Ing.` → title 'Dipl.Ing.' as an example. The spaced twin reads the field as the family (`John Smith Dipl. Ing.` → family 'Ing.'; `John Smith Dr. Med.` → family 'Med.').
  - A given name written short and glued to the next given name (`Gio.Batta`, `Alex.John`) reads given 'Gio.Batta' today. Its twin loses the given name to rules/H2's leading-abbreviation title (`Gio. Batta Rossi` → title 'Gio.').
  So the division needs the stop to be the end of a word on both sides. Of the revision that followed, the left-hand half stands: the chunk before the stop must be one the parser reads on its own, so the twin reads it as what it is. Its right-hand half asked what follows to be written out, with no stop of its own, on the premise that a field abbreviation always has one. The second review refuted that premise (below), so the right-hand test is open. A prototype of the revision (a scratch script dividing each word and parsing the result against the glued writing) kept the closed field rows whole (`Dipl.Ing.`, `Dr.Med.`, `Dr.-Ing.`, `Dipl.-Kfm.`, `Lt.Gen.`), and the unlisted stubs too (`Gio.Batta`, `Alex.John`). It divided each of #1255's own rows (`Dr.John`, `Prof.Jane`, `Hon.Rashid`, `J.Smith`, `Ц.Сандаг-Очир`, `Sta.Ana`, `Ma.Teresa`, `M.Hassan`, `Lt.Gov.John`, `Yu.A.Chibrikova` → `Yu.A.` + `Chibrikova`). Those are the rows any replacement test has to match.
  THE SECOND REVIEW FOUND THAT SHAPE CANNOT TELL A NAME FROM A FIELD. The revised test asked what follows the stop to be "written out": a capital, a lowercase letter, and no stop of its own. A field or title modifier written without its stop passes that test. In the ClinicalTrials.gov officials (`ctgov-officials`), 47 distinct names hold one of five such spellings as a whole word, each with a chunk the parser reads on its own before the stop: `Dr.Med`, `Prof.Dr.Med`, `Dr.Sc` or `Dr.Sci`, `A.Prof` or `A.Professor`, and `MA.Sc` (`ma` licensed as a given name written short). Measured 2026-11-09 with `grep -E '(^|[^A-Za-z.])(Prof\.)?(Dr\.Med|Dr\.Sci?|A\.Prof(essor)?|MA\.Sc)([^A-Za-z.]|$)' ~/.cache/nameparser-tools/local/ctgov-officials/corpus.jsonl | sort -u | wc -l`. That counts those five spellings only, not the class (`Dr.Phil`, `Dr.Ing` and the like are left out). In every row parsed, dividing moves the token from a title or suffix to name words, and most reverse the comma: `Ole Simonsen, Dr.Med` → given 'Med', family 'Ole Simonsen'. At the front the closed spelling was safe and the open one is not: `Dr.Med Hans Meier` divided → title 'Dr.', given 'Med'. The same review found three more classes the test reaches. A saint phrase opening a name divides into a title (`Sta.Maria Rossi` → title 'Sta.'). A glued name closed by a stop stays glued (`Mrs.Smith.`). And a name with an inner capital or in Greek meets the test, though the prototype's pattern missed them. So the target stays (divide into the spaced twin where what follows is a name), and the test is left open, with those rows as its grid. Vocabulary on the right-hand side (refusing a known field or modifier), a measured shape, or both, is for the implementing PR to measure over the local corpora.
  THE WHOLE-WORD LOOKUP COMES FIRST BECAUSE IT DOES REACH WORD CHUNKS. The framing posted to #1255 on 2026-11-09 claimed that no word with a word chunk can match an acronym spelled without its stops. That is false. `M.Sc.`, `B.Sc.`, `M.Phil.`, `D.Phil.`, `M.Med`, `Psy.D.` and `Pharm.D.` each have a chunk of two or more letters, none of those chunks counts on its own, and each reads as a suffix through its spelling without stops (`John Smith M.Phil.` → suffix 'M.Phil.', no report). Most also end in a stop the division would not cut at, but `M.Med`, written without one, shows the two readings meeting on one word. They do not conflict, because the listed reading is consulted first.
  WHAT IS KEPT. decisions/S3's 2026-11-07 #1131 Declined bullet holds: the title verdict stays ANY title chunk, because a title is written with its field (`Dr.philol.`, `st.repr.`, `Dut.ra`, `Lt.Gen.`). Each of those ends its field with a full stop or writes it in lowercase, so none divides, and each reads as today. What the division changes is a listed title chunk followed by a name, which that bullet never weighed.
  WHAT IT COSTS, AND WHY #998 COMES FIRST. Today rules/S3's shape verdict reads an unlisted word of alphabetic chunks as a suffix where a name word is to spare (`Ahmed Ali B.Pharm` → suffix 'B.Pharm', reported). The division takes a word of that shape whose chunk before the stop is one the parser reads on its own and whose chunk after it the name test lets through. An unlisted credential the test lets through then reads as name words. The division then ships with a measurement of what it still costs, over the local corpora #998 counted (the #998 Open line).   WHERE A RULE LANDS. No rule is written yet. The values the Open lines name decide its statement. The rule lands with the division, with this entry as its history, as a T rule by its signal: the chunks either side of a full stop. The division is to happen before grouping, so every later stage reads the spaced twin. It is the converse of mechanisms.md#MERGE-THEN-SETTLE. Rendering is expected to follow rules/R1's clause for words written with no space between them, as W1's divided halves already do (`김민준 John` renders '김민준 John' across two fields). That expectation is checked when the division is built. One move is visible already: `St.John Smith` would read title 'St.', given 'John', as `St. John Smith` does today, where it reads title 'St.John'. rules/R4 (#838) recases `st.john` as one word, and still will wherever it stays one.
