# period-joined-words — a word joined at its full stop: the vocabulary reads it whole first, and it divides only into a name

## Standing

- A word with a letter after an interior full stop keeps the whole-word readings the vocabulary already gives it, and these come before any division: a suffix spelled with every full stop removed (`M.Sc.`, `Pharm.D.`, `Ph.D.`), and every chunk of one kind (`Lt.Gov.` titles, `Msc.Ed.` credentials), and, since #1305 named it, a title listed with its stops (`Univ.Prof`, `Dott.ssa`, `Dr.C.`, rules/T8). Whether a title is also looked up without its stops was #490's question, not decided here (the Elsewhere line below gives its answer) — 2026-11-09 #1255.
- Otherwise, a word may divide after a full stop only where two things hold. The chunk before the stop must be one the parser reads on its own (a single letter, a transliteration digraph rules/H2 names since #1136, a listed title, a listed given name written short, or a saint particle), and what follows must be a name. A divided word is to read as it does with a space there: `Dr.John Smith` as `Dr. John Smith`, `J.Smith` as `J. Smith`, `Mark Jhon Sta.Ana` as `Mark Jhon Sta. Ana`, `Lt.Gov.John` as `Lt.Gov. John`. That target is decided. The test that tells a name after the stop from a field or a title written without its own stop (`Dr.Med`, `A.Professor`) was left open here; decisions/T8 answers it by position, dividing before any comma only, and shipped the division, without the saint particle's chunk, which #1071 keeps open (`Mark Jhon Sta.Ana` stays one word) — 2026-11-09 #1255, amended 2026-11-10 #1230, #1235.
- Two kinds of word do not divide, and keep today's reading. The first has a lowercase letter after the stop (`G.ppe`, `Dr.philol.`, `Dott.ssa`, `Fr.co`). The second is a degree written with its field and closed by its own full stop (`Dipl.Ing.`, `Dr.Med.`, `Lt.Gen.`). What tells that field from a glued name closed by a stop (`Mrs.Smith.`) decisions/T8 answers by keeping both whole. Which other first chunks the parser reads on its own was #1230's question, answered there: a listed title divides, an unlisted one does not — 2026-11-09 #1255, amended 2026-11-10 #1230.
- A transliteration digraph is a chunk the parser reads on its own, the ones rules/H2 names and only those (`Yu`, `Ya`, `Ye`, `Yo`, `Zh`, `Kh`, `Shch`, `Dzh`), so `Yu.A.Chibrikova` is to divide as `Yu. A. Chibrikova` reads. `Ts`, `Ch` and `Sh` are not: rules/H2 reads each as a title by shape, since each is also a title written short, so under the default vocabulary, where `ts` is unlisted, `Ts.Sandag-Ochir` does not divide and keeps today's reading; what it should read was #1230's question for an unlisted first chunk rules/H2 reads as a title, answered by decisions/T8 (it keeps its one word), and stays #1306's for a Mongolian `Ts.`. Under a choice that lists `ts` as a title (`vi`), it is a listed title chunk, and the second Standing line applies to it — 2026-11-09 #1136.
- A run of single letters joined by full stops stays one word wherever the division acts (`E.T.`, `U.S.`, `L.C.P.`, `B.K.`). The division does not decide how it reads. At the front of a name, rules/H10 reads it as a title marked `dotted` where a title follows it (decisions/H10, #490), and #979 asks which `es` degree acronyms join that set. What reads it at the end stays open (rules/S3, #864) — 2026-11-09 #1255.

Elsewhere: a run of single letters spelling a title at the front of a name, decisions/H10's 2026-11-09 #490 entry.
Elsewhere: the division itself and the name test that left it open, answered by position (a chunk read alone and a name before any comma divide, nothing after a comma does), with the one-case, caseless-script and unlisted-credential writings the Open lines here named, decisions/T8's 2026-11-10 #1230/#1235 entry.
Elsewhere: a given name written as a run of single letters, parted into its initials once roles are final (`E.T. Smith` → given 'E.', middle 'T.'), decisions/T7's 2026-11-10 #1296 entry.
Open: [#1255](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/1255)'s comment, a given name written short and glued to the next given name (`Gio.Batta`, `Alex.John`). An unlisted stub is not divided, because its spaced twin reads it as a title by rules/H2 (`Gio. Batta Rossi` → title 'Gio.'). The Italian contraction `Fr.co` keeps its title reading through its listed first chunk.
Open: [#1306](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/1306), which field a Mongolian glued initial takes under the default order. This is a pack question, the same one the spaced `Ц. Сандаг-Очир` already raises; it moved from #1235, closed by decisions/T8's division.

Decided together, because the parser already gives such a word three readings and these issues ask for a fourth. (i) The whole word with every stop removed is looked up as a suffix, which is how `M.D.` matches `md`. No title lookup is made that way, which is #490's miss. (ii) The word is cut at its stops and each chunk is looked up (rules/S3; rules/H5's `Lt.Gov.`). Any title chunk makes the word a title, which is why `Dr.John` reads title 'Dr.John'. (iii) The word stays a name word (`J.R.R.`, `B.K.` #864, `St.John` #838). The fourth reading, missing until now, divides the word into the words it would be with a space after the stop.

  WHY THE CASE OF THE NEXT LETTER IS NOT ENOUGH. The first draft of this entry divided before any capital that opened a chunk written in Title case. The design-docs review measured two classes that rule breaks:
  - A degree written with its field in Title case and closed by a full stop (`Dipl.Ing.`, `Dr.Med.`, `Dr.Ing.`) is a title today. rules/S3 and rules/H5 hold `John Smith Dipl.Ing.` → title 'Dipl.Ing.' as an example. The spaced twin reads the field as the family (`John Smith Dipl. Ing.` → family 'Ing.'; `John Smith Dr. Med.` → family 'Med.').
  - A given name written short and glued to the next given name (`Gio.Batta`, `Alex.John`) reads given 'Gio.Batta' today. Its twin loses the given name to rules/H2's leading-abbreviation title (`Gio. Batta Rossi` → title 'Gio.').
  So the division needs the stop to be the end of a word on both sides. Of the revision that followed, the left-hand half stands: the chunk before the stop must be one the parser reads on its own, so the twin reads it as what it is. Its right-hand half asked what follows to be written out, with no stop of its own, on the premise that a field abbreviation always has one. The second review refuted that premise (below), so the right-hand test is open. A prototype of the revision (a scratch script dividing each word and parsing the result against the glued writing) kept the closed field rows whole (`Dipl.Ing.`, `Dr.Med.`, `Dr.-Ing.`, `Dipl.-Kfm.`, `Lt.Gen.`), and the unlisted stubs too (`Gio.Batta`, `Alex.John`). It divided each of #1255's own rows (`Dr.John`, `Prof.Jane`, `Hon.Rashid`, `J.Smith`, `Ц.Сандаг-Очир`, `Sta.Ana`, `Ma.Teresa`, `M.Hassan`, `Lt.Gov.John`, `Yu.A.Chibrikova` → `Yu.A.` + `Chibrikova`). Those are the rows any replacement test has to match.
  THE SECOND REVIEW FOUND THAT SHAPE CANNOT TELL A NAME FROM A FIELD. The revised test asked what follows the stop to be "written out": a capital, a lowercase letter, and no stop of its own. A field or title modifier written without its stop passes that test. In the ClinicalTrials.gov officials (`ctgov-officials`), 47 distinct names hold one of five such spellings as a whole word, each with a chunk the parser reads on its own before the stop: `Dr.Med`, `Prof.Dr.Med`, `Dr.Sc` or `Dr.Sci`, `A.Prof` or `A.Professor`, and `MA.Sc` (`ma` licensed as a given name written short). Measured 2026-11-09 with `grep -E '(^|[^A-Za-z.])(Prof\.)?(Dr\.Med|Dr\.Sci?|A\.Prof(essor)?|MA\.Sc)([^A-Za-z.]|$)' ~/.cache/nameparser-tools/local/ctgov-officials/corpus.jsonl | sort -u | wc -l`. That counts those five spellings only, not the class (`Dr.Phil`, `Dr.Ing` and the like are left out). In every row parsed, dividing moves the token from a title or suffix to name words, and most reverse the comma: `Ole Simonsen, Dr.Med` → given 'Med', family 'Ole Simonsen'. At the front the closed spelling was safe and the open one is not: `Dr.Med Hans Meier` divided → title 'Dr.', given 'Med'. The same review found three more classes the test reaches. A saint phrase opening a name divides into a title (`Sta.Maria Rossi` → title 'Sta.'). A glued name closed by a stop stays glued (`Mrs.Smith.`). And a name with an inner capital or in Greek meets the test, though the prototype's pattern missed them. So the target stays (divide into the spaced twin where what follows is a name), and the test is left open, with those rows as its grid. Vocabulary on the right-hand side (refusing a known field or modifier), a measured shape, or both, is for the implementing PR to measure over the local corpora.
  THE WHOLE-WORD LOOKUP COMES FIRST BECAUSE IT DOES REACH WORD CHUNKS. The framing posted to #1255 on 2026-11-09 claimed that no word with a word chunk can match an acronym spelled without its stops. That is false. `M.Sc.`, `B.Sc.`, `M.Phil.`, `D.Phil.`, `M.Med`, `Psy.D.` and `Pharm.D.` each have a chunk of two or more letters, none of those chunks counts on its own, and each reads as a suffix through its spelling without stops (`John Smith M.Phil.` → suffix 'M.Phil.', no report). Most also end in a stop the division would not cut at, but `M.Med`, written without one, shows the two readings meeting on one word. They do not conflict, because the listed reading is consulted first.
