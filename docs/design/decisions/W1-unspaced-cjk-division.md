# W1 — unspaced CJK division

- 2026-07-27 #271 (decision; shipped in 2.1.0 via PR #294) — Korean division ships as a default: the census surname list is closed, hangul is self-selecting (a hangul entry can only match hangul text), and being unsplit is recoverable while a wrong split is not — which is also why an unrecognized name stays whole. The filed proposal (#271, 2026-07-07) asked for OPT-IN segmentation for Korean too, "like all localization"; default-on is the later refinement, and the census/self-selecting argument above is what justified promoting Korean past the blanket opt-in stance.
- 2026-07-29 #272 (the ja amendment; shipped in 2.1.0 via PR #297) — Han division is opt-in per language pack because Han text does not identify its language (高橋一郎 under a Chinese list divides wrongly); a pluggable segmenter takes what the vocabulary declines, so pack + segmenter compose mechanically: a listed surname is a dictionary certainty and wins.

- 2026-07-29 (ja amendment §1a) — script CLASSIFICATION NFC-normalizes its input (NFD katakana carries combining marks outside the block; NFD hangul decomposes to jamo, which would miss the order rule for macOS-origin names) while SEGMENTATION stays raw — chosen over offset-mapping complexity because NFD then degrades to no-split, never to a wrong split. The read-only folds are the deliberate exceptions to "nothing rewrites the text" (rules.md T Background), and neither ever enters a token. (Corrected 2026-09-10, #cjk-full-stops: this read "segmentation MATCHING stays raw" and "the one deliberate exception". Vocabulary matching composes NFC too since #322 — `_lexicon._normalize` folds every lookup and every stored entry the same way — so the folds are two, and what stays raw is segmentation proper: the surname site's membership test and the peel's tail slice, which index the token's own text. The no-split degradation is what still holds there.)

Declined:

- JMnedict as bundled segmentation data (#272's body, 2026-07-07) — the pip packaging (jamdict-data) was last compiled 2021-04; JMnedict carries no frequency data, so it cannot resolve the ambiguous 2+2/3+1 splits that motivate a segmenter at all; and bundling raised licensing questions the core avoids by delegating to namedivider — whose code and GBDT model are MIT, whose surname data carries Myoji-Yurai terms permitting exactly this name-dividing use, and whose CC-BY-SA BERT model is unused (the 2026-07-29 amendment corrected an earlier flat-"MIT" description; so does this entry). The kind of decline someone re-proposes in two years.

- 2026-08 — zh and ja packs are corpus ALTERNATIVES, one per corpus; stacking them (parser_for(ZH, JA, segmenter=...)) is for genuinely mixed data that accepts the trade: a listed Chinese surname wins before the segmenter is consulted, so 高橋一郎 still divides 高 + 橋一郎 under ZH+JA exactly as under ZH alone. Kana gating resolves through the same script function order uses, so kana-licensed composites (高橋みなみ) gate in under JA while pure-katakana tokens never do (amendment 2026-07-27: activation is per script because the ambiguity is per script).


- 2026-10-18 #695 (weighing; folds in #657) — the ZH surname list reaches past the 2020 census top 100. The question was what a caller who applies `locales.ZH` wants, and the answer taken is Chinese names divided: applying the pack is already the "my data is Chinese" declaration (decisions/W1's 2026-08 entry, on zh and ja as corpus alternatives), and a Japanese corpus has `locales.JA`. So a common Chinese name left whole (柯洁 → family 柯洁) counts as a failure, not as the safe side of W1's "whole rather than wrong". The single-character list became the census rows plus the union of two rankings' top 300: Taiwan's Ministry of the Interior surname ranking (2023, Open Government Data License v1) and Wikidata's Chinese family names (Q1093580) ranked by bearers (CC0). Neither alone, because each is biased by region in its own direction: Taiwan ranks Hokkien surnames high (詹 37, 柯 46) and lacks simplified forms, and Wikidata counts notable people, Korean and Vietnamese bearers of the same character included. `tools/zh_surnames.py` derives the block and writes it into zh.py; it adds a traditional character's simplified form from Unihan when Unihan gives exactly one in the BMP, and adds a traditional form only where a source attests it, because the reverse mapping is one-to-many (余's traditional variants include 餘, which is no one's surname).
  Measured on 16ee2851 against this change, with `parser_for(locales.ZH)`: the 80 Chinese rows of the native-script corpus went from 74 to 80 agreeing (`wikidata_native_agreement_locales.json` holds it); the CLDR locale view stayed at 42/96, and every rendering that moved there was wrong before and after (a given name alone, 文傑 → 文 + 傑 where it had been family 文傑; the transcription 辛巴达; the given-first 安怡张). Recompute: `uv run python tools/differential/cldr_agreement.py`.
  Accepted: Japanese names read under ZH split wrongly more often. On the 60 Japanese rows of the native-script corpus, wrong splits went from 2 to 11 (安達禎 → 安, 平賀淳 → 平, 吉川亨 → 吉, …), taken from "left whole"; the rows read right stayed at 12. On CLDR's native Japanese renderings, 安藤一郎 now gives 安 in the two family-first orders. Transcribed foreign names written without the 间隔号 split too (安娜 → 安 + 娜), since 安, 艾 and 伊 are surnames inside the cut. Recompute: parse each `ja-jp` row of `wikidata_native_fields.jsonl` under `parser_for(locales.ZH)` at the parent commit and at this one, and count a family that is a proper prefix of the text but not the stated surname.
  Accepted: a listed compound reads a two-character name that spells it whole. 张简 is family 张简, not 张 + 简, because a token that is wholly a surname is not divided; this was already true of the 34 shipped compounds (司马), and it is the price of 張簡's 9,162 bearers in the Taiwan file.
  The cutoff, as measured when it was chosen (wrong Japanese splits of 60, Chinese rows right of 80): Wikidata top 200, 4 and 79; Wikidata top 300, 9 and 80; Taiwan top 300 alone, 10 and 77 (traditional only, so 卫 and 华 missed); both at 300, 11 and 80; both at 400, 13 and 80.
  Compounds stay hand-curated (the note above `_COMPOUND` in zh.py states the admission test). A compound ships where Wikidata or the Taiwan file attests a bearer, and where no Wikidata bearer of its first character alone has a name opening with it; the check was read by hand, since most of its hits are compound bearers tagged with the single character (左丘明 under 左, 鍾離權 under 鍾). 26 simplified compounds were added, 钟离 and 段干 among them.

Declined:

- A pack with every surname either source ranks (#695 review, 2026-10-18). Taiwan's single-character surnames past rank 300 hold 78,203 of its 23,373,283 people (0.33%; recompute by summing the population column of the MOI CSV past rank 300), and the tail is where the characters that open Japanese surnames sit (山 369, 川 663, 大 797, 小 798, 中 933, 本 1023). For data that is Chinese, a complete list acts as "divide after the first character unless a compound opens the name"; the list's work past the head is screening out text that is not a Chinese name, and the tail stops paying for it. A stricter "divide after the first character" mode was raised in the same review and declined: one ZH pack.
- Sources not used as data: the PRC Ministry of Public Security name reports (top 100 only, no licence); Yuan Yida's 300- and 400-surname books (copyrighted; usable as a cross-check only); the CRAN package ChineseNames (GPL-3, not redistributable here); zh.wikipedia's 中国姓氏排名 tables (CC BY-SA, compiled from the sources above).
- Hundred Family Surnames compounds no source attests a bearer of (宗政, 谷梁, 东门, 南门, 归海 and others). They divide nothing real, and where the first character is a listed surname they would cut a name that opens with it (宗 + 政X).
- 成公 — 成公亮, the guqin player, is 成 + 公亮, and a Wikidata bearer of 成 whose name opens with 成公 is the case the compound test exists to refuse.
