# W1 — unspaced CJK division

## Standing

- Korean division is on by default and an unrecognized word stays whole, an unsplit name being recoverable where a wrong split is not: `김민준` → family '김', given '민준', `남궁민수` → family '남궁', `뿡민준` → family '뿡민준' — 2026-07-27 #271.
- Han division is opt-in per pack, Han text not identifying its language: `毛泽东` reads family '毛泽东' by default and family '毛' under `locales.ZH`; a listed surname is a dictionary certainty and wins before a pluggable segmenter is asked — 2026-07-29 #272.
- Japanese division is delegated to namedivider, and JMnedict is not bundled as segmentation data (the log's Declined line) — 2026-07-29 #272.
- Script classification composes its input to NFC while segmentation reads the raw text, so a decomposed name degrades to no split, never a wrong one: `김민준` typed decomposed reads family '김민준'; vocabulary matching composes too since #322, the correction inside the entry — 2026-07-29.
- `locales.ZH` and `locales.JA` are corpus alternatives; stacked, a listed Chinese surname still wins before the segmenter (`高橋一郎` divides 高 + 橋一郎 under ZH+JA as under ZH alone), and kana gating follows the script function order uses, so a wholly-katakana token never gates in — 2026-08.
- The ZH single-character list is the census rows plus the union of Taiwan's and Wikidata's top 300, written by `tools/zh_surnames.py`, so `柯洁` and `关羽` under `locales.ZH` read family '柯' and '关' — 2026-10-18 #695.
- Five mainland surnames that both rankings miss are added by hand at the census block's tail, with the traditional forms Taiwan attests, each annotated with its mainland rank, so `付晓明` under `locales.ZH` reads family '付', given '晓明'; a mainland-weighted third source is the Open line — 2026-10-30 #952.
- Accepted under `locales.ZH`: a Japanese name splits wrongly more often (`安達禎` → family '安', and since #952 `代田稔` → family '代' and `芦川誠` → family '芦', 芦 opening 102 of a list of 172,413 Japanese names), and so does a transcription written without the 间隔号 (`安娜` → family '安', given '娜') — 2026-10-18 #695, amended 2026-10-30 #952.
- A listed compound wins over its first character whatever the name's length, and a token that is wholly a surname stays whole: `张简明` → family '张简', given '明', and `张简` → family '张简' under `locales.ZH`; compounds stay hand-curated against bearer data, 东野 and 成公 left out — 2026-10-18 #695.
- A pack holding every ranked surname, a stricter divide-after-the-first-character mode, the unlicensed or copyrighted sources and the unattested Hundred Family Surnames compounds are declined — 2026-10-18 #695.
- A name the interpunct divides stands division down, vocabulary and segmenter alike (decisions/T3, #298), and rules/W1 states it as an Except rather than an Accepted cost: `安东尼·陈志明` under `locales.ZH` → family '陈志明', given '安东尼' — 2026-10-20 (Derek), #761.

Elsewhere: a segmenter is asked only of a name its writer left undivided, decisions/W3's 2026-08 entry; the Japanese surnames and married doubles kept out of the ZH list, decisions/vocabulary-exclusions' #695 lines.

Open: a mainland-weighted ranking as a third source in `tools/zh_surnames.py`, which would catch the next surname both rankings miss without a hand addition — the route the 2026-10-30 #952 entry did not take; wainshine/Chinese-Names-Corpus, the candidate it weighed, is under Sources not used as data.

## Log

- 2026-07-27 #271 (decision; shipped in 2.1.0 via PR #294) — Korean division ships as a default: the census surname list is closed, hangul is self-selecting (a hangul entry can only match hangul text), and being unsplit is recoverable while a wrong split is not — which is also why an unrecognized name stays whole. The filed proposal (#271, 2026-07-07) asked for OPT-IN segmentation for Korean too, "like all localization"; default-on is the later refinement, and the census/self-selecting argument above is what justified promoting Korean past the blanket opt-in stance.
- 2026-07-29 #272 (the ja amendment; shipped in 2.1.0 via PR #297) — Han division is opt-in per language pack because Han text does not identify its language (高橋一郎 under a Chinese list divides wrongly); a pluggable segmenter takes what the vocabulary declines, so pack + segmenter compose mechanically: a listed surname is a dictionary certainty and wins.

- 2026-07-29 (ja amendment §1a) — script CLASSIFICATION NFC-normalizes its input (NFD katakana carries combining marks outside the block; NFD hangul decomposes to jamo, which would miss the order rule for macOS-origin names) while SEGMENTATION stays raw — chosen over offset-mapping complexity because NFD then degrades to no-split, never to a wrong split. The read-only folds are the deliberate exceptions to "nothing rewrites the text" (rules.md T Background), and neither ever enters a token. (Corrected 2026-09-10, #cjk-full-stops: this read "segmentation MATCHING stays raw" and "the one deliberate exception". Vocabulary matching composes NFC too since #322 — `_lexicon._normalize` folds every lookup and every stored entry the same way — so the folds are two, and what stays raw is segmentation proper: the surname site's membership test and the peel's tail slice, which index the token's own text. The no-split degradation is what still holds there.)

- 2026-08 — zh and ja packs are corpus ALTERNATIVES, one per corpus; stacking them (parser_for(ZH, JA, segmenter=...)) is for genuinely mixed data that accepts the trade: a listed Chinese surname wins before the segmenter is consulted, so 高橋一郎 still divides 高 + 橋一郎 under ZH+JA exactly as under ZH alone. Kana gating resolves through the same script function order uses, so kana-licensed composites (高橋みなみ) gate in under JA while pure-katakana tokens never do (amendment 2026-07-27: activation is per script because the ambiguity is per script).

Declined:

- JMnedict as bundled segmentation data (#272's body, 2026-07-07) — the pip packaging (jamdict-data) was last compiled 2021-04; JMnedict carries no frequency data, so it cannot resolve the ambiguous 2+2/3+1 splits that motivate a segmenter at all; and bundling raised licensing questions the core avoids by delegating to namedivider — whose code and GBDT model are MIT, whose surname data carries Myoji-Yurai terms permitting exactly this name-dividing use, and whose CC-BY-SA BERT model is unused (the 2026-07-29 amendment corrected an earlier flat-"MIT" description; so does this entry). The kind of decline someone re-proposes in two years.
- A pack with every surname either source ranks (#695 review, 2026-10-18). Taiwan's single-character surnames past the 300th hold 77,355 of its 23,373,283 people (0.33%). Recompute over the order `tools/zh_surnames.py`'s `read_moi` returns — single unified ideographs ranked among themselves, which is not the CSV's `ranking` column, since that column counts two-character surnames too — summing the population column for every surname past index 300. The tail is where the characters that open Japanese surnames sit (in that order: 山 369, 川 656, 大 787, 小 788, 中 920, 本 1008). For data that is Chinese, a complete list acts as "divide after the first character unless a compound opens the name"; the list's work past the head is screening out text that is not a Chinese name, and the tail stops paying for it. A stricter "divide after the first character" mode was raised in the same review and declined: one ZH pack.
- Sources not used as data: the PRC Ministry of Public Security name reports (top 100 only, no licence); Yuan Yida's 300- and 400-surname books (copyrighted; usable as a cross-check only); the CRAN package ChineseNames (GPL-3, not redistributable here; #952 read its registry counts as a cross-check); wainshine/Chinese-Names-Corpus (Apache-2.0, but a list of distinct names a word splitter extracted from text, with no counts of people and some bad entries by its own README, so its ranking of first characters measures how often a surname occurs in text rather than how many people bear it; #952 measured with it); zh.wikipedia's 中国姓氏排名 tables (CC BY-SA, compiled from the sources above).
- Hundred Family Surnames compounds no source attests a bearer of (宗政, 谷梁, 东门, 南门, 归海 and others). They divide nothing real, and where the first character is a listed surname they would cut a name that opens with it (宗 + 政X).
- 东野 — two Wikidata people named 东野 have 野 as their stated given name, and the Taiwan file has no 東野 bearer to outweigh them (the amended compound test, 2026-10-18).
- 成公 — 成公亮, the guqin player, is 成 + 公亮, and a Wikidata bearer of 成 whose name opens with 成公 is the case the compound test exists to refuse.
