## Standing

- The typographic delimiter pairs ship as default delimiters — 2026-07-18 #273.
- Conventions that share a character are read by one interleaved leftmost-match scan, the leftmost valid opener winning (`Hans „Erster“ und “Zweiter” Müller` → nickname 'Erster Zweiter'), and a dangling-open report on a character inside another pair's match is filtered out — 2026-07-18 (05fe693, the same day)
- The word-internal carve-out covers only the straight apostrophe, the one delimiter character real names carry mid- and end-of-word: `Mari' Aube'` reads family "Aube'" with no report, while `Mari" Aube"` reports `unbalanced-delimiter` twice — 2026-07-18 (05fe693, the same day)

- So does one opening a listed particle hyphened to the name after it, unless the word ends with the same apostrophe (`Arent van 's-Gravesande` reports nothing, `Aaron 'T-Bone' Walker` keeps nickname 'T-Bone'), and `'s` is listed beside `'t` — 2026-11-09

## Log

- 2026-07-18 #273 (7ee6e3a) (its per-pair scan superseded the same day by the 05fe693 entry below) — the typographic delimiter pairs shipped with a per-pair full-text scan; three independent review agents broke it the same day: with two shared-character conventions genuinely present, the earlier-sorted pair stole the close and the legitimate match dropped silently, zero ambiguity.
- 2026-07-18 (05fe693, the same day) — the interleaved leftmost-match restructure replaced it (an altitude review called it the right depth); the author's own first fix — post-hoc offset filtering of unbalanced candidates alone — was superseded within the day and survives only as the bulk-recorded dangler filter rules/N2 now names. The word-internal carve-out covers ONLY the straight apostrophe because it is the one delimiter character that occurs mid- and end-of-word in real names; Derek's distinguishing test: with double quotes the same shape ("Mari\" Aube\"") is genuinely ambiguous, while Mari' Aube' is not. A cached delimiter-charset prescreen landed in the same commit, measured: no-delimiter names parse faster than the pre-#273 baseline.
- 2026-11-08 #1241 — A LISTED APOSTROPHE PARTICLE OPENS NO QUOTE. `Pieter 't Hart` read given 'Pieter', family "'t Hart", and reported `unbalanced-delimiter`: the word-initial apostrophe of Dutch `'t` (nl.toml, an article) passed the open test and found no close. The report was the only defect, and on a name nobody finds ambiguous it teaches callers to ignore the field. In the Tweede Kamer's open data every member whose name holds `'t` or `'s-` was reported; in ORCID NL 12 family boxes open with `'t`, 48 with `van 't` and 10 with `in 't` (measured 2026-11-08). Of the two fixes #1241 offered, the open test now asks whether the word the apostrophe opens, up to the next space or comma and folded as the vocabulary is, is a listed particle (`_extract._opens_a_listed_particle`, the word ending where tokenize's own `_ignorable` or a comma ends it, and asked of any same-character open, so a caller quoting with the curly apostrophe gets the same reading of the listed `’t`, as the PR's code review found it did not at first), rather than widening the word-internal carve-out to an apostrophe before one letter and a space. The vocabulary test is the narrower: it reaches the one listed word and any a caller adds, where the shape reaches every quoted clause opening with one letter (`Bob 'A Team' Smith` would lose its nickname). And it moves a reading the carve-out could not: with the particle's apostrophe no open, a quote after it pairs with its own close, so `Jan van 't Hof 'Jantje'` reads nickname 'Jantje' as 1.4.0 did, where master read nickname "t Hof 'Jantje". Still reported, each a row in `tests/v2/pipeline/test_extract.py`: an unlisted word (`'Bob Smith`), the unlisted `'s` (`Jan 's Jacob`, `'s-Gravesande`), and a word the listed `'t` only begins (`'t-Hart`, `'tje`), since the test is the whole word; and `Pieter 't(Bob) Hart`, whose word the scan ends at a space or comma, not at the bracket tokenize ends it at.
  - Accepted: a quote that does open at the particle is no longer one, so `Pieter 't Hart' Smith` reads family "'t Hart' Smith", 1.4.0's reading, where master read nickname 't Hart'. No measured name writes it. The same holds for a configured maiden pair: `Anna 't Hart' Jansen` under `maiden_delimiters` reads family "'t Hart' Jansen", no maiden. The vocabulary folds case, so a quoted nickname that opens with the bare letter T is read this way too (`Bob 'T Bone' Smith` reads family "'T Bone' Smith", where master read nickname 'T Bone'): the one quoted clause the vocabulary test takes that the shape alternative would also have taken, against every other one-letter opening the shape would take.
  - Open as vocabulary, not here: whether `'s` (des, as in `'s-Gravesande` and `'s-Hertogenbosch`) earns a particle reading, which would end the report on `Jan 's Jacob`; the hyphen-joined `'s-` form would still report, its word being the whole `'s-Gravesande`.
- 2026-11-09 — `'S` IS LISTED, AND A HYPHEN ENDS THE PARTICLE. The question #1241 left open: `'s` (des, the genitive article of `'s-Gravesande` and `'s Jacob`) is now an article in nl.toml with its curly twin, `may_be_given = false` like `'t`. No committed or local corpus writes it; a Wikidata people search found 42 humans matching `"'s Gravesande"` (Willem Jacob 's Gravesande, Arent van 's-Gravesande, Carel Nicolaas Storm van 's Gravesande) and 21 matching `"'s Jacob"` (2026-11-09; that search also matches the `s'Jacob` spelling, which this does not reach). Spaced, `Willem Jacob 's Gravesande` read middle "Jacob 's", family 'Gravesande' from 1.4.0 through 3.0.1, and reported `unbalanced-delimiter` from 2.0.0; it now reads family "'s Gravesande", and a sweep of the four comma shapes under all three orders read every `'s` name exactly as the same name with `'t` does. Hyphened, the reading was already right and only the report went wrong, so `_opens_a_listed_particle` now also asks the word's head before its first hyphen, and that settles `Pieter 't-Hart` as well. It does not ask the head of a word that ends with the opening character: a quoted word closes itself, so `Aaron 'T-Bone' Walker` keeps nickname 'T-Bone' (`'t` being listed, cutting at the hyphen alone would have taken it), and so does `Bob 'De-Niro' Smith`. The refusing rows moved with it: `tests/v2/pipeline/test_extract.py` refuses an unlisted head before a hyphen (`'tje-Hart`, `'x-Hart`) and still the rest.
  - Accepted: an UNCLOSED quote opening at a listed particle hyphened to a word is read as the word, with no report: `Aaron 'T-Bone Walker` reads middle "'T-Bone" where 3.0.1 reported the stray quote. The quote has no close, so the reading is unchanged and only the report goes.
  - Not here: case repair writes a particle opening a hyphenated word with a capital (rules/R4, `Ter-Petrosyan`), so `WILLEM 'S-GRAVESANDE` repairs to "Willem 'S-Gravesande" where Dutch writes `'s-Gravesande`, as did 3.0.1; `'t-` the same. The spaced form repairs right (`'s Gravesande`).
