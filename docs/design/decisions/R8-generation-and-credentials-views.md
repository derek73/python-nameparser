# R8 — the generation and credentials views

## Standing

- `ParsedName.generation` and `ParsedName.credentials` partition `suffix` by the reading each word took, a generation or an agnomen in the first and every other word in the second; they are views, not roles, so `suffix`, `as_dict()`, `replace()`'s fields and the repr are unchanged, item 4 of decisions/vocabulary-model's #754 output-model entry — 2026-10-29 #754, sub-phase 4b.
- A listed suffix word's kind is read from the reading index (`_reading_index.suffix_kind_of`, through `_vocab.suffix_kind`), since the compiled sets do not record it — 2026-10-29 #754, sub-phase 4b.

Elsewhere: the field's composition and the 2026-08-16 decision to keep it one field, which still stands, decisions/suffix-field-composition; whether `credentials` should leave the honorifics out, CLDR having no third field, is a value question under the RFC's Phase 4, Left open (docs/design/vocabulary-model-rfc.md).

Open: none in this file.

- 2026-10-29 #754, sub-phase 4b (#326) — IMPLEMENTED item 4 of decisions/vocabulary-model's 2026-10-28 #754 entry. SUPERSEDES: decisions/A1's 2026-10-26 #752 ACCEPTED clause that a listed suffix word neither a generation by its tag nor an agnomen reads as `post-nominal`, `jr` and `esq` alike. What the implementation decided, beyond that entry:
  1. **The kind of a listed suffix word comes from the reading index.** `jr` and `esq` compile into `suffix_words` with the same tags, so 4a read both as `post-nominal` and pinned the gap with strict xfails. Classify writes a kind tag beside `vocab:suffix-word` (`vocab:generation-word`, `vocab:credential-word`), asking `_reading_index.suffix_kind_of` through `_vocab.listed_word_kind_tag`, a memo whose hit costs no frame, and `_vocab.suffix_kind` reads the tag, so `Token.reading` and `Ambiguity.alternative` move together: a lone `Jr` names a `generation` alternative where it named a `post-nominal` one, and a credential written as a word (`博士`) reads `credential`. The vocabulary's courtesy, honorific, professional honorific and status kinds have no reading kind of their own and stay `post-nominal`. `_vocab` imports `_reading_index` for it, which tests/v2/test_layering.py's table records. The first draft looked the kind up in `suffix_kind` from the token's text, which keeps a delimiter glued on (rules/C10): `John Smith, Jr.; MD` read `Jr.;` post-nominal and put it in the credentials, as `John Smith, V; MD` did a numeral, found by the orchestrating review of #947. Classify reads every word by its glued stem already, so the kind is read there, by the key every other tag of the word is read by, and assemble drops the two tags from the published token, `Token.reading` carrying the kind out: `Token` compares its tags, and a published tag would make a name unequal to its own 2.x pickle (tests/v2/test_types.py's 2.8.0 fixture, `…de la Vega III`, caught it); a declared delimiter glued to either end (`Jr./`, `-Jr.`) reads alike, while before the first comma, where C10 reads a glued delimiter as part of the word and classify cuts no stem, `Jr.;` is listed by no set and reaches the suffix only by `period_joined_vocab`, which reads it by its chunks (`Jr.` and `;`, the `Lt.Gov.` path) and tags it `vocab:suffix` with no kind, so `John Smith Jr.; MD` keeps it in the credentials, as `Jr.III` is there after a comma or not, a word made only of generation words; `John Smith Jr; MD`, without the period, reads family `Jr;`. Recorded as rules/R8's cost rather than given a kind of its own (two reviews of the fix commits); a view keeps the delimiter in its word's text, as the suffix does, so `John Smith, Jr.; MD` gives generation 'Jr.;'; and `test_every_suffix_words_reading_alternative_and_index_kind_agree` walks every suffix-set word (826 on 2026-10-29) holding the reading taken, the alternative and the index's kind to one answer.
  2. **The kind is read whatever the reading's default.** `st`, a Czech generation written `default = false`, reads `generation` when a caller's `Lexicon` lists it, while its `languages` name no file, since none holds it on. A pack turning such a reading on (phase 3's `cs`, 3f) then needs nothing more for the kind; a word only a caller's own `Lexicon` lists has no row and reads `post-nominal`.
  3. **Each word is placed by its own reading, and separated as the writer separated it.** A `joined` mark is not only the second word of a split credential (`Ph.` `D.`, which takes the first word's reading) but every word of a suffix run written without commas (`Jr. III PhD`), so a view places each word by its reading. Two words of one view are separated by a comma where the suffix holds one anywhere between them, R1's separator (a typed comma, or a semicolon, hyphen, slash or delimiter C10 drops: `MD / Jr. / PhD` gives credentials 'MD, PhD'), and by a space where not, so `MD Jr. PhD` gives credentials 'MD PhD'; the first draft started a new comma-joined entry wherever a word of the other view stood between, writing a comma nobody typed ('MD, PhD'), found by the design-docs review. A connective goes with the word before it (`PhD and MD`, `Jr. and III`), so one joining a generation to a credential dangles at the end of the generation (`Jr. and MD` gives generation 'Jr. and'), accepted rather than given a rule of its own, no corpus name holding the shape.
  4. **A suffix word with no reading is a credential.** `replace()` and `Parser.revise()` write tokens with no `Token.reading` (4a), and a `ParsedName` unpickled from 2.x has none either, so `replace(suffix="Jr.")` reads credentials 'Jr.' and the 2.8 pickle of `Dr. Juan Q. de la Vega III` credentials 'III': nothing records what the word was read as, and the catch-all is the view that claims least. A name word a run took into the suffix by position is a credential too, 4a's reading of it (`Smith, Jr., John` gives credentials 'John').
  5. **The views stay out of `as_dict()`, `--json` and the repr**, as `family_base` and the other derived views do: `as_dict()` is the seven fields `replace()` takes back. They are `render()` keys and assertable fields of a rule's example lines.
  6. **The snapshot records them** (`tools/differential/snapshot.py`, a `RECORDED_LATER` pair): `generation` where the suffix holds one, and `credentials` beside it where the suffix holds both, so a row with neither is a suffix of credentials only, or none. No other key moved on recording.
