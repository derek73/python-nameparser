# salutation-titles — salutations, offices and ranks for the languages TITLES had none for (2026-10-04, #606)

Closes #606 (#662 later added owura and bapak, last bullet). No parser code moves: 41 words join TITLES and one, knt (Knight, beside kt), joins SUFFIX_ACRONYMS with a `Knt` case mask. The class is decided once — a word that addresses or ranks a person and is written in FRONT of the name — and each word is then argued on #vocabulary-collisions C-i, the position test, because TITLES has no ambiguous subset and the only two answers available are ship and do not ship. The per-word lists, grouped by language, are the titles carrying `issue = 606` (`uv run python tools/vocab/query.py --issue 606 --kind title`; one line per file a word is in; the #606 block of nameparser/config/titles.py until 2026-10-18, then TOML comments in nameparser/vocab/und.toml, and since 2026-10-19 (#750) the salutations' entries in their language files, and the offices', the noble Counts' and knight's too since the second part of #750 the same day).

## Standing

- A word that addresses or ranks a person and is written in FRONT of the name ships as a title unless it is borne as an ordinary name in the leading position, the position test of vocabulary-collisions' C-i, TITLES having no ambiguous subset to mark a collision in, so the only answers are ship and do not ship — 2026-10-04 #606.
- One exception to C-i, for TITLES alone: a word borne as a name only RARELY as the first word of one ships on the precedent of `graf` (`herra`, `batoni`, `hrabia`, `kreivi` and the rest of the graf class, each with its counts on its title reading's `note`), and no word in the particle or suffix sets, and no later title, takes it without its own counts — 2026-10-04 #606.
- The cost falls on a name that BEGINS with such a word: `Greve Anna` reads title 'Greve', family 'Anna' and `Herra Wijaya` title 'Herra', family 'Wijaya'; a declared `FAMILY_FIRST` does not help (`Hrabia Anna` reads title 'Hrabia' under it); the family comma keeps the word as the family where no name word stands behind it (`Hrabia, Anna` reads given 'Anna', family 'Hrabia', and so does `Hrabia Jr., Anna`), while a name word behind it makes it the title by H6 (`Hrabia Nowak, Anna` reads title 'Hrabia', family 'Nowak'); a name that ends in the word keeps it (`Gladys Knight`, `Knight, Gladys` read family 'Knight'), unless a period follows it (`Gladys Knight.` reads title 'Knight.', family 'Gladys', by H5, accepted rather than prevented) — 2026-10-04 #606.
- Words judged a COMMON leading-position name stay out (`thiru`, `puan`, `pan`, `rodina`, `ông` and its pair `bà`, and `marshal` and `justice`, whose accepted costs their entries state: `Field Marshal Bernard Montgomery` reads title 'Field', given 'Marshal'), and words with no evidence either way are not shipped (`gróf`, `cik`, `paron`, `tikin`, `janab`), each an `excluded` entry in its language's file (`uv run python tools/vocab/query.py --excluded title --issue 606`) — 2026-10-04 #606.
- Accents are not folded, so `paní` and `härra` match only their accented spellings (`Paní Nováková` reads title 'Paní', `Pani Anna` reads given 'Pani'); folding the lexicon would turn both into collisions, and whoever proposes it re-argues the entry — 2026-10-04 #606.
- `gravos` was dropped, not excluded, since nothing showed it is the Greek word for Count; `κόμης`, a Greek surname written first in official lists, is neither added nor excluded, and adding it is a decision still to make — 2026-10-04 #606.
- `knt` (Knight, beside `kt`) is a suffix acronym with a `Knt` case mask: `Mary Knt` reads suffix 'Knt' — 2026-10-04 #606.
- `owura` (Akan) and `bapak` (Indonesian) are titles, on counts under 100 US Census bearers each and none found on Wikidata, and `malam` (Hausa) and `bay` (Turkish) are excluded, each an `excluded` entry with its counts — 2026-10-17 #662.

- The Italian abbreviated salutations and letter-heading honorifics whose letters after the full stop are the word's own ending ship as titles with `cased` masks: `sig.ra`, `sig.na`, `dott.ssa`, `prof.ssa`, `gent.mo`, `gent.ma`, `gent.le`, `ill.mo`, `ill.ma`, `ch.mo`, `ch.ma`, `preg.mo`, `preg.ma` (`Dott.ssa Anna Bianchi` reads title 'Dott.ssa', given 'Anna'; `SIG.RA MARIA ROSSI` repairs to 'Sig.ra Maria Rossi'); the bare `sig` and `ing` are excluded as given names, their period-marked spellings already titles by H2's shape — 2026-10-22 #840.

- **The graf class, decided (Derek, 2026-10-04).** A word borne as a name, but RARELY as the first word of one, ships anyway, on the precedent of graf, which shipped though Steffi Graf exists. This is a judgment on each word's incidence in the leading position — the position C-i asks about — and not a rule, and it is an EXCEPTION to C-i rather than an application of it: C-i is an iff with no threshold, and herra, batoni, hrabia and kreivi were measured as borne in that position, so C-i says do not ship them (TITLES having no ambiguous subset to mark them in). What the exception adds is a weighing C-i does not make, a small measured incidence against the benefit of the title reading, and it is scoped to TITLES and to the words this entry names. It licenses nothing in the particle or suffix sets, and a later TITLES word that wants it is argued on its own counts. The title claim acts only in front, so most bearers are untouched in the forms they write: `Gladys Knight` and `Knight, Gladys` keep family Knight. The cost falls on a name that BEGINS with the word, the cost `Graf Steffi` already paid: `Greve Anna` reads title Greve, family Anna, and a given name is lost the same way, `Herra Wijaya` reading title Herra, family Wijaya. Declaring `FAMILY_FIRST` does not help, since the title claim is read before the order (`Hrabia Anna` reads title Hrabia under it); the family comma does. Two further limits, both pre-existing: a trailing period makes the word the title by rules/H5 (`Gladys Knight.` reads title `Knight.`, as `Mary Jane King.` does), and every word this entry adds widens that rule's reach, H5 claiming any listed title behind a period. Accepted rather than prevented, and stated at rules/H1's Accepted clause. The issue proposed the class for three words — greve (Scandinavian Count), graaf (Dutch Count), knight. The spot-check the issue asked for (forebears.io, 2026-10-04, worldwide counts rounded) found ten more of its "no known collision" words borne, and Derek extended the decision to all of them rather than excluding them: herra, batoni, ponas, counsel, ponia, the Filipino ginoo, ginang and binibini, and two whose registers often write the surname first, hrabia and kreivi. Each word's counts are on the `note` of its title reading in one of its files (herra's in fi.toml), a note beginning "borne" (`uv run python tools/vocab/query.py --issue 606 --kind title`; this bullet held them until 2026-10-19, #750). Those last two, and the given-name uses of herra and batoni, are the leading-position bearers this class accepts, and were decided on with their counts in hand. The line is NOT reproducible from this record, and that is said rather than hidden: the accepted side was counted, while the excluded side — thiru, puan, pan, rodina, ông/bà — was judged from general knowledge of each word, as #606 drafted it, with no count taken (the count source refused further lookups the same day).
