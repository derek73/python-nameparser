# T4 — the bare letter as an initial

- 2026-10-18, #723 — A ONE-CASE BARE LETTER IS THE INITIAL ITS CAPITAL IS. #721 applied S10's "a name written wholly in one case says nothing about any letter in it" to the one-letter roman numerals only (decisions/S10, the Open line of its /simplify entry), so a bare lowercase `j` was a name word where `J` was an initial, and every rule that reads an initial differently read the two spellings of one name differently: M4 (`j nee smith` family 'j', `J NEE SMITH` given 'J'), O5 (a lone `j` reported `given-or-family`, `J` did not), and P3's count, which passes over an initial (`jose j y lopez` family 'j y lopez', `JOSE J Y LOPEZ` middle 'J Y', family 'LOPEZ', which 1.4.0 read too). The rule is now stated once, for every letter, and the numeral case is an instance of it: in a name written in one case, a bare letter is tagged an initial wherever its capital would be. Its capital is asked of the bare-capital shape (one ASCII capital), so `é` and `ж`, whose capitals are no initial, read as before in both spellings. `ı` and `ſ`, whose capitals are `I` and `S`, are tagged initials too; `ı`'s twin is not a true one, since `I` is also the marked Catalan connective and `ı` is in no vocabulary (`JOSE I LOPEZ` reports `conjunction-or-initial`, `jose ı lopez` nothing). Measured on the first commit's tree against 16ee2851, a dated snapshot from a scratch grid not committed: letters a–z, `é` and `ж`; heads (a, b, c) = (john, smith, jones), (jose, lopez, garcia), (mary, ma, lee); all three orders; the 35 shapes `{n}`, `{n} nee {b}`, `{a} {n}`, `{n} {a}`, `{a} {n} {b}`, `{a} {n} y {b}`, `{a} {n} e {b}`, `{b}, {n}`, `{b}, {n} y {c}`, `{b}, {a} {n}`, `, {n}`, `{n}, phd`, `{a} {n}, phd`, `{a} {n}, jr.`, `{a} nee {b} {n}`, `{a} nee {n}`, `{a} {b} nee {n} {c}`, `dr {n}`, `dr {n} {b}`, `sir {n} {b}`, `{a} van {n}`, `{n} van {b}`, `{a} van {n} {b}`, `abdul {n}`, `{n} {a} {b}`, `{a} {n} {n} {b}`, `{a} {n}. {b}`, `({c}) {n} {b}`, `{n} y {b}`, `{a} {n} phd`, `{a} {b} {n} prof.`, `{n} {b} {n}`, `{n} {n}`, `{a} {n} de la {b}`, `{b} {n} jr`; each lowercase text against its `.upper()`, compared on case-folded fields, sorted report kinds and `initials()`, keyed by order and text, so the 8,820 products collapse to 7,794 distinct pairs where a shape has no head. 16ee2851 disagreed on 504 of them, this tree on none, and no capital spelling moved. The committed invariant is `test_a_one_case_name_reads_as_its_capitals` (tests/v2/test_properties.py), whose grid gains the issue's shapes and the letters `j`, `y` and `e`; its stated controls are the recompute.
  WHAT MOVES: lower-case letters in a name whose own words are written in one case, each onto its capital twin's reading — wholly lower-case names, and (since the case question is asked of the own words, P3) a one-case name beside a clause of another case, so `j nee Smith` reads given 'j' as `J nee Smith` does. In three classes — a lone letter loses O5's report, a letter before a maiden marker keeps the given name M4 would have taken, and a letter beside a single-letter link is passed over by P3's count, so the link in what is then a three-word name stays a name word. No corpus name moved (the snapshot and the CLDR, GND and native-script agreement records are unchanged). And P3's Accepted paragraph on a declined marker loses its "one shape" in which the name's own words read differently in the two spellings: `dr. née juan y lopez` read family 'juan y lopez' where `DR. NÉE JUAN Y LOPEZ` reads middle 'JUAN Y', because the `y` past the declined marker is outside the own words P3's fork reads; it is this rule's letter now, and reads as its capital does.
  And one move the snapshot cannot see (R4): case repair of a connective letter in a one-case maiden clause, now tagged an initial as its capital is, so `jose lopez nee y garcia` repairs to `Jose Lopez (Y Garcia)` where it gave `(y Garcia)` — the same tag in the two spellings that P3's rewritten paragraph intends. No corpus name's `capitalized()` moved (the design-docs review compared every one across the two trees).
  A GAP THE RULE DOES NOT CLOSE, found by the design-docs review and present on 16ee2851 too: under `Policy(lenient_comma_suffixes=False)` the strict comma-suffix test asks a one-letter numeral's shape as written, not its tag, so `john smith, v` reads suffix 'v' where `JOHN SMITH, V` reads given 'V', and `smith, john, i` reports nothing where `SMITH, JOHN, I` reports `comma-structure`. The committed grid runs the default policy only. Left for its own change.
  The connective letters are left where P3 put them. Among the name's own words, P3's fork answers by vocabulary before this rule is asked — the marked `e` and `i` read as initials and report, the unmarked `y` as a connective — and does so in both spellings, so the invariant already held for them; a connective letter outside the own words was always read by the bare-capital shape in its capital spelling, and its lower-case spelling now follows.
- 2026-10-18, #723's code review — A LETTER OUTSIDE THE OWN WORDS ASKS THE WHOLE NAME'S CASE. The first version gated the new tag on the one-case fact classify records, which is asked of the name's own words only (P3), so a lower-case letter in a clause of a name whose own words are capitals passed it: `MARIA GARCIA NEE LOPEZ y FERNANDEZ` and `MARIA GARCIA (LOPEZ y FERNANDEZ)` tagged the `y` an initial (case repair wrote `Lopez Y Fernandez`), and `DR. NÉE JUAN y LOPEZ` moved from family 'JUAN y LOPEZ' to middle 'JUAN y' — mixed-case writing, which this rule reads as no initial, and moves the twin-only grids could not see, since every pair in them is wholly one case. The fix asks a word outside the own words about the whole name instead; an own word keeps the own-words fact, so a clause beside the name still changes nothing about the name's own letters (P3). The defect predated #723 for the numeral letters, which #721 gated the same way; they are read by this rule now and move with it, and the move is not only a tag (found by the design-docs review). After a marker among capitals, a lower-case numeral no longer holds the clause up as an initial would: `MARY SMITH NEE v JONES` reads middle 'SMITH NEE v', family 'JONES' (on 16ee2851, maiden 'v JONES'), `MARY SMITH NEE i JONES` family 'NEE i JONES', and `JANE DOE NEE SMITH v` drops `suffix-or-name`. Each is the reading of its mixed-case spelling (`Mary Smith nee v Jones`) and of 2.4.2 (and 1.4.0 for the first), so only the unreleased #721 had moved them and no release bullet is owed; the capital twin `MARY SMITH NEE V JONES` keeps maiden 'V JONES'.
