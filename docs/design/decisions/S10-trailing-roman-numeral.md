- 2026-10-17 (Derek), #718 — A ONE-LETTER NUMERAL NEEDS WORDS TO SPARE, AND THE RULE IS WRITTEN DOWN. Until this entry no rule stated the comma-less numeral at all: the behavior was pinned in tests/v2/cases.py and credited in code comments to rules/S2, whose statement covers the suffix vocabulary and never the shape fork that reads `VI` to `X`, nor the initial veto that sends `V`, `I` and `X` there. The extraction found one reading worth changing. `John V` and `Malcolm X` read suffix with an EMPTY family in every 2.x release (2.0.0 through 2.4.2 measured), alongside 2.0.0's `Johnson PhD` fix; 1.4.0 read family `V` and `X`. Derek: a generational V is mostly archaic, X rarer still and I hardly ever written without a comma (Sr. and Senior are what people write), so a capital letter standing alone behind the given name is far likelier an initial or a one-letter surname. The numeral was never what S2's Accepted clause (an unambiguous suffix is consumed even when that leaves no family) covers, since the fork has always reported itself ambiguous; S4's count is the analogue, and the numeral now asks it.
  WHAT MOVES. The count is S4's own, in name UNITS, so a particle chain is one word (P2) and a title none.   THE LOWERCASE HALF. A numeral in lower case in a mixed-case name is not how anyone writes an initial, so it is the generation and reports nothing (Derek). Measured before the change, the reports had no rationale: `John Smith v` and `John Smith i` were silent because the listed letters reach the vocabulary path, `John Smith vi` and `John Smith x` reported because the shape fork reports everything it takes, and the one-case `john smith i` reported because the connective tag (P3, #479) makes a one-case `i` an initial. Now the mixed-case lower case is uniformly silent and a one-case name is counted like capitals, `v` excepted (the ACCEPTED paragraph below). `john smith i` keeps its report, by Derek's call.

  ACCEPTED, and recorded in rules/S10: a one-case lowercase `v` is read as the vocabulary reads it, so `john v` still loses its family and `john smith v` reports nothing, where `JOHN V` and `john x` keep their family and `JOHN SMITH V` reports — the listed lowercase letter is taken as a suffix word before the numeral fork is reached, and closing that means touching the suffix-vocabulary path, which is not this change. - 2026-10-18 (Derek), #718 — THE DECLINED NUMERAL REPORTS TOO, BOTH DIRECTIONS AS S4'S FORK DOES. - 2026-10-18 (Derek), #721 — A ONE-CASE ONE-LETTER NUMERAL IS THE INITIAL ITS CAPITAL IS, AND A LETTER ENDING ITS PART IS NO CONNECTIVE. Two Accepted gaps of the entries above close.
