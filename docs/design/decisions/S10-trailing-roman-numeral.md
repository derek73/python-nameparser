# S10 — the trailing roman numeral

- 2026-10-17 (Derek), #718 — A ONE-LETTER NUMERAL NEEDS WORDS TO SPARE, AND THE RULE IS WRITTEN DOWN. Until this entry no rule stated the comma-less numeral at all: the behavior was pinned in tests/v2/cases.py and credited in code comments to rules/S2, whose statement covers the suffix vocabulary and never the shape fork that reads `VI` to `X`, nor the initial veto that sends `V`, `I` and `X` there. The extraction found one reading worth changing. `John V` and `Malcolm X` read suffix with an EMPTY family in every 2.x release (2.0.0 through 2.4.2 measured), alongside 2.0.0's `Johnson PhD` fix; 1.4.0 read family `V` and `X`. Derek: a generational V is mostly archaic, X rarer still and I hardly ever written without a comma (Sr. and Senior are what people write), so a capital letter standing alone behind the given name is far likelier an initial or a one-letter surname. The numeral was never what S2's Accepted clause (an unambiguous suffix is consumed even when that leaves no family) covers, since the fork has always reported itself ambiguous; S4's count is the analogue, and the numeral now asks it.
  WHAT MOVES. The count is S4's own, in name UNITS, so a particle chain is one word (P2) and a title none. Measured on the shipped change against origin/master at 958d252e, the snapshot moves four corpus names: `John V` and `Mohamad X` (family V and X, as 1.4.0 read them), `John née Jones Smith V` (the clause-free name is `John V`, so the V is no suffix and the clause keeps it, maiden 'Jones Smith V' — M2's Accepted clause on the lone numeral), and `Freiherr von Richthofen V`, family 'von Richthofen V': behind a title the chain is the one name word, so the V has no word to spare and the chain takes it as P2 takes any name word, as it already took `Freiherr von Richthofen Ma`. That last is the reading a scratch prototype counting raw pieces did not show, and the one most worth a second look. Four case rows moved with it (`strict_comma_reads_the_bare_numeral_into_the_run`: `Smith V, Ma` under the strict knob reads family V, suffix Ma, as 1.4.0 did; and the rows whose purpose needed a suffix kept their ids on a spelling that still has one — `abdul Doe nee Smith V`, `John III, Dr.`, `John III, Sir` — with the old inputs re-pinned as new rows: `abdul nee Smith V` maiden 'Smith V', `John V, Dr.` and `John V, Sir` given John, family V). `Sir abdul V` reads given 'abdul V': the V is a name word, and P5's licence behind a given-name title joins it. Nothing with a word to spare moves: `John W. Ingram V`, `King John Alexander V`, `Larry James Johnson I` (a v1 test) keep the suffix and the report. A title is no word to spare, so `Dr Smith V` reads given Smith, family V, which is 1.4.0's reading too, where `Dr. von Richthofen V` keeps suffix V, the particle and the surname being two words there.
  THE LOWERCASE HALF. A numeral in lower case in a mixed-case name is not how anyone writes an initial, so it is the generation and reports nothing (Derek). Measured before the change, the reports had no rationale: `John Smith v` and `John Smith i` were silent because the listed letters reach the vocabulary path, `John Smith vi` and `John Smith x` reported because the shape fork reports everything it takes, and the one-case `john smith i` reported because the connective tag (P3, #479) makes a one-case `i` an initial. Now the mixed-case lower case is uniformly silent and a one-case name is counted like capitals. `john smith i` keeps its report, by Derek's call.
  Declined: dropping the shape fork for single capital letters outright. Prototyped in a scratch edit, it moved 26 readings and cost the real generational names the count keeps: `John W. Ingram V` read middle 'W. Ingram', family 'V', and `John van der Berg V` folded the numeral into the particle chain. A variant that dropped the fork for I and X only, on top of the count, moved four more, among them the v1 test `Larry James Johnson I`.
  ACCEPTED, and recorded in rules/S10: a one-case lowercase `v` is read as the vocabulary reads it, so `john v` still loses its family where `JOHN V` and `john x` keep theirs — the listed lowercase letter is taken as a suffix word before the numeral fork is reached, and closing that means touching the suffix-vocabulary path, which is not this change. And a one-letter numeral with a credential behind it is not the last word and is read as a name word (`John Smith V PhD` → family V, 1.4.0 parity), where `John Smith III PhD` reads suffix 'III PhD'.
  Recompute: the moved names are this commit's diff of tools/differential/snapshot.jsonl (or `uv run python tools/differential/snapshot.py` on the parent tree with this commit's code); the prototype figures in the Declined paragraph were measured over every cases.py row and snapshot name with the fork patched in a scratch process.
- 2026-10-18 (Derek), #718 — THE DECLINED NUMERAL REPORTS TOO, BOTH DIRECTIONS AS S4'S FORK DOES. The count's first form reported only the generation, so `John V` read family V in silence while `Jack Ma` reports keeping its family; Derek: report both, as `Jack Ma` does. A declined numeral is a pick of the walk, and its branches are taken in three places, so three sites report it (mechanisms.md#AMBIGUITY-AT-THE-DECISION-SITE): assign where it stays a lone name word (`John V` — "read as a family name rather than a generational suffix"), the particle chain where it joins the run (`Freiherr von Richthofen V`), and a maiden clause that keeps it as its last word (`John née Jones Smith V`, `abdul nee Smith V`). Each wording is its own, since the acronym pick's ("both a post-nominal and an ordinary name") misdescribes a numeral.
  ONE STAND-DOWN, FOUND BY INV10 rather than designed. In a one-case name the connective `i` reaches the fork as a letter classify has already reported as `conjunction-or-initial`, read as an initial, and the new report put a second report on the same token: 138 rows of the connective grid in tests/v2/test_properties.py (`AND ROVIRA I`, `HENRY I`) tripped INV10's kind pair. The two reports did not contradict each other — both read the letter as a name word — but they named one reading twice, so the numeral's report stands down where the initial reading is already reported, the shape of rules/S7's "a member its own capitals already made the credential reports nothing new". The guard was left untouched: its kind pair still means "two readings" everywhere else, and narrowing it to suit this change would have retaken its recorded control. `HENRY I` therefore reports `conjunction-or-initial` alone, and moved in test_assemble's contrast list from the withdrawn side to the side where the fork resolved to a name word.
