# P9 — a particle that is also a suffix word, beside a family comma

- 2026-10-02 (Derek), #573 — A WORD OF BOTH THE PARTICLE AND THE UNAMBIGUOUS SUFFIX VOCABULARY READS ALIKE IN EVERY CASE AT THE THREE SITES WHERE THE UNIFORM CASE SPLIT IT. Resolves the shapes decisions/C4's 2026-10-01 #554 entry left open. `vd` and `mc` split in names written wholly in one case, in three shapes, each split one stage acting on a reading the next never took. The mixed-case spellings were not all right either: `Smith vd Ma, John` read family 'Smith Ma', suffix 'vd' in 2.2.0 and 2.3.0, #530 having fixed it only in this cycle, and the fixes below move mixed-case names as well as uniform ones (blast radius, below).
  BEFORE A FAMILY COMMA (P2 when decided, rules/P9 since its 2026-10-19 split). `SMITH VD MA, JOHN` read family 'SMITH MA', suffix 'VD' (2.2.0, 2.3.0 and this cycle's tree; 2.0.0 and 2.1.0 read family 'SMITH VD MA'). In one case the S2 trailing reading takes `VD MA`, so the particle chain stops before `VD`; the part before a family comma then takes strict suffix words one piece at a time, and the lone `VD` was peeled from between two family words. DECIDED in that per-piece read: a particle that is suffix vocabulary too, with a name piece behind it in the part, heads that name and stays family (P2's join), so `VD MA` reads family whatever the case. Not decided in the chain: a comma followed by no name word reads that same part positionally, trailing credential included (`Berg de MA, Prof.` keeps suffix 'MA' and its report), and the chain runs before that choice is made. A first draft skipped the chain's stop for the whole family part and moved exactly that shape; a widened grid caught it. Declined: reading `VD MA` as credentials, family 'SMITH', which the comma's naming of the family argues against. `SMITH VD, JOHN` and `SMITH VD JR, JOHN` keep the suffix: nothing, or a suffix word, stands behind the particle.
  BETWEEN CREDENTIALS AFTER A FAMILY COMMA (P6 when decided, rules/P9 since its 2026-10-19 split). `DOE, JANE PHD VD MA` read family 'VD DOE', suffix 'PHD, MA': assign had read `PHD VD MA` whole, and P6's attachment, which outranks the suffix reading for such a word by design, reached into the run and pulled `VD` out. DECIDED: P6 gains a third exception — a lone such word with a post-nominal read in front of it and another behind, a title between it and the one in front transparent (H5), keeps the post-nominal reading in any case, reported as S2's credential fork. The test is the roles assign gave, asking whether it read the run whole, so a member read as the credential in front speaks as a degree does (`DOE, JANE MA VD PHD` stays whole); a first draft asked S2's company query instead, which counts only an unambiguous credential in front, and missed that shape and the titled one (`DOE, JANE PHD PROF. VD MA`). A generation counts on either side, and that is a decision rather than a side effect: P6's tussenvoegsel stands right behind the given name, so any post-nominal between them says the word is not one, and `Berg, Jan PhD vd Jr.` reads suffix 'PhD vd Jr.' where 2.2.0 and 2.3.0 read family 'vd Berg', suffix 'PhD, Jr.' — the shape `Berg, Jan van Jr.` P6's rationale names has no post-nominal in front and still attaches. No title is skipped BEHIND the word: a title there leaves it a name in assign (`DOE, JANE PHD VD PROF. MA` reads middle 'VD PROF.', as before), so a skip there was dead code, measured never to fire over the grid below, and was removed. Boundaries: with nothing behind it the word ends the name and attaches (`Doe, Jane PhD vd` → family 'vd Doe', 2.2.0's and 2.3.0's reading); two such words side by side are one particle run, which P6 takes whole as its first exception says (`DOE, JANE PHD VD MC MA` → family 'VD MC DOE', unchanged); a plain particle between credentials was never read as a post-nominal and attaches as before (`Doe, Jane PhD de PhD` → family 'de Doe'). Declined: middle 'VD MA', 2.0.0's and 2.1.0's reading — under #554 run membership asks what the word IS.
  ONE NEVER-GIVEN PARTICLE OPENING THE GIVEN PART (S2, P1). `SMITH, VD MA` read family 'SMITH VD', suffix 'MA'. S2's given-part slot reads a member ending the given part as the credential because "the words to spare are there by construction", the first word after the comma being the given name; a never-given particle is not one — P1's fold takes it forward into the family — so `MA` was read as a credential with no given name left, and the fold took `VD` alone. DECIDED: where the given part opens with a never-given particle and nothing but particles stands before the member, chained (`VD DE LA`) or not, the member is the word the particle attaches to and reads as a name: family 'SMITH VD MA', the reading of 2.0.0 through 2.3.0 and of `Smith, vd Ma`. The particle test is P1's own predicate (`_pieces.is_lone_never_given_particle`), so the slot cannot drift from the fold it predicts. Plain never-given particles share the shape (`smith, de ma`). Boundaries: capitals in a mixed-case name still make the member the credential, as they do with no words to spare anywhere (`Smith, de MA` keeps suffix 'MA'); an ambiguous particle can be a given name (`SMITH, VAN MA` keeps given 'VAN', suffix 'MA'); and a name word between the particle and the member is the word it attaches to, leaving the member a word to spare (`SMITH, VD BERG MA` keeps suffix 'MA'). Declined: family 'SMITH', suffix 'VD MA' (as `SMITH, PHD MA` reads) — under #554 `VD` speaks for nothing, so only the missing given name could have made `MA` a credential, and it is the particle's surname.
  NOT DECIDED HERE: the mixed-case `Doe, Jane PhD vd Ma` (middle 'vd Ma', silent), the issue's fourth bullet. Its reading is unchanged; whether the family-comma path should report the chain's fork there is a separate question. Open: #578 (decided the same day, the S2 section's #578 entry: silent, as it reads). Nor the positional read of a part before a comma followed by no name word, where the case still splits the word: `Smith vd Ma, Prof.` reads given 'Smith', family 'vd Ma', and `SMITH VD MA, PROF.` family 'SMITH', suffix 'VD MA', on this tree and on `e56dd296` alike — that part reads as the comma-less name does, and the comma-less name splits the same way.
  BLAST RADIUS, measured 2026-10-02 with `e56dd296` (the pre-change master) as the comparator. The gate's output is byte-identical at all five baselines apart from the tree path: no corpus name holds these shapes, the rules.md examples aside. Recompute: from the pool `vd mc de van do Ma MA PhD Jr John Berg V la Prof.`, build every `<1 or 2 words>, <exactly 2 words>`, `<3 words>, <1 word>`, `Smith <the same 1-3 words>, John`, `Doe, Jane <2-4 words>` and `Smith, <2-4 words>`; deduplicate, then parse each as written, `.upper()` and `.lower()`, keyed by the resulting text; keep the entries whose `as_dict()` or sorted ambiguity kinds differ between the trees, and class each by whether the new family holds every old family word and more, or the old family lost only `vd`/`mc` while the suffix kept every word.
