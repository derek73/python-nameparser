## Standing

- C-i: whether a word is marked ambiguous turns on whether it is borne as an ordinary name in the position the vocabulary claim acts on, a bearer elsewhere not counting (`Bimal De` leaves `de` never-given, the surname being trailing), and under uncertainty it is marked ambiguous; for the particles being borne there earns the marking, while for the suffix acronyms it only opens the question, which relative frequency answers by removal, marking or leaving the word (decisions/suffix-acronym-collisions) — 2026-08-16 #348/#360/#342/#385; 2026-08-17.
- C-i applies to particles, suffix acronyms and titles alike, with one exception for titles alone: words measured as borne in the leading position at small incidence ship as titles (`herra`, `batoni`, `hrabia`, `kreivi`), and the exception reaches no other set or word without its own counts — 2026-08-16 #348/#360/#342/#385; 2026-10-04 #606.
- The gap between the ambiguous shares of the particles and of the suffix acronyms is base rate, not inconsistency: most particles are short words that double as names and most credential acronyms are not, so the shares are not to be harmonized — 2026-08-16 #348/#360/#342/#385.
- C-ii: where two sets claim a word and neither reading is a name, precedence is a frequency judgement recorded per word; `vd`, a never-given particle and a credential acronym, takes the Dutch van der reading where a family is named (`Berg, Jan vd` → family 'vd Berg') — 2026-08-16 #348/#360/#342/#385.
- `mc`, the other word of both the particle and the unambiguous suffix vocabulary, inherits `vd`'s answer through rules/P6's statement for the shape without being weighed itself (`Berg, Jan mc` → family 'mc Berg'), a cost #454 accepted; `do`, in the ambiguous suffix half, is outside that precedence and joins the family by P6's ordinary attachment (`Berg, Jan do` → family 'do Berg'), except in capitals, where S6 reads it as the credential (`Berg, Jan DO` → suffix 'DO') — 2026-08-16 #348/#360/#342/#385.
- `ó` and `ní` are never-given particles (`Liam Ó Murchú` and `Ó Murchú` → family 'Ó Murchú'); `ua`, `binti` and `binte` are ambiguous, `ua` for want of a thorough search and `binti` and `binte` because Binti is a Swahili given name (`Binti Jua` → given 'Binti', reported) — 2026-10-04 #604.

Elsewhere: #454's acceptance of `mc`'s inherited answer is decisions/suffix-acronym-collisions' 2026-09-07 #342/#454 bundle; for P3's count, a word placed to join counts as a connective whatever other vocabulary holds it, decisions/P3's 2026-09-20 #397 entry answering a C-ii question in its THE COUNT REACHES THE CLASS paragraph, scoped to that count; `mc` and `ste` left the ambiguous half of the particles under #360 (decisions/P1); `do` left TITLES in #296's audit, so `Do Quang Minh` reads given 'Do' and reports the fork, and the overlap of TITLES with the ambiguous particles, `freiherr` and `st` since, is kept load-bearing by the particle-line note in decisions/vocabulary-exclusions; `rai` left the suffix acronyms rather than earning the marking, and the relative-frequency criterion that chooses between removal and the marking is decisions/suffix-acronym-collisions'.

Not standing: the C-i correction's call to re-judge `lo` and `el` as candidate particles, which #360 took up, judging `lo` ambiguous and leaving `el` needing judgement; neither was added to the particles — 2026-08-17.

Elsewhere: C-i reaches the titles through a `given` reading, which makes a title word a title only inside a title phrase (rules/H9; the vocabulary is decisions/vocabulary-model's 2026-10-25 #752, phase 2i entry); where the word reads as a name, which forks are reported, #348's question, is decisions/H9's 2026-10-26 #752, phase 2i (Derek) and phase 2i, the report's reach (Derek) entries, a word no listed phrase holds and one after a comma reporting nothing; since phase 2a the particles' and suffix acronyms' ambiguous markings are derived from name readings too (decisions/vocabulary-model's 2026-10-25 #752 — A CLASH WITH A NAME IS A NAME READING entry).

- A credential written as a word (`pc`, `dl`) may also earn the marking by being written as a person's initials in the position its claim acts on, which is C-i's ground read for initials, and as for a name it takes a per-word count of that writing on the word's name reading (ORCID's boxes for `pc` and `dl`) — 2026-10-30 #965.

- **C-i, vocabulary vs. name.** (For the suffix acronyms, the "iff" superseded by decisions/suffix-acronym-collisions' 2026-09-07 #342/#454 bundle: being borne only opens the question, and relative frequency chooses removal, marking or leaving the word.) A word belongs in its set's ambiguous subset iff it is borne as an ordinary name IN THE POSITION THE VOCABULARY CLAIM ACTS ON. Existence of a bearer anywhere is not the test, and the first draft of this criterion (recorded 2026-08-16, corrected 2026-08-17) got that wrong. Under uncertainty, default to AMBIGUOUS. This generalizes the evidence standard already stated in NON_GIVEN_NAME_PARTICLES' docstring — a wrong unambiguous claim misparses a real person, a wrong ambiguous marking only adds a flag — from "which set" to "which subset". Applies uniformly to particles, suffix_acronyms and titles.
