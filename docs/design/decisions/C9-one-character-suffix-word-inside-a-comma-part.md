# C9 — a one-character suffix word inside a comma part

## Standing

- Inside a comma part a one-character suffix word is read by what stands before it: behind another suffix word it continues the run, with a period or without (`Smith, PSM I` → suffix 'PSM I'; `Smith, PSM I.` → suffix 'PSM I.'), and behind a name word an ASCII period makes it a middle initial while the bare letter, with no further comma after it, is the suffix (`Smith, John V.` → middle 'V.'; `Smith, John V` → suffix 'V', v1 parity) — 2026-08-25 #430.
- The class is one-character suffix vocabulary, not a roman numeral (`Smith, John 2.` → middle '2.'), and a run's first word that is title vocabulary too stays in the run (`Smith, MD I` → suffix 'MD I'; `Smith, Jr. I` → suffix 'Jr. I') — 2026-08-25 #430.
- The no-comma numeral predicate is not asked in the given part, so a numeral behind an initial stays the suffix (`Chang, Andy C I` → middle 'C', suffix 'I') — 2026-08-25 #430.
- The given part's walk is strict-blind and the fork reports nothing: `Smith, John V` reads suffix 'V' under `lenient_comma_suffixes=False` as under the default, with no `suffix-or-name`, where `John Smith V` reports — 2026-08-25 #430.

Elsewhere: the strict switch, which vetoes the numeral in the comma's decision and in a part that is nothing but suffix words — decisions/C14's 2026-10-19 (Derek), #761 entry; a credential starting a run to the end of its part, which reads `Smith, John PSM I I.` as suffix 'PSM I I.' where #430 had accepted middle 'I I.' — decisions/S8's 2026-10-04 (Derek), #602 entry; the same run, unrecorded in that entry, reads `Smith, John PhD I, Jr.` and `Smith, John PhD I., Jr.` as suffix 'PhD I, Jr.' and 'PhD I., Jr.' (middle 'I' and 'I.' before #602), which rules/C9's further-comma clause and its Accepted bullet still give as the middle initial; a bare letter behind a name word with a suffix comma after it stays a middle initial (`Smith, John V, Jr.` → middle 'V', suffix 'Jr.') — decisions/v1-xfail-triage's 2026-09-01 (Derek's triage) entry; an ideographic or fullwidth full stop is no abbreviation mark there (`Smith, John V。` → suffix 'V。') — decisions/cjk-full-stops' 2026-09-10 #322/#323 bundle.

Open: [#819](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/819) (whether rules/C9's further-comma clause or the parser is right for a letter behind a credential since #602, `Smith, John PhD I, Jr.`).

## Log

- 2026-08-25 #430 / #432 (its accepted `Smith, John PSM I I.` reading superseded by #602, decisions/S8) — one boundary, fixed as one: inside a comma segment a ONE-CHARACTER suffix word — the only kind a reader could take for an initial — is read by WHAT STANDS BEFORE IT. Behind another suffix it describes that suffix and continues the run, period included — `Smith, PSM I` and `Smith, PSM I.` are suffix 'PSM I'/'PSM I.' where they read given 'PSM' plus suffix 'I', an initial in that slot being no shape anyone writes. Behind a name word the period is decisive, marking an abbreviation and so name material — `Smith, John V.` is middle 'V.' where it read suffix 'V.', which is what `Smith, John B.` always did; the only thing that made V differ is that V is also suffix vocabulary. The bare form is the boundary and does not move: `Smith, John V` stays suffix 'V' (v1 parity, #144), which is why the test is the PERIOD and not the numeral. SCOPE, stated as the code reads rather than as the intent was argued: the class is one-character SUFFIX VOCABULARY, not "a roman numeral". It sweeps in `2` (`Smith, John 2.` is middle '2.'), misses `X` and `IX` (roman, but not vocabulary — `Smith, PSM X` is unchanged), and never touched `II`/`III`/`IV` at all, which are two characters and so were never initial-shaped and never vetoed. Anyone adding a one-character suffix in a script that has initials extends it. And the strict knob is consulted: under `lenient_comma_suffixes=False` the initial veto stands and the run ends at the numeral, which the first draft silently overrode by reading no policy at all.
  #430's cause was rules/S2's initial veto in `is_suffix_piece`, which keeps a numeral out of a suffix reading — right after a name, wrong inside a credential run — so the segment did not look like a run at all and fell to the given-and-suffix walk. Two wrong answers came out of that one cause, and a fix verified on `Smith, PSM I` alone would have left the other: where the run's first word is TITLES vocabulary too (`Smith, MD I`, and `Smith, Jr. I` through the period-abbreviation inference) the leading-title peel took it instead, giving title 'MD' plus given 'I'.

TWO readers since 2026-09-06: #436/#437 took the render join out of group, and `segment_suffix_reading`'s remaining readers are assign's gate and assign's router, one call site between them. The predicate is not moved — one call site is no reason to relocate what two sites will contest again — and what is worth carrying away is unchanged, since the reason the three had to agree is the reason the two still do.
  Declined, and the v1 suite caught it: asking `is_trailing_numeral_suffix` for #432's half. It answers the period question — `_ROMAN` is anchored and admits no period — but it also refuses a numeral standing behind an initial-shaped piece, which is a NO-COMMA rule and the opposite of this path's parity: `Chang, Andy C I` is first 'Andy', middle 'C', suffix 'I', and asking that predicate here made the numeral a middle. decisions.md's #401/#421 entry records the same fork declining to transfer to this walk under LENIENT. The half that applies is the period alone.
  BLAST RADIUS, over the 1069 corpus names against 56fb505c (PR #443): seven carry a one-character numeral behind a word inside a comma segment, every one bare, and none moves; the only movers are the three names this change added as examples, so the case table is the whole coverage. `Berg, abdul V` cannot reach the walk at all, the bound-given join making `abdul V` one piece, which is why the LENIENT mirror #401/#421 declined does not collide here.
  Two things the /simplify round settled by measurement rather than argument. The walk asks `is_suffix_piece(pieces[m - 1])` — a VOCABULARY test — where the reading carries the verdict it just recorded, and unifying them looks obviously right until you run it: the walk assigns `PSM` to GIVEN in `Smith, PSM I.`, so a reading-style flag says "behind a name" and sends the numeral to middle, losing the strict parity restored two commits earlier. The two sites ask different questions on purpose, and the vocabulary answer is the load-bearing one. The cost is a reading the walk cannot reach: `Smith, John PSM I I.` moves from middle 'I' + suffix 'PSM, I.' to middle 'I I.' + suffix 'PSM', two consecutive one-character numerals behind a credential — accepted, both readings being defensible for a shape nobody writes, and recorded here rather than chased at the price of two correct pins.
  The walk is also strict-blind, pre-existing and unchanged: it calls is_suffix_lenient unconditionally, so `Smith, John V` reads suffix 'V' under `lenient_comma_suffixes=False` as under the default. The reading consults the knob and the walk does not, which is now conspicuous where it was merely unexamined. Not this change's to fix.
