# C9 — a one-character suffix word inside a comma part

## Standing

- Inside a comma part a one-character suffix word is read by what stands before it: behind another suffix word it continues the run, with a period or without (`Smith, PSM I` → suffix 'PSM I'; `Smith, PSM I.` → suffix 'PSM I.'), and behind a name word an ASCII period makes it a middle initial while the bare letter, with no further comma after it, is the suffix (`Smith, John V.` → middle 'V.'; `Smith, John V` → suffix 'V', v1 parity) — 2026-08-25 #430.
- The class is one-character suffix vocabulary, not a roman numeral (`Smith, John 2.` → middle '2.'), and a run's first word that is title vocabulary too stays in the run (`Smith, RA I` → suffix 'RA I'; `Smith, Jr. I` → suffix 'Jr. I') — 2026-08-25 #430.
- The given part asks no initial in front of a numeral, the no-comma reading of one, so a numeral behind an initial stays the suffix (`Chang, Andy C I` → middle 'C', suffix 'I') — 2026-08-25 #430.
- A lone letter ending the given part ahead of a further comma is the middle initial whatever stands before it, a credential run in front stopping short of it (`Smith, John PhD I, Jr.` → middle 'I', suffix 'PhD, Jr.'), at the cost of splitting a certification level from its credential (`Smith, John PSM I, Jr.`) — 2026-10-25 #819.
- The given part's walk asks the numeral's predicate, which reads a one-letter numeral there by the vocabulary, so an unlisted letter stays the middle initial (`SMITH, JOHN X`), and a numeral of two or more letters as the generation (`Smith, John VI` → suffix 'VI') — 2026-10-25 #819.
- The given part's walk is strict-blind: `Smith, John V` reads suffix 'V' under `lenient_comma_suffixes=False` as under the default — 2026-08-25 #430; 2026-11-04 #877.
- The bare letter read as the generation behind a name word reports `suffix-or-name`, naming the middle initial, as `John Smith V` reports with no comma, and the reading stays: `Smith, John V` → suffix 'V', reported; a letter behind another suffix word, a period-marked one, one ahead of a further comma, one in lower case in a name of mixed case and one behind a particle (`Smith, Van V`) report nothing — 2026-11-04 (Derek), #877.

Elsewhere: the strict switch, which vetoes the numeral in the comma's decision and in a part that is nothing but suffix words — decisions/C14's 2026-10-19 (Derek), #761 entry; a credential starting a run to the end of its part, which reads `Smith, John PSM I I.` as suffix 'PSM I I.' where #430 had accepted middle 'I I.' — decisions/S8's 2026-10-04 (Derek), #602 entry; a bare letter behind a name word with a suffix comma after it stays a middle initial (`Smith, John V, Jr.` → middle 'V', suffix 'Jr.') — decisions/v1-xfail-triage's 2026-09-01 (Derek's triage) entry; an ideographic or fullwidth full stop is no abbreviation mark there (`Smith, John V。` → suffix 'V。') — decisions/cjk-full-stops' 2026-09-10 #322/#323 bundle.

## Log

- 2026-08-25 #430 / #432 (its accepted `Smith, John PSM I I.` reading superseded by #602, decisions/S8) — one boundary, fixed as one: inside a comma segment a ONE-CHARACTER suffix word — the only kind a reader could take for an initial — is read by WHAT STANDS BEFORE IT. Behind another suffix it describes that suffix and continues the run, period included — `Smith, PSM I` and `Smith, PSM I.` are suffix 'PSM I'/'PSM I.' where they read given 'PSM' plus suffix 'I', an initial in that slot being no shape anyone writes. Behind a name word the period is decisive, marking an abbreviation and so name material — `Smith, John V.` is middle 'V.' where it read suffix 'V.', which is what `Smith, John B.` always did; the only thing that made V differ is that V is also suffix vocabulary. The bare form is the boundary and does not move: `Smith, John V` stays suffix 'V' (v1 parity, #144), which is why the test is the PERIOD and not the numeral. SCOPE, stated as the code reads rather than as the intent was argued: the class is one-character SUFFIX VOCABULARY, not "a roman numeral". It sweeps in `2` (`Smith, John 2.` is middle '2.'), misses `X` and `IX` (roman, but not vocabulary — `Smith, PSM X` is unchanged), and never touched `II`/`III`/`IV` at all, which are two characters and so were never initial-shaped and never vetoed. Anyone adding a one-character suffix in a script that has initials extends it. And the strict knob is consulted: under `lenient_comma_suffixes=False` the initial veto stands and the run ends at the numeral, which the first draft silently overrode by reading no policy at all.
