# S1 — a bracketed credential is not a nickname

## Standing

- A clause wholly of words the vocabulary reads as no name is set apart from the name: it is no nickname and no maiden name, each word takes the role its vocabulary gives it (a title the title, a credential or generation the suffix, a word that is both the title in front of the name's first word and the suffix elsewhere), the words around it read as they would beside a nickname (a lone name word beside it the family name, N3), and a member of the ambiguous class is one of its words where its writing settles it after the name (S13): `John Smith (PhD, MBA)` and `John Smith (Ph.D., MBA)` → suffix, `John Smith (Dr)` → title, `John Smith (PhD) Withers` → family 'Withers', suffix 'PhD' — 2026-11-04 (Derek), #792.
- A credential or generation S3 reads joined by a slash or a hyphen is a word of such a clause, asked as the bare word is asked, a member of the ambiguous class first left to S13, and the initials Except asks each of its chunks, so it reads as its words written apart do: `John Smith (MD/PhD)` → suffix, `John (MD-PhD) Smith` and `Justin (JP/MD) Stearns` → nickname, as `(MD, PhD)` and `(JP, MD)` read there, `John Smith (MD/Bob)` and `(MA/MBA)` → nickname — 2026-11-05 (Derek), #1088; a joined word holding a member is a member itself since 2026-11-07 #1138 (`John Smith (MA/PhD)` → suffix, `John (MA/PhD) Smith` → nickname, reported).
- It keeps its pair's reading where the clause is nothing but title modifiers and styles (`(UK)`, `(Hon)`), a lone title of address (`(Sir)`, `(Mr)`), titles alone straight behind a credential (`FRCS (SN)`), or a credential written as initials before a name word of its comma part (`Justin (JP) Stearns`); content that is not such a clause and ends in an ASCII period keeps v1's escape, its brackets dropped — 2026-11-04 (Derek), #792.
- (superseded for a clause of non-name words by #792, 2026-11-04 below) The rule says what the escape does: quotes escape as brackets do, and a clause of two or more words is not suffix-shaped unless an ASCII period ends it, so `(PhD MSc)` keeps its nickname reading with nothing reported, as 1.4.0 read `(PhD MBA)`, which reports suffix-or-nickname since `mba` was marked ambiguous (decisions/suffix-acronym-collisions, 2026-11-03) — 2026-10-21 #807.
- A member of the ambiguous class its writing leaves unsettled is a word of such a clause where a credential of the same clause settles it, after the name only (S13's position): `John Smith (Ma, PhD)`, `(Ma PhD)` and `JOHN SMITH (PHD, MBA)` → suffix, where each was the nickname, reported. A credential that is no name settles one, or a member its periods settle; a generation, a numeral, an honorific, a title or a title-and-suffix word (`Edward Smith (Ed Jr)`, `(Ma, Ms)`, `(Ma, 씨)`) and a member its capitals settle (`(MA, Ed)`) settle nothing, nor do members alone (`(Ma, Ed)`); a joined word settles one where a chunk of it would (`(Ma, PhD/Jr)` → suffix, `(Ed, Jr/Sr)` → nickname) — 2026-11-07 (Derek), #1138.

Elsewhere: an ambiguous member alone in the brackets staying a nickname, reported, is decided under decisions/suffix-acronym-collisions (its Standing line ending 2026-09-25 #540; 2026-10-21) and decisions/ma-do's 2026-07-17 entry; a member after the name whose writing settles it reading as the suffix is decisions/S13 (2026-10-26 #810).
