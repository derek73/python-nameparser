# C10 — a suffix delimiter parts a suffix run

## Standing

- A delimiter the policy declares parts a trailing suffix part as a comma would: each side reads as the same text with a comma in the delimiter's place, the core dropped, so no join, maiden walk or link reaches across it (`Smith, John, PhD - and MD` → suffix 'PhD, and MD' under `extra_suffix_delimiters={' - '}`) — 2026-10-03 #549.
- Outside a trailing part, before the first comma or in the listing form's given part, the core stays a word (v1 parity), and a core inside a token keeps the token whole (`RN/CRNA` under `/`) — 2026-10-03 #549.
- The semicolon is a suffix delimiter every policy has, beside the comma, and a delimiter glued to one end of a word after the first comma leaves the word read as the word without it, the text kept (`John Smith, MD; PhD` → suffix 'MD; PhD'); a semicolon before the first comma stays a word — 2026-10-24 #752.
- A hyphen and a slash standing alone are suffix delimiters every policy has too, after the first comma (`John Smith, MD - PhD` → suffix 'MD, PhD'), but neither is read glued to a word or inside one unless the caller declares it, so `al-` and `MD/PhD` keep their readings and only the semicolon and a declared core glue — 2026-10-27 #889.

Elsewhere: a maiden marker in a part after a suffix comma is an ordinary word, core or no core, so under `extra_suffix_delimiters={' - '}` `Smith, John, PhD née Puig - i Soler` reads suffix 'PhD née Puig, i Soler' with no maiden name, where #549 gave maiden 'Puig', and the clause #549 accepted losing beside a core (`Smith, John, MD - née Jones Smith` → suffix 'MD, née Jones Smith') is lost without one too (`Smith, John, MD née Jones Smith` → suffix 'MD née Jones Smith' under any policy), so the loss rules/C10's Accepted bullet calls the delimiter's cost is M2's reading now — decisions/M2's 2026-10-04 (Derek), #601 entry; `Smith, RN - CRNA` reads suffix 'RN - CRNA', the core a word of the run a credential opens — decisions/C13's 2026-10-04 (Derek), #603 entry.

Open: none in this file.

- 2026-10-03 (Derek), #549 (its maiden 'Puig' in `Smith, John, PhD née Puig - i Soler` superseded by #601, decisions/M2) — A DECLARED DELIMITER PARTS A TRAILING SUFFIX PART AS A COMMA WOULD, AND THIS REVERSES #538'S SKIP READING (decisions/M2, 2026-09-26). The question #549 asked was whether a core a connective join swallows should be skipped (the text read as if the core were absent) or should end the run it stands in; Derek's ground for the second was the one a caller has when declaring one: a delimiter set to ` - ` is meant to behave like a comma around suffixes, and the simplest parser that honours that is one where it IS a comma there. So `group` cuts a tail segment at its cores before grouping anything, groups each part as the comma twin's own segment would be grouped, and drops the cores, which post_rules' entry pass still reads back as boundaries. No join, maiden walk or link search can reach across a core because none of them ever sees one, and the machinery that let them step over it is gone: the `cores` parameter of `_group_segment`, `_maiden_take`, `_run_neighbours` and `_link_joins_inside_the_clause`, `_maiden_take`'s `seen` index list, the `skip` arguments of `peel_walk` and `trailing_start`, and the #206 drop block that ran after the joins. One call per parse fewer (`tools/perf/call_count.py`, py3.11, 2026-10-03: parse 369 against 370 at 061f02da).
  WHAT MOVES, against 1.4.0 and 2.3.0, measured 2026-10-03 with the released wheels (PR #600). A connective beside a core no longer joins across it: `HumanName("Smith, John, PhD - and MD", suffix_delimiter=" - ").suffix` is 'PhD, and MD', 1.4.0's answer, where 2.0.0 through 2.3.0 gave 'PhD - and MD'. `Smith, John, Puig - i Soler` and `Smith, John, PhD née Puig - i Soler` read as 2.3.0 did again ('Puig, i Soler'; maiden 'Puig', suffix 'PhD, i Soler'). A MAIDEN MARKER BESIDE A CORE TAKES NO CLAUSE, the larger cost by count: `Smith, John, MD - née Jones Smith` reads suffix 'MD, née Jones Smith' and `Smith, John, PhD née - Jones` 'PhD née, Jones', where 2.0.0 through 2.3.0 read maiden 'Jones Smith' and 'Jones'; rules/C10 records it as Accepted. At the default policy nothing moves.
  THE DECLINED READING, TAKEN. #538 declined the boundary reading because it moves `Smith, John, PhD née Puig - i Soler` from maiden 'Puig i Soler' to maiden 'Puig' with suffix 'PhD, i Soler', "a birth-name link pushed into the credentials". That is what the same text with a comma typed in the core's place reads, and it is what 2.3.0 read. The marker beside a core (WHAT MOVES, above) is the same trade at a higher price, a maiden name lost rather than a link moved, and it is taken on the same ground; the review of this change found it, the first draft of this entry having weighed only the link shape. The comma reading is accepted as the cost of one rule for a declared separator, where the skip reading kept two (a lone core dropped like a comma, a core beside a connective stepped over like a word) and #549 was the seam between them.

It replaces #538's `test_a_delimiter_core_reads_as_if_it_were_not_written`, whose invariant this decision reverses. Scoped to TRAILING parts on purpose: before the first comma, or in the given part of the listing form, the core is a word (rules/C10's Accepted entry, v1 parity), and a comma typed there changes the name's structure rather than parting a suffix run, so the twin is a different parse. The ambiguity reports are not part of it: C2 reports `comma-structure` once per extra comma part, so a typed comma can add a report the core does not (`Smith, John, Puig - Puig` against `Smith, John, Puig, Puig`).

- 2026-10-24 #752 (D15 of the vocabulary phase 2 spec) — THE SEMICOLON IS A SUFFIX DELIMITER BY DEFAULT, AND A DELIMITER GLUED TO A WORD AFTER THE FIRST COMMA READS AS THE WORD WITHOUT IT. ClinicalTrials.gov's study officials (`ctgov-officials`, data as of 2026-10-21) write a semicolon between credentials where a comma would go: 494 of their names hold one, 480 after a comma, and of those 377 glue it to the word before (`MD; PhD`), 95 put it inside a word (`MD;PhD`), 4 stand it alone and 4 mix the forms; every release from 1.4.0 through 2.5.0 read the glued forms as the listing form, given 'MD;', family 'John Smith'. Derek asked on 2026-10-24 for both to be investigated as a 2.x change, the semicolon as a default delimiter and a glued delimiter that counts; the design below is this entry's, not yet reviewed.
  WHERE THE DEFAULT LIVES. A constant beside `_vocab.delimiter_cores`, which every reader of the delimiters already calls, not a default value of `Policy.extra_suffix_delimiters`: that field is a set a caller replaces (the facade's `suffix_delimiter` replaces it too), so a default inside it would be lost to every caller who declares a delimiter of their own, and its docstring promised additions only. No policy removes the semicolon, as none removes the comma: Derek decided on 2026-10-24 that it gets no opt-out switch, built in like the comma.
  THE GLUED WORD, AND WHY EVERY READER ASKS ITS STEM. One predicate, `_vocab.glued_stem`, answers what a core glued to one end of a word leaves, and four sites ask it: classify tags a word after the first comma as its stem, `_comma.decide` reads the stem in its shape tests (the caps shape and paired initials), `is_wholly_suffix` reads it in the run test, and segment's C2 flag reads a glued title. The first draft read the stem in the run test alone, as the D15 spike had: over the officials it put 365 reversed names right. Tagging the stem put 412 right, the rest being words other rules read by their tags, a listed credential opening the part (C13), a word of the ambiguous class (C4) and a glued title; reading the stem in the comma decision's shape tests added the unlisted all-caps credentials C6 admits (`MBBS;`), 431 in all. Behind a head that is not a whole name the run test asks the strict token test, as C8 reads `Smith, V MD`, so `Smith, V; MD` keeps given 'V;'.
  JOINING WORDS JOIN NOTHING ACROSS IT. A stem's connective and bound-given tags are dropped: tagged in full, `Smith, Juan y; Maria` joined 'y; Maria' to 'Juan' as one given name, a connective reaching across the delimiter C10 says none reaches across. A particle's are dropped too, its backward attachment with them, Derek decided on 2026-10-24: the design-docs review found `Berg, Jan van; MD` reading middle 'van;' where both twins read family 'van Berg' (P6 attaching the particle backward, away from the delimiter), the tags were kept, and the second review then found the kept tags joining forward across the delimiter (`Berg, de; Smith` → family 'Berg de; Smith', P1's fold, and P2's chain inside the given part). Blocking the forward joins alone would guard every site that reads the particle tag, about a dozen against P6's two, for shapes no local corpus holds (no glued particle after a comma in the officials, FEC, TSE, PSC, UKEC or Nepal corpora); so a glued particle joins nothing, middle 'van;' as every release from 1.4.0 read it, and rules/C10's Accepted bullet records the cost.
  A WORD DELIMITER IS NOT GLUED. Only a core written without a letter or digit (`_vocab.glues`) is read glued: the review found ` and ` under a declared policy cutting `Stand` into `St` and reading it as a title (`John Smith, Stand` → title 'Stand'), since the core is found inside words by a substring test; a word core parts only standing alone, as before.
  ONE ANSWER WHATEVER THE SET ORDER. `glued_stem` tries every gluable core, in sorted order, and takes one standing once at an end of the word: the second review found it stopping at the first core the frozenset yielded, so under `extra_suffix_delimiters={'/'}` `Jane Smith, MD/PhD; MBA` read suffix 'MD/PhD; MBA' on 5 of 8 hash seeds and given 'MD/PhD;' on 3, the '/' inside the word hiding the ';' at its end.
  BEHIND ONE NAME WORD a semicolon standing alone stays a word of the run the credential opens (`Smith, MD ; PhD` → suffix 'MD ; PhD'), as a declared core does there: the parity reason rules/C10 gives for a declared core does not reach the semicolon, which no release read as a delimiter, and the reading is kept so that every delimiter follows one rule, not on its own merits; the officials hold no such name (the 4 standing alone all stand behind a whole name).

- 2026-10-27 #889 — A HYPHEN AND A SLASH STANDING ALONE AFTER THE FIRST COMMA ARE BUILT-IN DELIMITERS, AND THEY DO NOT GLUE. #889 asked whether the default had learned enough that `suffix_delimiter` and `extra_suffix_delimiters` could go. Measured on 8a65ee18 over ClinicalTrials.gov's study officials (`ctgov-officials`, data as of 2026-10-21), after the first comma: ` & ` stands in 184 names, ` and ` in 140, ` - ` in 54, ` / ` in 38, ` + ` and ` : ` in one each, ` | ` in none. Declaring ` - ` or ` / ` put a reversed name right where a title stood before the separator (`Michael Thomas, Prof. / MD` read given '/', family 'Michael Thomas'), and otherwise split the suffix ('MD - PhD' → 'MD, PhD'). Declaring ` & ` moved 6 names' fields and broke a title phrase (`Gavin Solomon, President & CEO` → title 'President CEO'), and ` and ` moved 34, nearly all job descriptions (`Professor of obs and gyn`); so Derek chose on 2026-10-27 the hyphen and the slash only, and kept `extra_suffix_delimiters` for the separators a caller still declares (the en and em dashes, ` | `, ` + `, ` : `), which the officials write too rarely for a default.
  WHY THEY DO NOT GLUE. The first build added both cores to the built-in set whole, as the semicolon is, and broke rules/P6's `al- Rashid`: a hyphen glued to the end of a word is the Arabic article's own spelling (#700), and a slash or hyphen inside one is a joined credential (`MD/PhD`, #887). So the set splits in two: `_vocab.BUILTIN_SUFFIX_DELIMITERS` holds the cores that part standing alone (`;`, `-`, `/`), and `_vocab.glued_cores` the ones read glued to a word or inside one (the semicolon and every declared core). A caller who declares ` - ` still gets the glued reading the semicolon has, as before; a declared ` - ` reads `John Smith, V - al-` with family 'John Smith', the glued hyphen taken for a delimiter, where the built-in keeps 'al- John Smith', and the sweep below found no other difference between the two where the core stands alone. Accepted, and recorded in rules/C10: `John Smith, MD- PhD` keeps given 'MD-', family 'John Smith', as every release from 1.4.0 read it.
  WHERE IT REACHES. A sweep of 69,804 texts on 8a65ee18, under each of the three orders 23,268: every ordered pair of the twenty-three words `MD PhD M.D. RN CRNA Jr. III V Dr. Prof. Smith Jones Mary van de al- y and née FACS Ed Ma Chief`, joined by each of the seven separators ` - `, ` / `, `- `, ` -`, `/ `, ` /`, ` – ` (standing alone, glued on either side, the en dash) after each of the six heads `John Smith, `, `Smith, John, `, `Smith, `, `, `, `John Smith ` and `Smith, John ` (22,218), and every ordered pair of `Smith Jones Mary MD van` joined by each separator in the six frames `John A-B`, `A-B, John`, `John A-B, MD`, `A-B, John, MD`, `Mary A-B & John Smith` and `John & Mary A-B` (1,050); moved 4,686, every one with the hyphen or slash standing alone, behind a whole name or in a part after a second comma. None moved before the first comma, behind one name word or none, glued, or under the en dash, and no `&`-joined name moved. Of the 4,686, 4,650 read every role but the suffix as the same text with a comma in the core's place does; the 36 that do not are a title beside the connective `and` (`John Smith, Dr. - and` → title 'Dr. and'), where a typed comma makes a different structure. The design-docs review's wider grid (twenty-three words of its own, pairs after six heads, both cores, three orders: 19,044 texts, 8,952 moved) found the same class with `of` and on either side of the core (`Prof. - of Surgery`, `and - Dr.`, `Dr. / and MD`), 192 texts, every one a connective beside a title; a declared ` - ` read them so at 8a65ee18 too. Kept as rules/C10's last Accepted bullet rather than fixed, Derek decided on 2026-10-27: the title run reads connectives wherever it runs, and the officials hold no such name.
