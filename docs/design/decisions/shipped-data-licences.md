# shipped-data-licences — the licences of the data the package ships

## Standing

- `pyproject.toml`'s `license` is an SPDX expression naming the package's own licence AND the licence of each list the wheel ships copied whole from a third party, `LGPL-2.1-or-later` alone since 2026-11-13, when the last such list left (THE NICKNAMES LIST LEAVES); such a list whose licence asks its text or notice to travel with copies has a `LICENSE-<source>` file at the root, named in `license-files` and saying which files hold the data. A list the project measured on a dataset names no licence in the expression and is credited in the module that holds it. README.rst's License section lists both kinds. `tests/v2/test_licensing.py` holds each licence file to the expression, `license-files` and the README, and each credited source's licence and module to the README's License section and the licence to the module — 2026-11-13 #1382 MEASURED DATA LEAVES THE EXPRESSION, 2026-11-13 THE NICKNAMES LIST LEAVES.
- A dataset under CC0, a corpus the package measured counts on without copying its words, a list the project measured on a dataset, its words included, and a source that states no terms for reuse add nothing to the expression; the last kind is listed in this file's Log as left out, by name, so the next source of its kind is weighed against it — 2026-11-12 #1370 THE LICENCE EXPRESSION, 2026-11-13 #1382 MEASURED DATA LEAVES THE EXPRESSION.
- Data the project derives by measuring (counts, rankings, cutoffs and filters of its own over its sources) is the project's own, its sources credited as a courtesy; a curated list copied as a whole, one compiler's judgements of which entries belong, keeps its licence, and a list the core will not carry under that licence ships as an optional extra package, as the Japanese segmenter does, or not at all — 2026-11-12 #1370 FACTS WE MEASURE.
- The familiar forms copied from nam_dict are dropped before 3.1 (done, 2026-11-12 #1370 NAM_DICT'S FAMILIAR PAIRS LEAVE THE PACKAGE), and rebuilt from pairs the project finds in CC0 data and reviews (done, decisions/name-matching's 2026-11-12 ALTERNATE SPELLINGS ARE OPT-IN) — 2026-11-12 #1370 FACTS WE MEASURE.
- No shipped data derives from Old Dominion University's nicknames list since 2026-11-13, nor does the repository hold its pairs: its familiar readings, its refusals file and the tool's path that read it are gone, with `LICENSE-nicknames` and the `Apache-2.0` term, and the English familiar forms are pairs ORCID's holders and US public records evidence, reviewed (decisions/name-matching, 2026-11-13 THE FAMILIAR FORMS FROM DATA ALONE) — 2026-11-13 THE NICKNAMES LIST LEAVES.
- No shipped data derives from nam_dict since 2026-11-12, so the GNU FDL and `LICENSE-nam_dict` left the package before any release named them: its familiar pairs were dropped (#1370 NAM_DICT'S FAMILIAR PAIRS LEAVE THE PACKAGE) and the suggester's common given names are ranked from ORCID (CC0) — 2026-11-12 GIVEN NAMES FROM ORCID.
- No shipped data derives from Unihan since 2026-11-12: `locales/zh.py`'s simplified forms come from the simplified-Chinese labels of Wikidata's family-name items and `_order_names.py`'s pinyin surnames from those items' Hanyu Pinyin statements, both CC0, so `Unicode-3.0` and `LICENSE-unicode` left the package; 2.5.0 through 3.0.1 shipped Unihan-derived forms under the expression naming the LGPL alone — 2026-11-12 UNIHAN REPLACED.

- **2026-11-12 #1370 FACTS WE MEASURE — DATA THE PROJECT DERIVES BY MEASURING IS ITS OWN; A CURATED LIST COPIED WHOLE KEEPS ITS LICENCE.** (Its keeping of the ODU list superseded by 2026-11-13 THE NICKNAMES LIST LEAVES below.) (Its Open question on the expression settled by 2026-11-13 #1382 MEASURED DATA LEAVES THE EXPRESSION below.) Decided in chat on #1370. A licence binds only what copyright or a database right protects, and a fact is neither: that 张 is a common surname, how many people bear it, that `Bill` is used for `William`. Copyright reaches a compilation of facts through its selection or arrangement alone (*Feist v. Rural*, 1991; in the EU, a database that is its author's own intellectual creation, *Football Dataco v. Yahoo!*, 2012). The sources split on that line:
  - Ours, the sources credited as a courtesy: `locales.ZH`'s surnames, a selection the project made (the census top 100 and the union of two rankings' top 300 at a cutoff measured in decisions/W1's #695 entry, five hand additions from a cross-check, #952); the suggester's tables and thresholds, counted on the local corpora; the INE given-name lists, a filter of the project's own (a given-name share) over a census's counts; and a character's simplified form or pinyin, a fact about the character.
  - Copied whole, keeping its licence: nam_dict's familiar-form pairs and the ODU nicknames list, each a compiler's judgement of which pairs are familiar forms, taken as a list rather than learned from data. The ODU list stays, its Apache-2.0 asking only that `LICENSE-nicknames` ship. nam_dict's pairs are dropped before 3.1, so no release ships them, and the familiar forms are rebuilt from pairs found in CC0 data (ORCID's credit names against its given-name box, GND's variant names) and reviewed by hand, as the ODU list was: candidates generated from that data alone, never by checking nam_dict's list against it, which would leave the result derived from it. Measured by the session that weighed the options: of nam_dict's 563 pairs the ODU list lacks, ORCID and GND together evidence 316, and dropping them leaves `familiar-form` matching 115 pairs fewer (`Sepp` / `Josef`, `Masha` / `Maria`, `Józsi` / `József`) and effectively English-only until the rebuild.
  - Declined: keeping nam_dict's pairs in the core under the GFDL. Had they been kept, they would have shipped as an optional extra package, as the Japanese segmenter is an extra, so that the core's licence review never meets the GFDL.
  - Open: whether `CC-BY-4.0`, `OGDL-Taiwan-1.0` and `Unicode-3.0` stay in the licence expression for data this rule makes the project's own. They are kept for now: the credits they ask for cost nothing, and a reader checking a module's provenance against the expression finds each source named.
