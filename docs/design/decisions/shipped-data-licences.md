## Standing

  So 2.5.0 onward shipped OGDL-Taiwan-1.0 data and Unihan-derived data, and 3.0.0 onward CC BY 4.0 data, behind an expression naming the LGPL alone. The Unihan data is the one whose terms asked a notice to travel: 19 of the 324 forms in zh.py's generated block are there only through Unihan's variant table (颜阙关练篮诸厐买云谌风迟东兰娄盖芈区盘, measured by the review of this change by running `tools/zh_surnames.py`'s `select()` with and without the table), all 19 in v2.5.0's block too, and zh.py's docstring did not name Unihan. The 3.1 cycle's nam_dict data had no GFDL text in the tree either. The expression is now `LGPL-2.1-or-later AND Apache-2.0 AND GFDL-1.2-no-invariants-or-later AND CC-BY-4.0 AND OGDL-Taiwan-1.0 AND Unicode-3.0`, which `packaging.licenses.canonicalize_license_expression` returns unchanged; the wheel and sdist built from this tree carry all four licence files and the expression as `License-Expression` (Metadata-Version 2.4).
  - Left out, CC0 or counts only: Wikidata's surname ranking in `zh.py` and ORCID's public data file filtering `_order_names.py`'s given names (both CC0), and the corpora the locale packs' counts were measured on (nepal-candidates, the Khural, JRC-Names), whose words the package does not copy. `CITATION.cff` keeps `license: LGPL-2.1-or-later`, the licence of the software being cited.
  - Left out, no terms stated: `zh.py`'s top 100 of the 2020 PRC census and `config/_ko_surnames.py`'s Korean census surnames, short lists of a country's commonest surnames in rank order, whose modules name no licence and whose sources state none the entry could find. The same holds for `SUFFIX_ACRONYMS`, which root AGENTS.md records arriving in `af5bdab` as a bulk import from Wikipedia, whose text is CC BY-SA; its entries have been reviewed and pruned word by word since (decisions/suffix-acronym-collisions), and whether a list of post-nominal abbreviations carries the terms of the page it was drawn from is not settled here.
  - Weighed, not taken in this change: relicensing the package as LGPL-3.0. The Apache-2.0 data needs a licence compatible with it, which LGPL-3.0 is and LGPL-2.1 alone is not, and the "or later" clause of the package's licence already lets a recipient take the 3.0 terms; Apache's §4(a) asks its text to travel whatever licence the combined work carries, so `LICENSE-nicknames` would stay; and an LGPL-3.0-only package could not be combined with GPL-2.0-only code, which LGPL-2.1-or-later can.
  - Debian: `debian/copyright` for python-nameparser will need a paragraph per source, the OGDL and Unihan data having been in every release it packaged from 2.5.0. Debian takes a GFDL work as free only without Invariant Sections or Cover Texts, which nam_dict's notice declares.
- **2026-11-12 #1370 FACTS WE MEASURE — DATA THE PROJECT DERIVES BY MEASURING IS ITS OWN; A CURATED LIST COPIED WHOLE KEEPS ITS LICENCE.** Decided in chat on #1370. A licence binds only what copyright or a database right protects, and a fact is neither: that 张 is a common surname, how many people bear it, that `Bill` is used for `William`. Copyright reaches a compilation of facts through its selection or arrangement alone (*Feist v. Rural*, 1991; in the EU, a database that is its author's own intellectual creation, *Football Dataco v. Yahoo!*, 2012). The sources split on that line:
  - Ours, the sources credited as a courtesy: `locales.ZH`'s surnames, a selection the project made (the census top 100 and the union of two rankings' top 300 at a cutoff measured in decisions/W1's #695 entry, five hand additions from a cross-check, #952); the suggester's tables and thresholds, counted on the local corpora; the INE given-name lists, a filter of the project's own (a given-name share) over a census's counts; and a character's simplified form or pinyin, a fact about the character.
  - Copied whole, keeping its licence: nam_dict's familiar-form pairs and the ODU nicknames list, each a compiler's judgement of which pairs are familiar forms, taken as a list rather than learned from data. The ODU list stays, its Apache-2.0 asking only that `LICENSE-nicknames` ship. nam_dict's pairs are dropped before 3.1, so no release ships them, and the familiar forms are rebuilt from pairs found in CC0 data (ORCID's credit names against its given-name box, GND's variant names) and reviewed by hand, as the ODU list was: candidates generated from that data alone, never by checking nam_dict's list against it, which would leave the result derived from it. Measured by the session that weighed the options: of nam_dict's 563 pairs the ODU list lacks, ORCID and GND together evidence 316, and dropping them leaves `familiar-form` matching 115 pairs fewer (`Sepp` / `Josef`, `Masha` / `Maria`, `Józsi` / `József`) and effectively English-only until the rebuild.
  - Declined: keeping nam_dict's pairs in the core under the GFDL. Had they been kept, they would have shipped as an optional extra package, as the Japanese segmenter is an extra, so that the core's licence review never meets the GFDL.
  - Open: whether `CC-BY-4.0`, `OGDL-Taiwan-1.0` and `Unicode-3.0` stay in the licence expression for data this rule makes the project's own. They are kept for now: the credits they ask for cost nothing, and a reader checking a module's provenance against the expression finds each source named.
