# H11 — a one-letter title against the initial it spells

## Standing

- A one-letter word written with its period is an initial and not a title where its letter has a capital, whatever title the letter spells, and so is such a letter cut from a word at its full stops (`U. S. Grant`, `Grant, U.S.` and `U.Thein Sein` under `locales.MY` read as the default reads them); written bare it is the title (`U Thein Sein` → title 'U' under the pack); a letter of a script without capitals keeps its title written with its period (`ড. রহিম উদ্দিন` → title 'ড.') — 2026-11-19 #1231.
- The veto is a shape, stated once for every one-letter title a lexicon holds, a caller's included, as rules/P7 states it for the particles; no per-word property (the reserved `bare_only`) is built for it — 2026-11-19 #1231.

Open: none in this file.

## Log

- 2026-11-19 #1231 — A ONE-LETTER TITLE WRITTEN WITH ITS PERIOD IS THE INITIAL IT SPELLS, WHERE THE LETTER HAS A CAPITAL. `locales.MY` turns on `u`, the Burmese honorific (my.toml, `default = false`), the first one-letter title in a cased script any lexicon the project ships has held: the default's are the Bengali `ড` and the Thai `ศ` (`sorted(w for w in L.titles | L.titles_ambiguous if len(w) == 1)` with `L = Parser().lexicon._sets`, 2026-11-19). The title match folds edge periods away, so the reading alone also claimed `U.`: the issue's simulated lexicon read `U. S. Grant` title 'U.', given 'S.', and `Grant, U.S.` title 'U.S.' through the period-joined derivation, which reads a word whose chunk is a title as a title. The issue asked whether `U` should need the absence of a period, and the working answer (decision 3 of the 2026-11-19 #1231 entry in decisions/vocabulary-collisions) was yes; the period is a fact about initials, the same line rules/P7 draws for a one-letter particle (`Juan Ó. Pérez`), so it is stated as a shape, not as a property of `u`, and reaches a caller's one-letter title too.
  - **Where it reads.** Two sites, the two that turn the vocabulary into a reading of a word written with periods: classify's title tag, asking `_lexicon._spells_a_cased_initial` after its membership hit (`_spells_an_initial`, P7's helper, narrowed to a letter that upper-cases to something else), and the period-joined derivation's chunk test, `_vocab._title_chunk`, which both `period_joined_vocab` and `period_joined_title` ask. The other readers of the title set (the comma's unit count, the glued-stop test, the title phrase's head run, the suggester) were not changed. Measured: over 23 shapes holding a one-letter word (every comma shape, behind and before a title, in one case, with `Jr.` and `PhD` after it) × 6 spellings (`U.`, `u.`, `U.S.`, `U.S`, `Ü.`, `U.Thein`) × the three orders, `parser_for(locales.MY)` read every name as the default does except where the fragment's `la` moved one (`Vega, U. de la`), 2026-11-19 on this branch (a scratch probe comparing `as_dict()` and the ambiguity kinds).
  - **The capital.** The Bengali `ড` is written `ড.` as the doctor (bn.toml's note), the full stop being the title's writing, so a letter of a script without case keeps its title reading. The default moves nothing: it holds no one-letter title in a cased script, and the committed snapshot is byte-identical across the change.
  - **Accepted:** under the pack a name opening with a bare `U` reads it as the title, a Korean surname `U` written bare included (`U Min Ho` → title 'U'; Wikidata holds 122 South Koreans with the family name U, tools/name_usage.py, 2026-11-19), and so does a bare `U` written as an initial without its period, which FEC writes 187 times beside 163 `U.`, mostly a middle initial after a comma, where no title reads.
  - **Left out:** #1205's `H.` (Haji) under an Indonesian choice asks the opposite, the dotted letter as the title; this entry makes that a title only where a reading or a rule says so for that letter, which #1205's decision would add.

Declined:

- A per-reading property read only bare (`bare_only`, reserved, unbuilt, for 3.x in tools/vocab/build.py) — the period convention is a fact about initials, not about `u`, as decisions/P7 declined a flag for `ó`; a property would be one more axis for every guard roster to sweep, for a question no entry answers differently today. #1205 may be the first entry that does.
