# R8 — the generation, credentials and honorifics views

## Standing

- `ParsedName.generation` and `ParsedName.credentials` partition `suffix` by the reading each word took, a generation or an agnomen in the first and every other word in the second (the honorifics leaving the second for a view of their own since 2026-11-03, the bullet below); they are views, not roles, so `suffix`, `as_dict()`, `replace()`'s fields and the repr are unchanged, item 4 of decisions/vocabulary-model's #754 output-model entry — 2026-10-29 #754, sub-phase 4b.
- The honorifics, a listed suffix word whose kind is `honorific` or `professional_honorific` (`さん`, `씨`, `先生`), leave `credentials` for a third view, `ParsedName.honorifics`, and read as `ReadingKind.HONORIFIC`; a courtesy (`Esq.`) and a status (`ret.`) stay in `credentials`, read as `post-nominal`, and the offices CLDR files as credentials (`議員`, `国会議員`, `太平紳士`) are credentials by kind — 2026-11-03 #326 (Derek).
- A listed suffix word's kind is the lexicon's, compiled with its sets (`Lexicon._sets.generation_words`, `credential_words`, and since 2026-11-03 `honorific_words`), which on the default is the shipped vocabulary's — 2026-11-02 #754, sub-phase 4i-b, superseding 4b's read from the reading index.

Elsewhere: the field's composition and the 2026-08-16 decision to keep it one field, which still stands, decisions/suffix-field-composition.

Elsewhere: the gated credential words `kc`, `pc` and `dl`, a credential written as a word beside a name reading, read as credentials, decisions/S11's 2026-10-30 #965 entry.

Open: none in this file.

  2. **The kind is read whatever the reading's default** (SUPERSEDED by the 2026-11-02 #754, sub-phase 4i-b entry below: a caller's `add("st", kind="suffix")` reads `St` as a post-nominal). `st`, a Czech generation written `default = false`, reads `generation` when a caller's `Lexicon` lists it, while its `languages` name no file, since none holds it on. A pack turning such a reading on (phase 3's `cs`, 3f) then needs nothing more for the kind; a word only a caller's own `Lexicon` lists has no row and reads `post-nominal`.
  3. **Each word is placed by its own reading, and separated as the writer separated it.** A `joined` mark is not only the second word of a split credential (`Ph.` `D.`, which takes the first word's reading) but every word of a suffix run written without commas (`Jr. III PhD`), so a view places each word by its reading. Two words of one view are separated by a comma where the suffix holds one anywhere between them, R1's separator (a typed comma, or a semicolon, hyphen, slash or delimiter C10 drops: `MD / Jr. / PhD` gives credentials 'MD, PhD'), and by a space where not, so `MD Jr. PhD` gives credentials 'MD PhD'; the first draft started a new comma-joined entry wherever a word of the other view stood between, writing a comma nobody typed ('MD, PhD'), found by the design-docs review. A connective goes with the word before it (`PhD and MD`, `Jr. and III`), so one joining a generation to a credential dangles at the end of the generation (`Jr. and MD` gives generation 'Jr. and'), accepted rather than given a rule of its own, no corpus name holding the shape.
  4. **A suffix word with no reading is a credential.** `replace()` and `Parser.revise()` write tokens with no `Token.reading` (4a), and a `ParsedName` unpickled from 2.x has none either, so `replace(suffix="Jr.")` reads credentials 'Jr.' and the 2.8 pickle of `Dr. Juan Q. de la Vega III` credentials 'III': nothing records what the word was read as, and the catch-all is the view that claims least. A name word a run took into the suffix by position is a credential too, 4a's reading of it (`Smith, Jr., John` gives credentials 'John').
  5. **The views stay out of `as_dict()`, `--json` and the repr**, as `family_base` and the other derived views do: `as_dict()` is the seven fields `replace()` takes back. They are `render()` keys and assertable fields of a rule's example lines.

- 2026-11-03 #326 (Derek) — THE HONORIFICS HAVE A VIEW OF THEIR OWN. #326's last open question was whether the honorifics after a name belong in `credentials`, CLDR's catch-all, CLDR having no third field. Three options were weighed: keep them in `credentials`; give them a third view and narrow `credentials`; or move them out of `suffix` into `title`, rules/W3's "no part of the name" argument.
