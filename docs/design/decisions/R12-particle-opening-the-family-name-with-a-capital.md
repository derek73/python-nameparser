# R12 — a particle opening the family name with a capital

## Standing

- A family word whose particle reading is written `capital_opening` (`ter` in nl.toml, `der` in de.toml and nl.toml, compiled into the private `CAPITAL_OPENING_PARTICLES`) is written with a capital where the word before it in the family name, in the order the family view joins its words, is no particle by its tag or by its last hyphen part, a last middle word ending in a particle part counting where it opens the family name (`LEVON TER PETROSYAN` → 'Levon Ter Petrosyan', `ARMEN DER KIUREGHIAN` → 'Armen Der Kiureghian', `VAN TER HORST` → 'Van Ter Horst'), and keeps its lowercase after one (`JAN VAN DER BERG` → 'Jan van der Berg', `DER BERG, JAN VAN` → 'Jan van der Berg', `ANKE MAITLAND-VAN DER ZEE` → 'Anke Maitland-van der Zee'); a middle `ter` keeps its lowercase (`JAN TER HORST VAN DER BERG` → 'Jan ter Horst van der Berg'); the costs are a Dutch surname opened by `ter` (`GERARD TER BORCH` → 'Gerard Ter Borch'), a German `der` behind an unlisted word (`KAREN AUS DER FÜNTEN` → 'Karen Aus Der Fünten') and a German epithet (`JOHANN DER JÜNGERE` → 'Johann Der Jüngere') — 2026-11-09 #1224, DECIDED.

Elsewhere: a particle opening a hyphenated word and the question this rule answers, decisions/R4's 2026-11-08 #1224 entry; how a particle is written otherwise, decisions/R4.

So the opening `der` is capitalized by its writers and the opening `ter` is split, and only the particle before the word separates the Dutch and German `der` written lowercase (`van der`, `von der`), which keeps its lowercase. The property is a reading's (`capital_opening`), not a kind's: `ter` is a `preposition` and `der` an `article`, the kind `de` and `la` are too. The word before is read in the family name as the family view joins its words, so a particle folded in after a comma counts (`DER BERG, JAN VAN` → 'Jan van der Berg') and a given name or a nickname that spells a particle does not (`VAN TER HORST` → 'Van Ter Horst', `JAN "VAN" TER HORST` → 'Jan "Van" Ter Horst'); it counts as a particle by its tag or by its last hyphen part being particle vocabulary, and where the word opens the family name the last middle word counts by that hyphen part alone, the case of `Anke Maitland-van der Zee`, whose `Maitland-van` the parse reads as a middle name. A middle `ter` keeps its lowercase (`JAN TER HORST VAN DER BERG` → 'Jan ter Horst van der Berg'). A family spliced in as raw text is read by the vocabulary, the word and the word before it alike, so `replace(family="TER PETROSYAN")` repairs as `revise()` and a fresh parse do ('Ter'). The first build read the word before in the written order and in any role; review found the comma shapes and the other roles, and that only `ANNA AZ- DER BERG` told the tag test from the hyphen test.
