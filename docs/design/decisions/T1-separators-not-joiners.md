# T1 — separators, not joiners

## Standing

- An ignorable character is a separator, never removed with its neighbors joined as v1's squash_emoji and squash_bidi did: `A😀B` reads given 'A', family 'B' — 2026-07 PR #288.
- The `strip_bidi=False` half of the opt-out carries no example, by decision; with it `John‏Smith` (a right-to-left mark between the words) reads given 'John‏Smith', the mark kept — 2026-10-20 #761.
- The zero-width space (U+200B) and the byte-order mark (U+FEFF) are separators with no opt-out; the zero-width non-joiner and joiner (U+200C, U+200D), the word joiner (U+2060) and the soft hyphen (U+00AD) stay inside the word, and an invisible character the tokenizer does not handle is reported nowhere — 2026-10-24 #851.

Open: none in this file.

## Log

- 2026-07 (v2 core, PR #288) — v1's squash_emoji/squash_bidi REMOVED the character and joined its neighbors. (v1.3.0 had no bidi handling at all: squash_bidi entered late v1 via #266, 2026-07-07, on the emoji precedent's shape.) ('A😀B' → 'AB'); v2 makes an ignorable character a separator ('A😀B' → 'A', 'B'). The unavoidable consequence of every part being an exact positioned piece of the input: with no rewriting stage, nothing can splice two half-words together.
- 2026-10-20 (Derek), #761 — the `strip_bidi=False` half of the opt-out carries no example, by decision: the example would be a name holding an invisible direction mark (`John‏Smith`, which reads given `John‏Smith` with the marks kept), and that is not worth a new policy annotation in `tests/v2/rules_doc.py`'s registry.
- 2026-10-24 (Derek), #851 — the zero-width space and the byte-order mark join T1's set, unconditionally, like whitespace and the katakana middle dot (rules/T2): neither strip switch fits, since neither is an emoji or a direction control, and no caller would keep either. Measured over every committed corpus and the eight local corpora on 2026-10-24: U+200B stands only in `tw-donors`, at the start of 520 of its 308,667 names, each of which read `given` where its twin without the character reads `family` (`​王小明`), so splitting there moves those 520 and nothing else. U+FEFF stands in 12 `jrc-names` spellings, each beside the space between two words (`Jerome ﻿Kerviel`), web text rather than a stray byte-order mark at a field's start; separating there changes no role and takes the character out of the field. Beside the interpunct the two read as whitespace does, not as a stripped direction mark (`威廉·​莎士比亚` reads as `威廉· 莎士比亚`): no corpus holds the combination, and the simpler rule won.
  - Excluded: the zero-width non-joiner and joiner, which decide how the letters beside them join and stand inside Persian (Arabic-script), Devanagari, Malayalam and Kannada names in the corpora (8,740 joiners between letters in `nepal-candidates` alone); about 575 of them stand at a word's edge, where they join nothing (`Antonio ‌Negri`), and are left to the caller. The word joiner, whose meaning is no break. The soft hyphen, which sits inside a word: of the 66 names holding one, 50 are an accented í decoded with the wrong encoding (`NATALÃ­CIO`) and 16 are typesetting hyphens (`Puig­demont`), and neither is the parser's to repair.
  - Declined: an ambiguity reporting an invisible character the tokenizer leaves in a word. An ambiguity records a reading the parse had to choose; this is a fact about the data, which the caller can find as easily as the parser can. Declined too: a cleanup recipe in the user docs — cleaning input is the caller's job, and the docs would own it.
