# P13 — a connective ending the name words

## Standing

- A single-letter connective in front of a lone letter ending the name words, in the part holding the family name, joins it to the word before, the letter being the second surname written as an initial, numeral or initial alike, the generational `i` included: `John Smith y V` → family 'Smith y V', `Juan García y L.` → family 'García y L.', `Puig i V, Joan` → family 'Puig i V' — 2026-11-05 #1097, #1098.
- A connective written as a word, not also suffix vocabulary, with nothing but suffixes or a trailing title H5 reads behind it, joins the word before it alone: `John Smith y III` → family 'Smith y', suffix 'III'; `John Smith and Jr.` → family 'Smith and', suffix 'Jr.'; `John Paul Smith y Prof.` → family 'Smith y', title 'Prof.' — 2026-11-05 #1097, #1098.
- Both joins are made only where the part is the family's: the part before a family comma under every order, and the end of the name under a given-first order alone (`John Smith y V` under FAMILY_FIRST keeps given 'Smith', middle 'y', family 'John', suffix 'V'); where the part is the whole name a piece must stand in front of the join past the title run (`Smith y V`, `Dr. Smith y V` keep the carve-out) — 2026-11-05 #1097, #1098.
- A name written in one case links a lone letter as its mixed-case twin does (`JOHN SMITH Y V` → family 'SMITH Y V'), but a cased single-letter connective with nothing after it keeps its reading there, being an initial as often (`CHENG GUI Y` → family 'Y'; family-first, middle 'Y') — 2026-11-05 #1097, #1098.
