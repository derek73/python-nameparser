# O9 — words in capitals as the family

## Standing

- The default reading of a name with words in capitals does not change: `DUPONT Mary` keeps given `DUPONT` — 2026-11-11 #659.
- Reading the capitals as the family is an opt-in `Policy` switch, `capitals_as_family`, off by default; no locale pack sets it, and the group suggester may — 2026-11-11 #659.
- The run of capitals words in a mixed-case name is the family wherever it stands, outranking the credential and title readings, the other words keeping their written order — 2026-11-11 #659.
- A capitals word holds two or more letters in capitals and no full stop, slash or digit; runs join across particles and conjunctions, a lowercase particle right before the run joins it, a run of particles alone or a lone roman numeral is none, the last of two runs is the family, a name whose lowercase letters stand only in titles, particles and suffixes is not read, and only the part before the first comma is read — 2026-11-12 #659.
- A listed credential or numeral ending the run peels off where a capitals word that is no particle is left — 2026-11-12 #659.
- The switch cannot be set with `unlisted_caps_suffixes=CapsSuffixes.EVERYWHERE`: construction raises — 2026-11-11 #659.
- Reports asking which field a word the rule placed takes (`suffix-or-name`, `title-or-name`, `particle-or-given`, `given-or-family`) are dropped, and the rule reports nothing of its own — 2026-11-12 #659, the IMPLEMENTATION.

Open: #659 (a bare `DR` in capitals at the head reads as the family under the switch, `DR Anna Berg`; whether a title that is never a surname should stand is undecided).

## Log

- **2026-11-11 #659 — THE CAPITALS ARE THE FAMILY ONLY WHERE THE CALLER SAYS THE SOURCE WRITES THEM SO.** Measured on ORCID holders' own boxes (the capped `orcid-researchers/raw.jsonl` of that day: mixed-case records with exactly one box wholly in capitals words of two or more letters), the family box is the one in capitals in 41,078 records against 4,500 for the given box at four or more letters (90.1%), and 5,122 against 747 at two or three (87.3%); 95,412 records are wholly in capitals and carry no signal. Nine in ten is a prior for a caller who knows the source, not a default: in sources that write credentials instead, an unlisted capitals word is a post-nominal (all 15 such names in `govuk-people`). Under the switch the run is the family wherever it stands (`DUPONT Jean`, `Jean DUPONT`, `Jeffrey LAM Kin-fung`), particles in capitals inside it belong to it, two surnames stay one family; capitals outrank the credential and title readings (`Hugo LAC`, `DOYEN Hugo`); a name wholly in capitals is unaffected, dotted or one-letter initials never count, a credential after a comma stays one (`Hon CHAN Kin-por, GBS, JP`); a listed credential or generation peels off the end of the run where a capitals word is left (`GRAJALES III`), a lone capitals word being the family (`Yanjun MA`), and the cost a surname whose last word spells a credential (`MBALLA MBA`, 19 of the 46,200 ORCID records with the family box in capitals); the other words keep their written order, which answers #1181 under the switch (`Jeffrey LAM Kin-fung` → given `Jeffrey`, middle `Kin-fung`); the switch composes with `unlisted_caps_suffixes` set to `AFTER_COMMA` or `OFF` and raises with `EVERYWHERE`, the two being opposite statements about an unlisted capitals word with no comma; capitals words of two or three letters count under the switch. - **2026-11-12 #659, the IMPLEMENTATION — THE RULE LAYS THE NAME OUT LAST, AND DROPS THE REPORTS IT SETTLES.** The rule runs in `post_rules` after H1, M4, P1, O1, O2 and P6, so none of their moves undoes it, and before O8 and O6, which stand down where it read the name, the capitals having named the family; O7 and O3 then read the words it leaves. It reads the words before the first comma whose role is a name, a title or a suffix, so a word read as a credential (S6) or a title (H1, H9) is in reach, while a nickname and a maiden clause are not. A report of the four kinds that ask which field a word takes is dropped where it names a word the rule placed, the capitals having settled that fork (`Hugo LAC` drops the `given-or-family` report the credential reading left); the rule adds none, the caller having declared the convention. The family words in capitals read their source as `shape` (`Token.reading`), the source the type already names for a word in capitals. A capital letter written alone is an initial or a connective, not a capitals word, so `Artur MAS I GAVARRO` reads family `GAVARRO`; and a conjunction joins runs as a particle does, and like one does not make a name mixed case, so `MARIA y LOPEZ` is read as a name in capitals.
