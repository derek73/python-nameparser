# R9 — the patronymic view

## Standing

- `ParsedName.patronymic` holds the words read as a patronymic, in whichever field the parse put them, which keep them; it is a view, not a role, so `family`, `middle`, `as_dict()`, `replace()`'s fields and the repr are unchanged, item 3 of decisions/vocabulary-model's 2026-10-28 #754 output-model entry — 2026-10-29 #754, sub-phase 4c.
- A patronymic particle and the words after it in its field all read `patronymic`, the particle by vocabulary and the rest by position, so the view is the tokens whose reading is `patronymic` — 2026-10-29 #754, sub-phase 4c.
- The East Slavic and Turkic rules write the reading wherever a policy turns them on, not only where their rotations fire: a middle word carrying the ending, and a marker with the name word before it — 2026-10-29 #754, sub-phase 4c.

Elsewhere: why the view leaves `family` and `middle` as they are, and the fielded-corpora measurements behind it, decisions/vocabulary-model's 2026-10-28 #754 entry, item 3; the rotations the two ending rules are named for, decisions/O1 and decisions/O2; the `clan`/`patronymic` line in the particle kinds, decisions/vocabulary-model's 2026-10-19 #749 entry.

Open: the Icelandic ending rule, which an `is` pack would turn on (the RFC's work order: 3b, 4c and 4f); whether the East Slavic endings should take the Ukrainian `-ivna`/`-ївна` and `-ovych`/`-ович` spellings, which would move O1's rotation too.

- 2026-10-29 #754, sub-phase 4c — IMPLEMENTED item 3 of decisions/vocabulary-model's 2026-10-28 #754 entry. What the implementation decided, beyond that entry:
  1. **Every word of a particle's patronymic reads `patronymic`.** The entry left open whether the particle reads `patronymic`, or only the name after it; the view's text is `bin Mohamad`, so a view over one reading kind needs both, and `ReadingKind.PATRONYMIC`'s docstring already gave "bin Mohamad" as the words it names. The particle's reading is the vocabulary's (`vocabulary`, its files `ar ms` for `bin`), each word after it `position`: what makes it a patronymic is where it stands, after the particle, so it names no file, and its own reading (a name word, a bound given name, a chained particle) is not kept. Weighed against: the particle alone reading `patronymic` and the view adding the words after it, which would put a run-walk in the view and make `Token.reading` say `name` for a word the view counts as the parent's name.
  2. **The run is the field, read at assemble.** The words after the particle in its field, a nickname skipped and a suffix ending it, measured over the final roles rather than group's pieces, since P1's fold and P6's attachment move words after group and a leading never-given particle stays a lone piece for the fold to find. So the run is what the family or middle holds after the particle: `ibn Ali Hasan` gives `ibn Ali Hasan` by default and `ibn Ali` under a family-first order, as the family does. Under `middle_as_family` the run follows the field the word stands as, so a folded middle's run stops at the family's own words.
  3. **A particle names a parent only with a word after it**, or where P6 attached it after a family comma or a family-first order to the family written before it, whose words then join the run (`Mohamad, Mahathir bin` gives `bin Mohamad` and `Bakar, Ali bin Abu` `bin Abu Bakar`, written in the family's order since P6's words carry the folded mark the family writes first). P6 records the attachment on the words it attaches (`vocab:attached`, beside the folded mark, at both of its sites), which assemble reads and drops as it drops the kind tag, so it is read, not inferred: O3's middle fold writes the same folded mark, and two inferences from shape each lost a case (item 8). The first draft read every patronymic particle read as a particle, and the snapshot showed `Fritz Freiherr und von Bar` reading patronymic `Bar`: the German family name spelled as Hebrew `bar`, ending its field, which group's chain had left a particle.
  4. **The kind is read at classify from the reading index**, as R8's suffix kind is: `particles` compiles every particle kind alike, so classify writes `vocab:patronymic` beside `particle` for a word the shipped files hold as a `patronymic` particle (`_vocab.is_patronymic_particle`, a memo over `_reading_index.holds_kind`), whatever the reading's default, and assemble drops the tag from the published token, a published tag making a name unequal to its 2.x pickle. Every patronymic particle has that kind only (`bar`, `bat`, `bin`, `bint`, `binte`, `binti`, `ibn` and the five native-script forms, 2026-10-29), so the lookup asks for the kind, not the first particle row. A word only a caller's `Lexicon` lists is a plain particle, `ben` included, which `ar.toml` excludes as a patronymic.
  5. **The report's alternative moves with the reading.** A leading patronymic particle read as a given name names `patronymic as family` where it named `particle as family` (`bin Mohamad`), through `_vocab.particle_reading`, which both of assign's particle alternatives now ask, so `Token.reading` and `Ambiguity.alternative` agree, as R8's 4b made them for `Jr`.
  6. **The ending rules read a word, not only a rotation.** O1 and O2 fire on exactly three and four name words; the patronymic is written in more shapes than that (`Ivanov, Ivan Ivanovich`, `Ivan Ivanovich Ivanov`, which the rotation leaves alone), so the East Slavic arm reads a middle word carrying O1's ending, and the Turkic arm a marker in a name field with the name word before it in its comma part, wherever the rule is on. The middle is the field a patronymic stands in after a given name, and a family word carrying the ending is a surname as often as not (`Roman Abramovich`). The patterns moved from `_post_rules` to `_vocab` as `has_patronymic_ending` and `is_patronymic_marker`, which the rotations and assemble both ask (mechanisms.md#ONE-PREDICATE-PER-QUESTION), the packs' hand copies pinned against them there.
