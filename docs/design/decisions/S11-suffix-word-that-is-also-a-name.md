# S11 — a suffix word that is also a name

## Standing

- A generation word with a `given` or `family` reading is gated as a bare ambiguous acronym is: the generation where the name has words to spare, the name otherwise, either reading reported (`Pedro Alves Neto` → suffix 'Neto', `Ana Neto` → family 'Neto') — 2026-10-25 #752.
- The Brazilian agnomens (`filho`, `filha`, `neto`, `neta`, `netto`, `sobrinho`, `sobrinha`) are generation readings of the `agnomen` kind, gated by the count alone, their writing never leaning; `fils` is a `generation` reading whose lower case in a mixed-case name is the generation and whose Title case leans to the name (`Marie Fils` → family 'Fils') — 2026-10-25 #752.
- A suffix word that is also a name makes no chunk claim inside a word with interior periods, which reads by its dotted shape as it did before the word was listed: `Pedro M.Filho` keeps family 'M.Filho', and `Pedro Alves M.Filho` reads suffix 'M.Filho' as on master — 2026-10-25 #752.

Open: none in this file; #843 keeps the agnomens' abbreviations (`FH`, `FO`, `NT`, `SB`).

## Log

- 2026-10-25 #752 — A GENERATION WORD THAT IS ALSO A NAME IS GATED BY THE COUNT, AND ITS KIND SAYS WHAT ITS WRITING MEANS. Carries out the generation half of the 2026-10-23 #752 entry in decisions/vocabulary-model (phase 2h), replacing decisions/generational-words' 2026-10-23 #843 decision to keep the agnomens family names. An agnomen is no part of the surname a family shares: it marks its bearer against a relative of the same name, as Júnior does, so it ends the name the way a generation does. But each one is a borne name as well, `Neto` a surname (US Census 1,645, Wikidata 276+) and a given name (Wikidata 12+), `Neta` a given name (US SSA 3,226 female births, mostly the Hebrew Neta), `Filho` and `Netto` surnames, counts from `tools/name_usage.py` on 2026-10-25 recorded on the words in `pt.toml` and `pt-BR.toml`, and a plain generation reading leaves a two-word name with no family name. So the reading is gated as an ambiguous acronym is, by the words to spare (rules/S4's count), through the same sites. Case is a cue for a generation word and none for an agnomen: French writes `Alexandre Dumas fils` with the generation in lower case and `Marie Fils` with her surname in Title case, where an agnomen is written in the case of the name around it (`Pedro Alves Neto`, `PEDRO ALVES NETO`). Capitals say nothing for either, a surname being often written in them. The kind carries the difference: an `agnomen` reading and a `generation` reading each beside a name reading derive `SUFFIX_WORDS_AMBIGUOUS`, and the agnomen kind `AGNOMENS` inside it.
