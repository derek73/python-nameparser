# S13 — a bracketed member settled by its writing

## Standing

- A member of the ambiguous class alone in brackets or quotes after the name, its writing settling it as the credential, is the suffix, read in one pass, and reports nothing (`John Smith (MA)`, `John Smith (M.A)`, `Smith, John (MA)` → suffix); any other member stays the nickname S1 reads and reports `suffix-or-nickname`, a dotted member inside the name included (`John Smith (Ed)`, `John Smith (Ma)`, `JOHN SMITH (MA)`, `Jean (M.A) Smith`) — 2026-10-26 #810.
- The writing that settles it is read by the rules that read writing: a period inside the word, S3's evidence, whatever case the name is in, and capitals in a name of mixed case, S6's, the case being that of the name's own words and never the clause's, so `john smith (MA)` keeps the nickname where bare `john smith MA` reads suffix. S13's own signal is the position alone — 2026-10-26 #810.
- Only a clause after the name is settled: one with a name word after it keeps the nickname however it is written (`Jeffrey (JD) Bricken`, `Jean 'JD' Smith`, `Smith (MA), John`), while a suffix, a title or a maiden clause after it does not count, however it is written (`John Smith (MA), PhD`, `John Smith (MA) Ph. D.`, `John Smith (MA) Dr.`), asked of the roles the parse read rather than of the following words' tags — 2026-10-26 #810.
- Where the rest of the name then reads otherwise than beside a bracketed unambiguous credential in the same place, the member's twin, the difference is an accepted cost, recorded in rules/S13's Accepted bullets and counted by tests/v2/test_parser.py's census (`Jack (MA)` → family 'Jack', where `Jack (PhD)` reads given 'Jack') — 2026-10-26 #810.

  - **Why the writing, and why after the name.** After the name, brackets hold either a credential or the name the person goes by, and the count of words cannot tell the two apart there, the name being whole without the clause. The writing can: credentials are written in capitals and with their periods, names are not, which is S6's and S3's ground. Inside the name the same member let out of the brackets would read as a middle name, where the brackets more likely hold the name the person goes by: a spike in the design round (2026-10-23, against 638685b4; a dated snapshot, the spike's switch being in no tree) that escaped every member written in capitals or with an interior period moved `JD` from the nickname to the middle name in two committed rows, `Jean 'JD' Smith` and `JEFFREY (JD) BRICKEN`. So a clause with a name word after it is not settled.
  - **Why the name's case, not the clause's.** The same spike asked the bracketed word's own case and moved 6 FEC names, every one wrong: the corpus is written wholly in capitals, so capitals there settle nothing, and `(ED)` in such a name is the nickname. Asking the case of the name's own words, the fact S6 records for every other site (`settled_by_case`), moves none of them. The clause's own words are no name of their own, so `john smith (MA)` asks the case of `john smith` and keeps the nickname, where bare `john smith MA` reads suffix; the two differ by construction, the bare word being one of the name's own words.
  - **Why the suffix, not the bare reading.** S1's escape lets suffix-shaped content read as if written bare. A settled member cannot, because since #872 a word of two dotted single letters in a script with capitals reads bare as paired initials (decisions/S3), so `John Smith (M.A)` let out of its brackets would read family 'M.A'. The clause is read as the suffix directly, the brackets having already set it apart from the name's words. The decision is made first in post_rules, where every word's role is read, and the `suffix-or-nickname` report moved there with it, from classify, so that it is raised where the fork is decided.
