# S13 — a bracketed member settled by its writing

## Standing

- A member of the ambiguous class alone in brackets or quotes after the name is the suffix where its writing settles it, written with a period inside it or in capitals in a name of mixed case, and reports nothing (`John Smith (MA)`, `John Smith (M.A)`, `Smith, John (MA)` → suffix); any other member stays the nickname S1 reads and reports `suffix-or-nickname`, a dotted member inside the name included (`Jean (M.A) Smith`) (`John Smith (Ed)`, `John Smith (Ma)`, `JOHN SMITH (MA)`) — 2026-10-26 #810.
- The case is that of the name's own words, as S6 asks it, never the clause's own, so `john smith (MA)` keeps the nickname where bare `john smith MA` reads suffix — 2026-10-26 #810.
- Only a clause after the name is settled: one with a name word after it keeps the nickname however it is written (`Jeffrey (JD) Bricken`, `Jean 'JD' Smith`, `Smith (MA), John`), while a suffix, a title or a maiden clause after it does not count, however it is written (`John Smith (MA), PhD`, `John Smith (MA) Ph. D.`, `John Smith (MA) Dr.`, `John Smith (MA) née Jones` → suffix 'MA'), asked of the roles the parse read rather than of the following words' tags — 2026-10-26 #810.
- A settled member is the suffix, not the word read as if written bare: bare, `M.A` reads as paired initials (decisions/S3 (#864)), and the brackets have already set it apart from the name's words — 2026-10-26 #810.
