# C6 — an unlisted all-caps word needs the name's case contrast

## Standing

- After a comma, an unlisted word of letters in mixed case that opens with two capitals or ends in one is the caps shape as an all-caps word is, in C4's count, C6's contrast and C16's company (`Jane Smith, MRes` → suffix 'MRes'); a capital only inside the word, or a hyphen or slash in it, keeps it a name word (`Jane Smith, LeBron`, `Jane Smith, Pharm-D` → given), and the trailing positions under `CapsSuffixes.EVERYWHERE` read capitals alone (`Alex Lee ONeill` → family 'ONeill') — 2026-11-21 #1517.

Elsewhere: this rule's decision, that an unlisted all-caps word after the comma joins the ambiguous credential class by default behind two name words, where the name carries a case contrast (`John Smith, XYZ` → suffix 'XYZ'; `Smith, XYZ` → given 'XYZ'; `MÜLLER WEIß, HANS` → given 'HANS'), with its two-letter rule (narrowed for two capitals followed by credentials by decisions/C5's 2026-11-11 #1323 entry, and for two capitals alone before a later part's listed credential by its 2026-11-15 #1392 entry) and the mixed-run membership — decisions/S9's 2026-10-01 (Derek), #564 entry, where the caps shape's switch is decided; the contrast test reused for a suffix word ending the name before the comma — decisions/C12's 2026-10-18 (Derek), #656 entry.

- 2026-11-21 #1517 — A WORD IN MIXED CASE WRITTEN THE WAY A CREDENTIAL IS JOINS THE CAPS SHAPE AFTER A COMMA. Decided 2026-11-21. An unlisted word of letters in mixed case that opens with two capitals or ends in one (`MRes`, `DClinPsy`, `AuD`, `BScN`) is the caps shape wherever an all-caps word is read after a comma: C4's count, C6's contrast and C16's company (`Jane Smith, MRes` → given 'Jane', family 'Smith', suffix 'MRes', reported, where 1.4.0 through 3.1.0 read given 'MRes', family 'Jane Smith'). Such a word is written neither in capitals nor the way a name is, and no vocabulary lists every degree written so. A capital only inside the word is a name's writing (`LeBron`, `McKenzie`, `DeShawn`), and a word in mixed case holding a hyphen or a slash is left out: a first build took joined chunks too, and given boxes are full of names joined so (`Cheng-I`, `I-Chun`, `Jay-R` in hundreds of ORCID given boxes, `Gul-E` and `Noor-E` in PSC's), so `Pharm-D` stays the given name, as does `PhDc`, whose ends hold one capital.
