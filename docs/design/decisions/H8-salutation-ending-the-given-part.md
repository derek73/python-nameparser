# H8 — a salutation ending the given part

## Standing

- A salutation ending the given part after a family comma, written bare or with a stop at its edge that H5's abbreviation shape does not take (`Mr..`), is the title, read as its period-marked spelling reads in every comma shape and under every name order: `Doe, Jane Mrs` → title 'Mrs', given 'Jane'; `DOE, JOHN Q MR` → title 'MR', middle 'Q' — 2026-10-24 #835.
- Only the salutations marked `after_given` in the vocabulary qualify, and a salutation borne as a given name or a surname is left out by an exclusion carrying its counts, since a middle name may be either (`Doe, John Goodman` keeps middle 'Goodman') — 2026-10-24 #835.
- No other title kind qualifies (`Doe, John King` and `Doe, Jane Dr` keep their middle names), a title/suffix dual stays a suffix (`Doe, Jane Ms`), and the comma-less writing is H5's (`Jane Doe Mrs` keeps family 'Mrs') — 2026-10-24 #835.
- A salutation that is someone's middle name reads as the title after a family comma, an accepted cost (`Doe, Jane Miss`); so does the empty head's one given word moving to the family as the period-marked spelling moves it (`, Jane Mrs` → family 'Jane') — 2026-10-24 #835.
- The rule reads the comma, not a declared order: `Doe Jane Mrs` under FAMILY_FIRST keeps middle 'Mrs', so the family-comma invariant of decisions/two-input-invariants does not hold for a trailing salutation — 2026-10-24 #835.

## Log

- 2026-10-24, #835 — A BARE SALUTATION ENDING A FAMILY COMMA'S GIVEN PART IS THE TITLE. `Doe, Jane Mrs` read middle 'Mrs' while `Doe, Jane Mrs.` (H5) and `Doe, John Jr Mr` (the salutation behind a post-nominal) read title, and `Doe, John Mr Jr` read middle 'Mr'; 1.4.0 and 2.5.0 read the first as middle 'Mrs' too (wheels checked 2026-10-24). The FEC's itemized contributions write `FAMILY, GIVEN MIDDLE SALUTATION` in capitals, the period often left off: on #834's `fec-individuals` corpus (file dated 2026-10-18, measured 2026-10-22, figures in #835) 13,243 names ended in a bare title word read as a middle name, `MR` 8,483 of them.
  THE REASON H5's BARE-WORD CLAUSE DOES NOT REACH HERE. H5 keeps a bare title word at the end of a name as a name word because TITLES holds ordinary surnames (`Mary Jane King`). After a family comma the surname's place is marked, so only the middle-name reading is left against the title, and of the title kinds (#750) the salutation is the one that is a form of address and not something a person is named after. So the rule is keyed on that kind, and narrower than the kind: the property marks the salutations not borne as names.
  THE VOCABULARY. A behavioral title property, `after_given = true`, allowed on a `salutation` reading only (a build check), compiles into `AFTER_GIVEN_TITLES` and the new `Lexicon.after_given_titles`; v1's `Constants` has no attribute for it, and `_snapshot()` intersects it with the titles, so deleting the title is the v1 knob. Considered and not taken: keying the rule on the `salutation` kind itself, with exclusions for the borne words — it makes a kind compile into a behavior, where every behavior so far is a property (decisions/vocabulary-model).
  THE VETTING (`uv run python tools/name_usage.py --json` over every salutation and the romanizations of the non-Latin ones, 2026-10-24). A middle name may be a given name or a surname, so a word borne as either is excluded. Excluded, each with its counts on the word: goodman (US Census 2010 surname 73,136), herr (11,980), herren (4,466), master (2,735; SSA given 476), mister (1,211; SSA 746), señora (SSA given 1,065, as SENORA), signor (999), signore (895), herra (638), paní (403, as PANI), señor (282, as SENOR), härra (237, as HARRA), fru and frú (a Cameroonian surname, about 10,800 bearers by the note on sv.toml's entry, and 132 in the Census), пан (Wikidata family 743), the Bengali মোঃ and মো (they abbreviate Mohammad, which #853 reads as a given name where it leads), श्री/শ্রী (also an element of given names, Sri or Shri; one Wikidata given-name bearer, excluded on the doubt rather than the count) and बाबू (romanized Babu, Census surname 1,496, ranked a given name in India and Sri Lanka by nam_dict, and an element of compound given names such as Ram Babu). A diacritic-free count stands for an accented word, the Census and SSA writing none. A romanization counts for a word in another script only where its bearers write that script: बाबू's do, while the bearers of the romanizations of सौ (Sau, Vietnamese), মি (Mi, Korean and Chinese), κα (Kyria, US given names) and пані (Pani, an Indian surname) do not, so those are kept. Kept with small counts, recorded on each word as a name reading: miss (Census surname 164), heren (123), sra (Census surname 149, SSA given 27), signora (SSA given 90), señorita, bwana and mr (SSA under 10 each), frau (Wikidata family 42), and gospodin (a Bulgarian given name in nam_dict, band 5, kept because a Bulgarian middle name is a patronymic, Gospodinov, rather than the given name itself). The line at roughly 200 US bearers is a judgment, not a measured threshold. ms and sr carry no flag, being title/suffix duals the suffix reading takes first. `hr` was excluded on the FEC read below. Recompute the set: `uv run python tools/vocab/query.py --kind salutation`, and the refusals with `--excluded after_given`.
  THE MECHANISM. H5's trailing title chain, in the given part after a family comma, admits a word the vocabulary marks, matched after its edge stops are folded, so a doubled stop (`Mr..`, which is not H5's abbreviation shape) is admitted as the bare word is. Everything H5's chain already does there — transparency to the suffix reading, the floor leaving one name piece, the title joining the field in input order — holds for it unchanged. The group-side predictors of that part (the particle chain's bound and the bound given-name join's decline, which read the part as `tail_reading` reads it) admit the same words, so they cannot disagree with the walk (the Gotcha on group-side predictors in AGENTS.md). Measured: over a grid of nine family-comma heads (an empty head, a nickname-only head, particle and connective surnames, a suffix and a title before the comma), 21 given parts (initials, a bound given name, particles, a title-particle, an ambiguous acronym, numerals V and VI, a post-nominal, a one-case part), seven salutation spellings and six tails (a second comma, a trailing suffix, acronym or period-marked title), under all three name orders, every one of the 23,814 bare rows reads exactly as its period-marked twin reads, fields and ambiguity kinds both, and none of the period-marked rows moved against master `3f8e29b2`.
  THE CORPORA (measured 2026-10-24 against master `3f8e29b2`, py3.11). No committed corpus name moved: the snapshot gains only this rule's own examples, and the CLDR, GND and Wikidata agreement views match their records. `snapshot.py --local fec-individuals`, base recorded with `--tree`: 12,381 of 1,893,964 names move, each a salutation leaving the middle name for the title, some with a credential or a generational word behind it: `MR` 9,339, `MRS` 1,908, `MISS` 1,108, `MR..` 24, and two more joining a title already read. Three had read `HR` as the Danish and German abbreviation of Herr, one of them behind a woman's given name: in US data it is more likely two initials run together, so `hr` was excluded on that evidence and those three are not among the moves. 385 of the moves lose a `CONJUNCTION_OR_INITIAL` report (`DOE, JOHN E MR`): with the salutation gone the letter has no name word behind it to connect, which is what P8 asks. In 361 of them the letter stays a middle initial; the other 24 are among the 59 below. `ukec-individuals` does not move.
  A CONSEQUENCE NOT DECIDED HERE: 59 of the FEC moves end the given part in a lone `V` or `I` once the salutation is the title (`DOE, JANE I MRS`), which then reads as the generational suffix, as `DOE, JANE I` and `DOE, JANE I MRS.` already read on master (#144's lenient tail test after a family comma). In these names it is a middle initial. The bare spelling now reads as its twins read; whether #144's reading should change is its own question.
  THE FAMILY-FIRST WRITING (the design-docs review). A declared FAMILY_FIRST order fixes the surname as a comma does, but the rule reads only the comma: `Doe Jane Mrs` under FAMILY_FIRST keeps middle 'Mrs' (FAMILY_FIRST_GIVEN_LAST: given 'Mrs'). decisions/two-input-invariants pairs those two writings; over the 82 flagged words written without a stop, an apostrophe or a hyphen, each after three family and three given names, they agree on 738 of 738 pairs on master and on none now. Recorded as an Accepted clause of rules/H8 rather than extended: the declared order says where the surname is, not where the given part ends, and the trailing slot of a comma-less name is H5's.
