# H6 — a title before a family comma

- 2026-10-17 (Derek), #701 — A TITLE OPENING THE PART BEFORE A FAMILY COMMA IS THE TITLE. `Dr. Smith, John` read family 'Dr. Smith', and every release did the same: 1.4.0 from PyPI gives last 'Dr. Smith' and 'Prof. Brühl' (suffix 'Dr.') for `Prof. Dr. Brühl, Ada Cornelia` (measured 2026-10-17, `PYTHONSAFEPATH=1`, wheel path asserted). The reading was written down on purpose as Accepted clauses of rules/H2 and #H3 ("before a family comma the pre-comma text is wholly the family name") and assumed by C1's Freiherr clause ('Prof. Cruz, Ed'); all three are rewritten here. The listing form moves the surname to the front and leaves the title where it was written, and CLDR's index renderings write exactly that in several locales (`prof. dr. von Selimović Hodžić, Izeta Amra`, `г-н Вернер, Иван Асен`).
  DECIDED (Derek, the five points of the design proposal): (1) VOCABULARY ONLY. H2's shape does not fire before a family comma, so `Xyz. Smith, John` keeps family 'Xyz. Smith' — the slot is a surname's slot too, as the trailing slot is (#H5), and every CLDR case is a listed title. (2) The run leaves the family its last piece (`Dr., John`, `Greve, Anna`). (3) A word that is a particle too stops the run, bare or period-marked, so `St Pierre, Ed` and the 1919 legal-surname reading of `Freiherr von Berg, Ed` (C1's Accepted clause) stand, and a title in front of them still peels. (4) The peeled titles join the title field in input order, and the comma decision (`_comma.decide`) is untouched — the count before the comma already treated a title as no name word. (5) ACCEPTED COST: a bare surname-like title opening a two-word surname is the title, `King Smith, John` → title 'King', as `Greve Anna` already reads (#H1).
  ADDED IN THE PROTOTYPE, not in the proposal: #H3's floor. The first version peeled `MD` from `MD PhD Jr., John`, leaving family 'PhD' — which a case row (`family_comma_segment_zero_is_not_the_run`) caught. A title addresses somebody, and a post-nominal is not somebody, so the run stops where nothing but post-nominals stands behind it: `Dr. Jr., John` keeps family 'Dr.'. The second version stopped in front of ANY piece of the suffix vocabulary, and the design-docs review found it too broad: a title/suffix dual standing SECOND in the run blocked it and then read as a suffix inside the family (`Rev. Sr. Smith, Mary` → family 'Rev. Smith', suffix 'Sr.', where the comma-less `Rev. Sr. Mary Smith` reads title 'Rev. Sr.'), and so did `Mc` and `vd`, which the family loop's own P2 clause reads as a surname's head (`Dr. Mc Donald, John`). Its check compared `T Smith, John` with `T John Smith` over every title word, bare and period-marked, alone, behind `Dr.` and ahead of it (4,788 pairs over the 798 title words before #705's merge): 36 disagreed, 12 the decided particle class and 24 the twelve duals in second position. The floor asks the H3 question instead — is everything behind this title post-nominal? — so the duals peel wherever a name word follows them, as they do in front of a name with no comma.
  Declined: excluding the title/suffix duals from the run (it would keep `Ms Smith, Jane` whole); peeling by H2's shape too (the trailing doctrine, #H5, says only vocabulary claims a word in a surname's slot).

  THE PR REVIEW (2026-10-17), three decisions. (a) `gov` JOINS TITLES (Derek). `Lt. Gov. Smith, John` read title 'Lt.', family 'Gov. Smith' — a compound split across two fields, where master kept it whole in the family and the comma-less `Lt. Gov. John Smith` reads title 'Lt. Gov.' by H2's shape. The run is vocabulary-only, so the fix is the vocabulary: `gov` is the abbreviation of the listed `governor`. Counts (`uv run python tools/name_usage.py Gov`, 2026-10-17): 236 US Census 2010 surname bearers, rank 80,419, 91.53% Asian/Pacific Islander; SSA given names under 5 a year; Wikidata 3 family-name bearers, all Israeli. The claim acts only in front, so `John Gov` keeps family 'Gov', and a surname written first loses it (`Gov Sokha` → title 'Gov'), the graf-class trade (#salutation-titles) at a smaller incidence. No corpus name moved. The other unlisted second words the review found (`insp`, `sen`, `supt`, `cmdr`, `atty`, `msgr`) still split; each is a vocabulary question on its own. (b) `Dr. PhD Smith, John` KEEPS the branch reading, family 'PhD Smith' (Derek): the first piece the run leaves is family whatever it is, as piece 0 is with no title (`PhD Smith, John`, read that way by 1.4.0 too); master and 1.4.0 gave `Dr. PhD Smith, John` family 'Dr. Smith', suffix 'PhD'. Garbage input, so no row. (c) `Graf Lambsdorff, Otto` reads title 'Graf' while `Freiherr von Berg, Ed` keeps the rank, and the clause first written for it gave the 1919 legal-surname argument as the reason — which holds for every German rank alike. The reason is the particle stop: `freiherr` is the one rank in `particles`. rules/H6's Accepted clause now says so, and the vocabulary question (should `graf`, `fürst`, `prinz` and the rest read as `freiherr` does, with and without a comma) is [#709](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/709).
