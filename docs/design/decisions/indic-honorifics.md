# indic-honorifics — the renunciate class and the Indic honorific vocabulary (2026-09-06, #346/#344/#343)

Closes #346, #344 and #343 as one bundle: each CLASS is decided once, and each script's membership in it is then argued word by word, so the three lists overlap without matching. No parser code moves in it. Four existing forks carry the whole thing — the renunciate fold (rules/H1), the spaced trailing suffix path, title vocabulary beating the one-letter-plus-period initial shape, and the unvalidated given_name_titles — and all four were confirmed on the pre-bundle tree with a throwaway override before any wordlist was edited — the recipe is `Parser(lexicon=Lexicon.default().add(given_name_titles={...}, titles={...}, suffix_words={...})).parse(text)`, and the fourth fork is the one a reader will not expect: `Lexicon` deliberately does not validate `given_name_titles` against `titles` (see #given-name-titles), so an entry in the given-name set alone is never recognized as a title at all. In the shipped data that is free, TITLES being GIVEN_NAME_TITLES | {...}.

## Standing

- The renunciate class — given-name titles for a tradition that drops the surname — is decided once, by how the title ADDRESSES and by surname retention, and each script's membership is argued word by word on those two criteria, never on symmetry between the lists: `Swami Vivekananda` reads title 'Swami', given 'Vivekananda', and `Lama Zopa` folds where `लामा जोपा` and `লামা জোপা` do not — 2026-09-06 #346.
- Native-script renunciate titles with no Latin twin ship alone: `संत कबीर` folds while `Sant Kabir` does not, Latin `sant` being a Punjabi given name and an `excluded` entry in hi.toml — 2026-09-06 #346.
- `rabbi` and `imam` stay out of the class, addressing by title and keeping surnames (`Rabbi Cohen` reads family 'Cohen'), and `venerable` stays a plain title, its traditions splitting on surname retention, so neither answer is right for the single-name case and it is deliberately not an `excluded` entry — 2026-09-06 #346.
- The class's accepted cost is a real given name that is also a title: `Guru Dutt` reads title 'Guru', given 'Dutt' — 2026-09-06 #346.
- `rinpoche`, जी, साहब, साहिब, साहेब, महाराज and সাহেব, বাবু, মহারাজ ship as suffix words and spaced only, none a glued honorific, on the evidence recorded for जी alone (`Mohan Sharma जी` reads suffix 'जी', `Mohan Sharmaजी` keeps family 'Sharmaजी'); the Tibetan -la particle needs no entry — 2026-09-06 #346.
- Bengali বাবু is trailing and Devanagari बाबू leading, different codepoints that cannot interact (`অমল বাবু` reads suffix 'বাবু'), and a sweep harmonizing the two argues with their wordlist comments — 2026-09-06 #346.
- মোঃ/মো and মোসাঃ/মোছাঃ are titles, the functional home since `given` must stay the name the person is addressed by (`মোঃ আবদুল করিম` reads given 'আবদুল') — 2026-09-06 #346.
- Latin `mst` is a credential and no title, as `md` is: a leading `Mst` is the given name its filers type it as (`Mrs Mst Nasima Akther` → title 'Mrs', given 'Mst'), at `md`'s accepted cost, the given part of a family comma it opens reading as a credential (`Begum, Mst Amina` → suffix 'Mst Amina'); after a name it is the degree (`Mary Smith, Mst` → suffix 'Mst'); `Mst.` with its period opening a name is a title by rules/H2's shape (`Mst. Nasima Akther` → title 'Mst.'), but opening the given part of a family comma it is a credential like the bare form, as `Md.` is (`Akther, Mst. Nasima` → suffix 'Mst. Nasima') — 2026-11-06 (Derek), #1130.
- An abbreviation carrying a mark is spelled with it, and the Bengali ones ship as the visarga form and the bare stem while the Devanagari ones ship as the bare stem alone, the dotted form listed in neither script — 2026-09-06 #346.
- Title vocabulary beats the one-letter-plus-period initial shape, measured and pinned by rows and not a rule: `ড. মুহাম্মদ ইউনূস` reads title 'ড.' while `র. কে. নারায়ণ`, with no entry behind it, keeps its initials — 2026-09-06 #346.
- An unlisted abugida honorific stays a name word, and rules/H2's executable example of it is `প্রকৌশলী. Sen`, so whoever ships প্রকৌশলী moves the example first; the Tier 4 candidates in the Indic scripts (transliterated military ranks, Advocate, Engineer, Marhum, the kinship forms, मां/माता/মা, the hafiz/syed/shahid forms) are skipped pending a corpus check and not declined — 2026-09-06 #346.
- Latin `md` is a credential and no title: a leading `Md` is the given name it abbreviates (`Md Abdul Karim` → given 'Md', middle 'Abdul'; `Mr Md Karim Uddin` → title 'Mr', given 'Md', middle 'Karim'), at two accepted costs, a particle behind it starting the family (`Md Abu Sayed` → family 'Abu Sayed') and the given part of a family comma it opens, titles in front or not, reading as credentials (`Uddin, Md Karim` and `Uddin, Mr Md Karim` → suffix 'Md Karim'); the period-marked `Md.` is still a title by rules/H2's leading-abbreviation shape (`Md. Abdul Karim` → title 'Md.'), and the Bengali মোঃ/মো entries stay titles — 2026-10-23 #853.
- The period-marked `Md.` stays a title by rules/H2's leading-abbreviation shape, an accepted cost: `Md. Karim Uddin` reads title 'Md.', given 'Karim', and `Mr Md. Karim Uddin` title 'Mr Md.' — 2026-10-27 (Derek), #853.
- The romanized `pandit`, `sardar`, `kumari`, `babu`, `acharya` and `begum` are titles under no pack and no strict choice, each opening far more names as a given name or a surname than as the title (`Kumari Selja`, `Sardar Ahmad`, `Babu Joseph`), and each is an `excluded` entry carrying its counts — 2026-11-03 (Derek), #345.
- `sushri` is a title only inside a title phrase under the plain Hindi choice and a title wherever one is read under a strict one: hi.toml's salutation is `default = false` and or.toml declares the Odia given name, so `parser_for(locales.HI)` reads `Sushri Sangita Behera` given 'Sushri' (and `Her Excellency Sushri Sangita Behera` title 'Her Excellency Sushri', rules/H9) and `parser_for(locales.HI, strict=True)` title 'Sushri' — 2026-11-03 (Derek), #345.
- `श्री` stays a salutation by default, the Hindi, Marathi and Konkani Mr.; the Nepali given-name element is `locales.NE`'s reading (ne.toml, `default = false`), so `श्री कृष्ण श्रेष्ठ` reads title 'श्री' by default and under the Nepali choice `श्री` is a title only inside a title phrase and elsewhere a name word placed by position (given 'श्री' in the default order), an accepted cost recorded in rules/H9 — 2026-11-03 (Derek), #345/#870.

Elsewhere: the bundle's class-level refusals are recorded under decisions/vocabulary-exclusions, not here: the Latin forms of the Indic titles belonging to the opt-in packs of #345 (2026-09-06, #344), the native spelling shipping alone where a transliteration collides with real names (sri/shri, sant, pandit), the ban on the spaced trailing set becoming glued honorifics, and the per-word title refusals (ঠাকুর/ठाकुर, कुमारी/কুমারী, बेगम/বেগম, শেখ/शेख, आचार्य, राजा/रानी), each an `excluded` entry (`uv run python tools/vocab/query.py --excluded title`).

- **Abbreviation marks and periods.** An entry carrying an abbreviation mark is spelled with it: the Bengali visarga ঃ (U+0983) is a spacing combining mark (Unicode category Mc, not a letter and not punctuation), and `_lexicon._normalize` lowercases, composes NFC for a non-ASCII word and strips edge whitespace and the four full stops of `FULL_STOPS` — no NFKC, no casefold anywhere in that module — so the mark is simply never touched and মোঃ reaches the lexicon and matches intact (corrected 2026-09-10, #cjk-full-stops: the fold was ASCII periods only and no NFC when this was written; the visarga argument is unchanged, ঃ having no canonical decomposition, so NFC leaves it exactly where it was). Recompute with `Lexicon.default().add(titles={"মোঃ"}).titles & {"মোঃ"}`, which is non-empty iff the mark survived, and `unicodedata.category("ঃ")`, which is `Mc`. Period spellings (মো., মোসা., ডা., प्रो., पं.) match the bare stem through the same edge-period strip, so every BENGALI abbreviation carrying the mark ships as two entries, the visarga form and the bare stem (মোঃ and মো, মোসাঃ and মোসা, মোছাঃ and মোছা, মিঃ and মি), while the Devanagari abbreviations carry no such mark and ship as the bare stem alone (प्रो, पं, प्रा, डा); the dotted form is never listed in either script.
