# indic-honorifics — the renunciate class and the Indic honorific vocabulary (2026-09-06, #346/#344/#343)

Closes #346, #344 and #343 as one bundle: each CLASS is decided once, and each script's membership in it is then argued word by word, so the three lists overlap without matching. No parser code moves in it. Four existing forks carry the whole thing — the renunciate fold (rules/H1), the spaced trailing suffix path, title vocabulary beating the one-letter-plus-period initial shape, and the unvalidated given_name_titles — and all four were confirmed on the pre-bundle tree with a throwaway override before any wordlist was edited — the recipe is `Parser(lexicon=Lexicon.default().add(given_name_titles={...}, titles={...}, suffix_words={...})).parse(text)`, and the fourth fork is the one a reader will not expect: `Lexicon` deliberately does not validate `given_name_titles` against `titles` (see #given-name-titles), so an entry in the given-name set alone is never recognized as a title at all. In the shipped data that is free, TITLES being GIVEN_NAME_TITLES | {...}.

## Standing

- The renunciate class — given-name titles for a tradition that drops the surname — is decided once, by how the title ADDRESSES and by surname retention, and each script's membership is argued word by word on those two criteria, never on symmetry between the lists: `Swami Vivekananda` reads title 'Swami', given 'Vivekananda', and `Lama Zopa` folds where `लामा जोपा` and `লামা জোপা` do not — 2026-09-06 #346.
- Native-script renunciate titles with no Latin twin ship alone: `संत कबीर` folds while `Sant Kabir` does not, Latin `sant` being a Punjabi given name and an `excluded` entry in hi.toml — 2026-09-06 #346.
- `rabbi` and `imam` stay out of the class, addressing by title and keeping surnames (`Rabbi Cohen` reads family 'Cohen'), and `venerable` stays a plain title, its traditions splitting on surname retention, so neither answer is right for the single-name case and it is deliberately not an `excluded` entry — 2026-09-06 #346.
- The class's accepted cost is a real given name that is also a title: `Guru Dutt` reads title 'Guru', given 'Dutt' — 2026-09-06 #346.
- `rinpoche`, जी, साहब, साहिब, साहेब, महाराज and সাহেব, বাবু, মহারাজ ship as suffix words and spaced only, none a glued honorific, on the evidence recorded for जी alone (`Mohan Sharma जी` reads suffix 'जी', `Mohan Sharmaजी` keeps family 'Sharmaजी'); the Tibetan -la particle needs no entry — 2026-09-06 #346.
- Bengali বাবু is trailing and Devanagari बाबू leading, different codepoints that cannot interact (`অমল বাবু` reads suffix 'বাবু'), and a sweep harmonizing the two argues with their wordlist comments — 2026-09-06 #346.
- মোঃ/মো and মোসাঃ/মোছাঃ are titles, the functional home since `given` must stay the name the person is addressed by (`মোঃ আবদুল করিম` reads given 'আবদুল'), and Latin `mst` is not in the default, its refusal an `excluded` entry in bn.toml — 2026-09-06 #346.
- An abbreviation carrying a mark is spelled with it, and the Bengali ones ship as the visarga form and the bare stem while the Devanagari ones ship as the bare stem alone, the dotted form listed in neither script — 2026-09-06 #346.
- Title vocabulary beats the one-letter-plus-period initial shape, measured and pinned by rows and not a rule: `ড. মুহাম্মদ ইউনূস` reads title 'ড.' while `র. কে. নারায়ণ`, with no entry behind it, keeps its initials — 2026-09-06 #346.
- An unlisted abugida honorific stays a name word, and rules/H2's executable example of it is `প্রকৌশলী. Sen`, so whoever ships প্রকৌশলী moves the example first; the Tier 4 candidates in the Indic scripts (transliterated military ranks, Advocate, Engineer, Marhum, the kinship forms, मां/माता/মা, the hafiz/syed/shahid forms) are skipped pending a corpus check and not declined — 2026-09-06 #346.
- Latin `md` is a credential and no title: a leading `Md` is the given name it abbreviates (`Md Abdul Karim` → given 'Md', middle 'Abdul'; `Mr Md Karim Uddin` → title 'Mr', given 'Md', middle 'Karim'), at two accepted costs, a particle behind it starting the family (`Md Abu Sayed` → family 'Abu Sayed') and the given part of a family comma it opens, titles in front or not, reading as credentials (`Uddin, Md Karim` and `Uddin, Mr Md Karim` → suffix 'Md Karim'); the period-marked `Md.` is still a title by rules/H2's leading-abbreviation shape (`Md. Abdul Karim` → title 'Md.'), and the Bengali মোঃ/মো entries stay titles — 2026-10-23 #853.
- The period-marked `Md.` stays a title by rules/H2's leading-abbreviation shape, an accepted cost: `Md. Karim Uddin` reads title 'Md.', given 'Karim', and `Mr Md. Karim Uddin` title 'Mr Md.' — 2026-10-27 (Derek), #853.

Elsewhere: the bundle's class-level refusals are recorded under decisions/vocabulary-exclusions, not here: the Latin forms of the Indic titles belonging to the opt-in packs of #345 (2026-09-06, #344), the native spelling shipping alone where a transliteration collides with real names (sri/shri, sant, pandit, mst), the ban on the spaced trailing set becoming glued honorifics, and the per-word title refusals (ঠাকুর/ठाकुर, कुमारी/কুমারী, बेगम/বেগম, শেখ/शेख, आचार्य, राजा/रानी), each an `excluded` entry (`uv run python tools/vocab/query.py --excluded title`).

Open: #514 (whether the Tibetan prefix titles Tulku, Khenpo, Geshe, Kyabje and Jetsun fold like `Lama Zopa`); #345 (hi and bn locale packs for Latin-transliterated Indic honorifics).

- **The renunciate class (#346).** What is decided once is the CLASS — renunciate given-name titles — against rules.md's H Background, which already carried the two criteria: membership in the given-name-title list follows HOW THE TITLE ADDRESSES, and whether an empty family is correct output follows SURNAME RETENTION. Renunciation answers both at once, so "Swami Vivekananda" reads given Vivekananda, family "". Each script's MEMBERSHIP is its own list, and the three overlap rather than coincide: Latin swami/guru/baba/lama (moved out of the TITLES-only block), Devanagari स्वामी/गुरु/बाबा/संत, Bengali স্বামী/শ্রীল/গুরু/বাবা. lama is a Tibetan title whose native script is Tibetan, and the Hindi and Bengali borrowings लामा/লামা are rare enough that no entry was argued for either — "Lama Zopa" folds, "लामा जोपा" and "লামা জোপা" do not. संत (Sant) and শ্রীল (Srila) run the other way: renunciate titles native to those scripts with NO Latin entry, — Latin sant collides with a real given name (its `excluded` entry in nameparser/vocab/hi.toml) and srila has no transliteration in use — "संत कबीर" folds, "Sant Kabir" does not. A sweep "harmonizing" the three lists must therefore argue each word on the two criteria, never on symmetry. rabbi and imam address by TITLE rather than by given name, so they fail the first criterion and stay out; the second agrees, those traditions keeping surnames. "Rabbi Cohen" keeps family Cohen and is pinned as the control beside the Swami row. The four Latin entries were already TITLES vocabulary, so the promotion changes only which field the one name word lands in, and C-i's position test was not re-run for them; the known leading-position collision is Guru Dutt, whose given name is Guru — family went from Dutt to "" — accepted as the pre-existing cost of guru being a title at all, and pinned by the row renunciate_title_swallows_a_real_given_name.
- **venerable is left in TITLES only, and is deliberately NOT an Excluded entry.** The traditions split on surname retention — Buddhist monastics drop the family name, Anglican archdeacons and Catholic candidates keep it — so neither answer is right for the single-name case. TITLES has no ambiguous subset and no AmbiguityKind, so, as for king in #v1-xfail-triage and roz in the maiden markers, the only two expressions available are ship and do not ship; this is the third thing, a membership that is right and an OUTPUT that is undecidable, and it stays where it is.
- **Rinpoche is postpositional** ("Sogyal Rinpoche", "Lama Zopa Rinpoche"), so it joins SUFFIX_WORDS and no title set. The Tibetan -la particle is vocative and glued and needs no entry. - **Abbreviation marks and periods.** An entry carrying an abbreviation mark is spelled with it: the Bengali visarga ঃ (U+0983) is a spacing combining mark (Unicode category Mc, not a letter and not punctuation), and `_lexicon._normalize` lowercases, composes NFC for a non-ASCII word and strips edge whitespace and the four full stops of `FULL_STOPS` — no NFKC, no casefold anywhere in that module — so the mark is simply never touched and মোঃ reaches the lexicon and matches intact (corrected 2026-09-10, #cjk-full-stops: the fold was ASCII periods only and no NFC when this was written; the visarga argument is unchanged, ঃ having no canonical decomposition, so NFC leaves it exactly where it was). Recompute with `Lexicon.default().add(titles={"মোঃ"}).titles & {"মোঃ"}`, which is non-empty iff the mark survived, and `unicodedata.category("ঃ")`, which is `Mc`. Period spellings (মো., মোসা., ডা., प्रो., पं.) match the bare stem through the same edge-period strip, so every BENGALI abbreviation carrying the mark ships as two entries, the visarga form and the bare stem (মোঃ and মো, মোসাঃ and মোসা, মোছাঃ and মোছা, মিঃ and মি), while the Devanagari abbreviations carry no such mark and ship as the bare stem alone (प्रो, पं, प्रा, डा); the dotted form is never listed in either script.
- **Vocabulary beats the initial reading.** A one-letter-plus-period Bengali abbreviation is is_initial-shaped and the title entry wins: "ড. মুহাম্মদ ইউনূস" reads title ড., given মুহাম্মদ, family ইউনূস, while "র. কে. নারায়ণ" — real initials with no entry behind them — is unchanged. #343 verified the same fork at 2.1.0; it is re-verified here on the shipped set.
