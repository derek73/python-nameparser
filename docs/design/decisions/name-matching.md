# name-matching — grouping keys and a consistency check that explains itself

## Standing

- Name matching answers "could these two names be the same person?" with a verdict and a per-role table, never a score: `MatchResult.consistent`, and one `RoleMatch` per `Role` in `Role` order holding the outcome, both sides' text and the side that carried more; consistent means nothing in the two names contradicts the match under the accepted outcomes, not that they name one person — 2026-11-08 #1006.
- One settings object, `Matching`, made from a `Parser` (`Parser.matching(accept=...)`), holds the accepted outcomes and the `other_readings` switch, and both `check` and `keys` are its methods, so keys built under one setting are never checked under another; `accept` replaces the default rather than extending it, `same` is always in it and `conflict` never can be — 2026-11-08 #1006.
- The default accepts the writing variants (`same`, `folded`, `listed-spelling`, `particles-aside`, `part-of-other`, `initial`, `initials-agree`, `one-side`, `generation-one-side`, `noted`); `familiar-form`, `family-initial`, `given-one-side`, `hyphenated-part` and `across-roles` are opt-in, and the search for other readings is a switch beside them, `Matching.other_readings` (`Parser.matching(other_readings=True)`), off by default; the first four are reported in the table either way, while `across-roles` is searched only where accepted and other readings only where the switch is on, so a setting without them reports the names as parsed (`Suharto` / `Suharto` reads family `conflict` under the default), amending the build's first call, which reported every opt-in either way — 2026-11-08 #1006, amended 2026-11-08 #1221 THE OTHER-READING SEARCH IS A SWITCH.
- The family role must be filled on both sides and agree, under every setting: by default a name with no family name is consistent with nothing and has no key, and where `other_readings` is on it reaches the others through its family reading (`Suharto` / `Suharto` consistent, key `suharto;`) — 2026-11-08 #1006, amended 2026-11-08 #1221 THE OTHER-READING SEARCH IS A SWITCH.
- In the suffix role a generation on one side only reads `generation-one-side` whatever credentials either side carries, credentials reading `noted` only where the generations agree — 2026-11-08 #1006.
- `DEFAULT_ACCEPT` stays private; the default is extended through a matcher, `parser.matching(accept=parser.matching().accept | {...})` — 2026-11-08 #1006.
- The check is symmetric (`check(b, a)` is `check(a, b).mirrored()`) and not transitive; a direction is read off `RoleMatch.more`, never a second call — 2026-11-08 #1006.
- Keys follow the accepted outcomes and the `other_readings` switch, with the guarantee that two names the check calls consistent share a key: one `word;g` key per folded family-base word (and each half of a hyphenated one beside its joined and its whole form, a half that is a listed particle left out), `g` the given name's first letter, each word under every word of its spelling set folded, the given letter likewise — 2026-11-08 #1006, amended 2026-11-08 #1221 LISTED SPELLINGS ARE READ PER ROLE.
- A listed spelling is read per role, in the `same_as` of the readings of the kind that stands there: given names (and bound given names) for the given name, middle name and nickname, surnames (and particles) for the family and maiden names, titles for the title, suffixes for the suffix. Two words are a listed spelling where their spelling sets meet, a word's set being every word reachable from it through that group's links, read as written and folded, each member beside its folded form; every full form a word carries in a group is kept, and a loop is all its words (`Ma, Jack` / `María, Jack` conflicts, `Ma. Cruz` / `María Cruz` reads given `listed-spelling`); a word whose name reading's `same_as` stands beside a name reading of the same kind with none in the lexicon's own vocabulary, a name of its own too (the derived marker `SAME_AS_NAMED`; its members on 2026-11-08 are in the entry this line points at), reaches its links only written with its trailing period, so `Jo Garcia` / `José Garcia` conflicts under the default — 2026-11-08 #1221 LISTED SPELLINGS ARE READ PER ROLE; amended 2026-11-08 A LISTED SPELLING OF A NAME OF ITS OWN NEEDS ITS PERIOD.
- `Outcome.strength` ranks the outcomes by declaration order, larger stronger and `conflict` lowest, `<` on an `Outcome` comparing names; `MatchResult.weakest` is the weakest row's outcome by it, and `MatchResult[role]` reads a row by `Role` or its name, the rows validated as one per `Role` in `Role` order — 2026-11-08 #1221 `Outcome.strength`.
- The fold drops case, full stops, apostrophes (the typed `'`, the typographic U+2019 and the modifier letter U+02BC, so `O’Brien, Pat` / `Pat O'Brien` reads family `folded` and both key `obrien;p`, and since 2026-11-09 the okina U+02BB, the left quote U+2018 and the backtick, three more of Uzbek Latin's spellings of `ʻ`) and a combining mark on a Latin, Greek or Cyrillic letter, and keeps a mark on any other script's letter, the result recomposed (NFC) so a key is precomposed text, a Hangul given letter `김;민` and never the conjoining jamo `김;ᄆ` — 2026-11-08 #1006, amended 2026-11-09 #1272 THE FOLD DROPS THE OKINA, THE LEFT QUOTE AND THE BACKTICK TOO.
- `part-of-other` compares the two family bases, particles excluded, a hyphen half that is a listed particle included (`Ahmed Al` / `Ahmed Al-Saud` conflicts), so a family of particles alone is part of nothing — 2026-11-08 #1006.
- Other readings are searched only where `other_readings` is on and the names as parsed are not consistent, one ambiguity per side at a time, never into the title or suffix; the search is a switch, not an `Outcome`, so no row reads it and `Outcome.strength` ranks only outcomes a row can read: each row keeps its own, and `MatchResult.left_alternative` and `right_alternative` name the ambiguity whose other reading each side used; an `accept` holding `"other-reading"` raises `ValueError` offering `parser.matching(other_readings=True)` — 2026-11-08 #1006, amended 2026-11-08 #1221 THE OTHER-READING SEARCH IS A SWITCH.
- A familiar form is a short given name that stands for longer ones without being a spelling of them (`Max` for Maximilian and Maxim, `Bill` for William, `Sepp` for Josef): the opt-in outcome `familiar-form`, ranked below every default outcome and above every other opt-in, reported in the table where it holds whether or not it is accepted, read in the given name, middle name and nickname, word by word beside a word the same or a listed spelling, never in the family or maiden name nor across roles, and read as a pair listed directly, whichever way the source wrote it and never chained: two longer names paired with one short name (`Maximilian` / `Maxim`, each paired with `Max`) do not reach each other. It is read from `familiar` readings, a kind of its own whose `short_for` compiles into `FAMILIAR`, which no parse reads, seeded from nam_dict and from a reviewed selection of the nicknames list (2026-11-09 THE NICKNAMES LIST), and keyed where accepted by the first letter of every name a word of the given name's spelling set is paired with, either way, so the guarantee holds; a pair is found from either name written with or without its accents. A row reports the strongest outcome that holds and is accepted, and where none holding is accepted, the strongest that holds. A caller's `add(w, kind="familiar", short_for=[...])` extends the word's names in a reading of its own; a replace is `remove` then `add` — 2026-11-08 FAMILIAR FORMS; amended 2026-11-09 THE NICKNAMES LIST.
- `across-roles` is searched only where it is accepted, counts only a full name on both sides (`same`, `folded`, `listed-spelling`), and consumes the middle word it uses; its keys are cut only from the cross-role texts the check could match (the check's own initials test), so a key part exists only where a match can — 2026-11-08 #1006, amended 2026-11-08 #1221 THE ACROSS-ROLES KEYS ASK THE CHECK'S INITIALS TEST.

Elsewhere: `matches()` and `comparison_key()` stay exact, one casefolded component per field (decisions/comparison-surface, whose Declined line on lenient matching this file's entry supersedes in part).
