# H9 — the title phrase

## Standing

- A title that may be a given name is a title only inside a title phrase, and elsewhere reads as a word the vocabulary does not list: `Dean Smith` reads given 'Dean', `Dean of Students Jane Smith` title 'Dean of Students' — 2026-10-25 #752.
- The fork is reported `title-or-name` where the word is the first name word after any titles and no comma has marked the given name: `Dean Smith` and `Dean Smith, MD` report, `SMITH, DEAN MR.` does not — 2026-10-26 #752, phase 2i (Derek). Only where a phrase the vocabulary lists holds the word: `Grace Smith` reports nothing, `Marshal Yanda` does — 2026-10-26 #752, phase 2i, the report's reach (Derek).
- `grace` (a style) and `marshal` (a rank) are titles that may be given names, `sergeant` attaches to the word `major` and `major` to `general` (`attaches` naming a word where a kind would reach too far): `His Grace John Smith`, `Field Marshal Bernard Montgomery` and `Sergeant Major John Smith` read the title; `Mr Justice Smith` keeps given 'Justice', no exception behind a salutation — 2026-10-26 #752, phase 2i (Derek).
- Its partner is a modifier or a style before it, a title before it that attaches to it or a title after it that it attaches to (`attaches`), or `of` after it; a salutation is never one, nor any other title before it (#853's `Mr Dean Smith`), nor a title after it it does not attach to (`SMITH, DEAN MR.`) — 2026-10-25 #752.
- A modifier in the leading run of titles before the first comma is a title only with a head title after it in that run, `of` after it, or in the object of `of` or `the` there; outside that run (ending the name, H5's, or after a comma) it reads as any title does, the given part after a family comma included (`Lau, Wing` → title 'Wing') — 2026-10-25 #752.
- A title that may be a given name which a caller lists in `titles` is a title wherever it stands, which keeps the 1.4 recipe `c.titles.add("dean")`; a v1 caller who empties `titles` turns the phrase reading off with them, while one who replaces the list keeps it (`c.titles.clear(); c.titles.add('dr')` still reads `Dean of Students` as the title), v1 holding no knob for the set — 2026-10-25 #752.

  - THE RULE. A contested word some phrase in `title_phrases` holds can head a title of itself, so a reader of `Dean Smith` can hesitate; a word no listed phrase holds is a title only behind a style or a modifier (`His Grace`), so standing alone it is no title anyone hesitates over, the test AGENTS.md sets for a reportable fork. The question is asked of the phrases `attaches` compiles (`_vocab.in_a_title_phrase`), so no property was added, and only of the phrases classify can read, two words each a title or a title that may be a given name: a caller's `your grace`, whose `your` is no title, makes `Grace Smith` report nothing (the review of this change). The review also measured the first version splitting every phrase per call, 175 frames more on each name opening with `Grace` (344 against 169); the words are now cached per vocabulary.
  - WHICH WORDS FALL WHERE (the default vocabulary, 2026-10-26). Report: `dean`, `justice` and `marshal`, each held by a phrase only because `chief` attaches to their kinds (office, military: `chief dean`, `chief justice`, `chief marshal`), and `major`, which attaches to `general` and which `sergeant`, `sgt` and `chief` attach to. Silent: `grace`, a style, held by none, since no title attaches to the style kind. The partners the rule reads but `title_phrases` does not hold — a modifier or style before the word, `of` after it — are the same for every contested word, so they cannot tell one word from another, and the derivation does not read them. Its consequence: a word reports only while a phrase holds it, so narrowing `chief` to a list of words, or a caller's `Lexicon` with no phrases, silences the report for the words it drops.
