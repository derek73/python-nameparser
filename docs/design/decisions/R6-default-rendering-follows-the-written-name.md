# R6 — the default rendering follows the written name

## Standing

- The default `str()` and `render()` lay a name out in `ParsedName.order`, the order it was read in, and keep the separator written between two words side by side, in the same order, both as written and as laid out, normalized by kind; a family comma is never kept, and a comma between the family and its suffix is (decisions/vocabulary-model's #754 output-model entry, item 6; the never-kept family comma narrowed to one between name words by the A SUFFIX COMMA entry below) — 2026-10-28 #754 (Derek).
- `render(spec: str | None = None)`: `None` is the order-aware default, and a caller's spec renders exactly as written, so passing 2.x's default spec keeps given-first output (decisions/3-0-reevaluations, THE RENDER SIGNATURE) — 2026-10-28 #754 (Derek).
- A comma with only a suffix after it stays as a suffix comma, whatever comma the parse recorded, so `Smith, Jr.` renders 'Smith, Jr.' — 2026-10-29 #754 (Derek), A SUFFIX COMMA.
- Two words are side by side as written when they are consecutive in `ParsedName.tokens`; a comma between two name words is the family comma, dropped also where P1's fold leaves its pair side by side; credentials written apart keep the suffix field's comma join — 2026-10-29 #754.

Open: whether a comma between two titles written after a comma, which the layout moves to the front, is kept, as the decided text's "one comma the parse reads" keeps it today, though it reads back as a family comma (`John Smith, Jr., Dr., Prof.` → 'Dr., Prof. John Smith, Jr.', and with one comma `John Smith Dr., Prof.` → 'Dr., Prof. John Smith'), or dropped, keeping a comma only before a suffix; rules/R6 records today's reading as Accepted. Also: how a pack's CLDR-shaped render pattern is reached, (a) through a convention recorded on the name or (b) through the parser (decisions/vocabulary-model's #754 render-patterns entry); this rule reads `order` alone and forecloses neither.

- 2026-10-28 #754 (Derek) — DECIDED, as item 6 of decisions/vocabulary-model's #754 output-model entry, which holds the reasons and the measurements: the default rendering follows the order a name was read in and keeps its written separators in a normalized form, through `render(spec: str | None = None)`, a family-first layout keeping the title first and the suffix last. This file records the rule that decision made, rules/R6.
- 2026-10-29 #754 — IMPLEMENTED (sub-phase 4e). `ParsedName.order` is the order assign read the name under, `GIVEN_FIRST` after a family comma and the declared `name_order` where no positional read happened; it carries `ADDED_IN = "3.0.0"`, so a 2.x pickle loads with `None` and renders exactly as 2.x did, by 2.x's default spec, separators not kept (the decided "a 2.x pickle rendering as today"; a first build laid such a name out given-first but kept its separators, so a 2.8.0 pickle of `John Smith, Jr.` rendered 'John Smith, Jr.' where 2.8.0 gave 'John Smith Jr.', found by review), and it is left out of `==` and `comparison_key()`, validated as `Policy.name_order` is. Where the decided text left a choice, the reading taken: two words are side by side as written when they are consecutive in `ParsedName.tokens`, so a marker word the parse dropped, or a field `replace()` spliced in, leaves the pair apart and takes the layout's joiner; the family comma, which the parse records nowhere on the name, is told by its neighbours, a comma between two name words being one, which drops it also after P1's fold (`Smith, de Mesnil Jean` → 'Smith de Mesnil Jean', a rendering read back given-first, the decided text's cost); credentials written apart keep the suffix field's comma join (`John Smith, MD, PhD` → 'John Smith, MD, PhD') and credentials written side by side keep their space (`John Smith MD PhD` → 'John Smith MD PhD'), as the suffix field already joined them (rules/R1); and a comma between two titles written after a comma, which the layout moves to the front, is kept, as the decided text reads, at the cost of its read-back, left open above. A first build kept a comma only before a suffix, a narrowing of the decided text a review found to depart from it in that title shape alone, so it was withdrawn rather than recorded; the second review found the shape's reach wider than first written, a one-comma name reaching it too (`John Smith Dr., Prof.`, its old rendering 'Dr. Prof. John Smith' reading back exactly), in no corpus entry but in every one of 108 two-part renderings of a generated grid that keep such a comma. Measured 2026-10-29 over the committed corpus's 6,556 distinct entries (`snapshot.corpus_entries()`), each under its declared order, against `9210f221` in a detached worktree, both `nameparser.__file__` paths asserted, each entry's `str()`, `initials()` and `parse(str(name)).as_dict()` taken with the same parser in each tree and compared: `str()` moves for 1,034 entries (543 of them only in `corpus_cldr.jsonl`), 548 laid out family-first again (497 declared `FAMILY_FIRST`, 2 `FAMILY_FIRST_GIVEN_LAST`, 49 by script order), 241 keeping a written nothing, dot or full-width comma between two words, 231 keeping a comma (before a suffix, or between two titles), 13 both of the last two, and one an Arabic comma before a suffix kept where the suffix field had joined with an ASCII one; `initials()` moves for 545 (97 only in `corpus_cldr.jsonl`), every one a Han, Hangul or kana initial dropped (rules/R7); `parse(str(name))` reads back differently for 1,318 entries on the base and 491 on this change, the 4 failing now that read back on the base being the title-comma shape (a count over the corpus, whose one one-comma instance is rules/R6's own example). - 2026-10-29 #754 (Derek), A SUFFIX COMMA — A COMMA WITH ONLY A SUFFIX AFTER IT STAYS AS A SUFFIX COMMA, WHATEVER STRUCTURE THE PARSE RECORDED. A review found that rules/R6's Except, then reading "A family comma is never kept", was contradicted by the shipped code where the part after a family comma holds suffixes alone: `Smith, Jr.` (read with a family comma) renders 'Smith, Jr.', as do `Smith, MD`, `John, Jr.` and `Smith, Jr., John`, which keeps both its commas ('Smith, Jr., John', the parse reading `John` as a second suffix entry). The code stays and the rule is narrowed: the family comma never kept is one between name words, and a comma before a suffix is a suffix comma for the rendering, as the decided text keeps the comma between the family and its suffix. Rejected: dropping the comma by the parse's recorded structure, which `ParsedName` does not carry and which would render `Smith, Jr.` as 'Smith Jr.'. Not a render question: `KIM, AE-RA` keeps its comma ('KIM, AE-RA') because the parse reads `AE-RA` as a credential (suffix 'AE-RA', family 'KIM'), where a reader sees the Korean given name Ae-ra; that is the parse's reading to revisit, and the rendering follows it.
