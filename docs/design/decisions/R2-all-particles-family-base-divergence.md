# R2 — the all-particles family_base divergence

## Standing

- A family part whose every word is particle vocabulary is a part where none of them is doing a particle's work, so its words read as ordinary name words: they anchor the base and leave the particles view, position deciding and whether a word is borne as a surname not entering into it (`Anh Do` → family_base 'Do', family_particles ''; `Juan van der` → family_base 'van der') — 2026-08-18 #404/#385.
- So a non-empty family always has a non-empty base (`Del Toro` under FAMILY_FIRST → family_base 'Del'), and the base and particles views partition the family, asserted over the whole case table — 2026-08-18 #404/#385.
- The words keep their `particle` tag, which means "from the particle vocabulary wherever it lands", and the decision is recorded beside it as UNJOINED_TAG rather than by stripping the tag — 2026-08-18 #404/#385.
- `initials()` reads the base, so the anchoring moves R3's output: `Anh Do` initials 'A. D.' and `Juan van der` 'J. v. d.' — 2026-08-16 (pre-merge coherence pass); 2026-08-18 #404/#385.
- A particle run that joins nothing still chains whole into one part, so `Juan Smith van der` reads family 'van der' and middle 'Smith'; splitting it was declined, roles reassembling adjacent same-role pieces into one part and the base losing its full extent — 2026-08-18.
- The base is the family without its working particles, wherever in the family they stand (`da Silva do Amaral de Souza, Miguel` → family_base 'Silva Amaral Souza', family_particles 'da do de'), a view the same under every convention and no culture's sort key — 2026-10-28 #754.
- The unjoined mark is a statement about a whole part, and `initials()` honors it for every word in it, a connective included: under `Lexicon.default().add(particles={'y'})`, `Juan de y` reads family_base 'de y' and initials 'J. d. y.'; narrowing the readmission to the `particle` tag alone was tried and backed out — 2026-08-29 #461; 2026-09-20 #461.

Elsewhere: where a connective initials, a part holding nothing else for it to join (`Jon Dough and` → 'J. D.', `Juan de y` → 'J. y.') — decisions/R3's 2026-09-20 #461 entry; case repair keeping that connective lowercase, its carve-out not gated on the mark (under `Lexicon.default().add(particles={'y'})`, `Juan de y` forced → family 'De y') — decisions/R4's 2026-08-29, the CONJUNCTION CARVE-OUT entry; case repair reading the unjoined mark for the whole part (`ANH DO` → 'Anh Do') — decisions/R4's 2026-08-29 #407 entry; the snapshot recording `initials()`, so a change to the base is visible there — decisions/differential-ledger's 2026-10-13 #664, a committed snapshot replaces the classified gate entry.

  Why the base is not a sort key, researched 2026-10-28. Portuguese and Brazilian practice files a name under its LAST surname, that surname's particle removed and set after the given names, the agnomens `Filho`, `Neto` and `Júnior` staying with the surname before them and an established compound surname kept whole: `SOUZA, Miguel da Silva do Amaral de`, `NOGUEIRA NETO, Wanderlino` (ABNT NBR 6023:2018, https://engenhariaeletrica.ufes.br/sites/engenhariaeletrica.ufes.br/files/field/anexo/abnt6023.2018.pdf; the ABNT Zotero style's comments, https://www.zotero.org/styles/universidade-federal-do-espirito-santo-abnt?source=1; USP's references manual, https://www.ip.usp.br/site/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Manual-Referencias-ABNT-SabadiniSampaioNascimento.pdf; Cambridge University Library, https://www.lib.cam.ac.uk/node/3142; the UN cataloguing manual, https://www.un.org/Depts/dhl/unbisref_manual/nameguide/personal.htm; IFLA, Names of Persons: Portugal, 2009, https://ifla.org/files/assets/cataloguing/pubs/ifla_names_of_persons_portugal_2009.pdf). Spanish practice files under the FIRST surname, the second its secondary key (RAE, Ortografía de la lengua española, 2010, ch. VII, "Alfabetización de antropónimos", https://www.rae.es/ortografía/alfabetización-de-antropónimos, read 2026-10-28 in a browser, the page refusing a plain fetch): by its rule (a) a preposition, with or without an article, heading the FIRST surname is left out of the order and written after the given name (`Torre Ibarra, Ramón de la`); by rule (e) one heading the SECOND surname counts as part of the second key (`Gómez Casas`, `Gómez de la Iglesia`, `Gómez de Lara`, `Gómez del Castillo`, `Gómez Echagüe`, in that order); by rule (b) a leading article or `San`/`Santa` stays and is sorted on (`La Calle Ruiz, Carmen`); by rule (d) a `y` between the surnames counts (`Aguirre y Gómez-Pacheco`). The same page states the Portuguese convention from the other side, the paternal second surname being the axis. Neither convention's key is this base: Portuguese practice sorts `…da Silva de Souza do Amaral` before `…da Silva do Amaral de Souza` (Amaral before Souza) while both bases begin `Silva`, and Spanish practice keeps a second surname's particle (`Gómez de la Iglesia`) where the base drops it ('Gómez Iglesia'). The base dropping every working particle is nearest the Spanish first key alone. So the base is the convention-neutral view, and a convention's key is built from the surnames by whoever knows the convention, the RFC's Phase 4 Left open. In the default policy, `Miguel da Silva do Amaral de Souza` already reads family 'de Souza', base 'Souza', which is the Portuguese entry element by coincidence: only `middle_as_family` gives the long family.
