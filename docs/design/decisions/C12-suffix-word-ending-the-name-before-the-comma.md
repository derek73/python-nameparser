# C12 — a suffix word ending the name before the comma

- 2026-10-18 (Derek), [#656](https://github.com/derek73/python-nameparser/issues/656) — A SUFFIX WORD ENDING A WHOLE NAME BEFORE THE COMMA IS EVIDENCE THAT THE COMMA IS A SUFFIX COMMA, AND THE PART AFTER IT DECIDES ONLY WHETHER IT HOLDS A NAME. Until this rule only the part after the comma decided (C1), so an unlisted credential there that no shape rule admitted reversed the whole name into the listing form: `John Smith II, AD` read given 'AD', family 'John Smith'. Derek's ground, in the #720/#656 session: a generation is part of the name, more so than a credential, and is commonly written before the comma that starts the credentials (`John J Ingram III, MD`), and `II` counts as such a word though it is also initial-shaped. The evidence is the suffix WORD (`vocab:suffix-word`: every member of `suffix_words`, 170 on 2026-10-18 by `len(Lexicon.default().suffix_words)` — the generations, `esq`, `ret`, `vet`, `2`, and the honorifics, `rinpoche`, जी, 先生, 씨 among them), not an acronym, because a listing with a long surname puts an acronym there as readily (`von bergen wessels MD, pennie`, a corpus.jsonl row, keeps its given name).
  DECLINED: the generation alone deciding. CLDR's own renderings hold the counter-case, a two-word surname with a generation before a listing comma: `Excm. Sr. Lloret Palol II, Josep Antoni Carles Joan` and `Excma. Dra. Van Dalen Hoogeveen II, Maria da Graça César Martín`, both keep the listing form and their given names before and after this rule (their title and family fields disagree with CLDR for other reasons, the title run left in the family). So the part after the comma still decides, by a narrower question than C1's: a word in it written as a name (`_vocab.written_as_a_name`, unlisted, no period) or an initial keeps the listing form. That needs the name's case contrast, which is C6's test (`_comma._name_contrast`) reused rather than restated; without it a given name written in capitals is indistinguishable from `AD`, so a one-case name and a caseless script decline (the Arabic, Gujarati, Kannada and Malayalam CLDR renderings of this shape stay as they read).
  THE NUMERALS, found by the sweep before review. The evidence is reached by vocabulary AND by shape: `ii`–`iv`, `v` and `i` carry the suffix-word tag, but `VI` to `X` are numerals by shape alone, so a tag-only test read `John Smith III, AD` and left `John Smith VI, AD` in the listing form, #610's class. The numeral path asks `_vocab.is_trailing_numeral_suffix`, the trailing fork's own predicate, behind a C-level `ROMAN.match` filter in `decide` so the listing form pays no frame. Both paths then count the words IN FRONT of the generation as C3 does (`_whole_name(seg0[:-1])`): `Smith VI` is one word and a numeral, as `Smith III` is, where counting the whole part had made the shape-only numeral its own name word. And a one-letter numeral is evidence only where S10 reads it as the generation, a name word to spare in front of it (`name_word_count`, a title being none): `Smith V, AD` and `St. Berg V, AD` read the V as a name word and keep the listing form.
  THE HONORIFICS, found by the design-docs review: the tag reaches every suffix word, so `Tenzin Gyatso Rinpoche, AD` reads suffix 'Rinpoche, AD' where the parent read given 'AD'. Kept as stated rather than narrowed to generations: an honorific written after the name stands in the same place a generation does, and no vocabulary set separates the generations from the rest. Dotted paired initials were the review's other find: `Smith Jones Jr., J.A.` read suffix 'Jr., J.A.', since the veto asked only the `initial` tag; `is_paired_initials` joins it, as C5 keeps such a pair the given name.
  DECLINED: adding `mbchb` to SUFFIX_ACRONYMS, approved in the session before the rule was measured. `MBChB` ends in a capital, so it is not written as a name and the rule reads `van den Berg jnr., MBChB PhD` without it; a vocabulary member would also reach `Smith, MBChB`, which nothing here measured.
  Measured 2026-10-18 on this change against its parent: five corpus names move, every one a CLDR rendering of the shape and each to the agreeing reading on family, given, suffix and title where CLDR states them (`Sig. Fabio Massimo Carlo Alberto Martini II, AD` in both orders, `prof. dr. Ada Cornelia César Martín von Brühl Jr, univ. bacc`, `Prof. dr. Cornelia Margarita Sofia Aletta van den Berg jnr., MBChB PhD`, `Skeggjason Hr. Geir Jón Magnús Brynjar yngri, þingmaður`); the last still disagrees on its given names, CLDR's `Geir Jón Magnús Brynjar` against `Hr. Geir Jón Magnús Brynjar`, a title inside a surname-first name, which is not this rule. No other corpus name moves, read off `snapshot.py`'s report. The issue's comment counted 25 disagreements of the shape on 2026-10-13; the `Jr, MP` renderings among them read correctly on the parent already, MP being listed.
- 2026-10-19 (Derek), PR #735's altitude review — A TITLE P3 JOINS READS AS A TITLE IN THE PART, AS A SINGLE TITLE WORD DOES. The rule's title test was a hand copy of C8's that asked only a one-word piece's vocabulary tag, so a joined title (P3's piece tag `title`) fell to the suffix side: `John Smith Jr., Mr. and Mrs. AD` read suffix 'Jr., Mr. and Mrs. AD', while `John Smith, PhD Mr. and Mrs. AD` (C8) and `John Smith Jr., Mr. and Mrs.` (no credential) both read title 'Mr. and Mrs.'. `_behind_a_generation` now asks C8's predicates, `not is_suffix_piece` then `is_title_piece`, which agree with the copy on every one-word piece: a dual is still a suffix, as stated. Measured 2026-10-19 over 8 names ending in a suffix word (`Jr.`, `II`, `VI`, `V`, `III`, `Dr. Smith Jr.`, `Esq.`, `Sr.`) × 29 parts (joined titles, the duals `MD`, `Ms`, `Sr.`, `CPT`, single titles) × the three orders: 120 of 696 readings move, exactly the five joined-title parts (`Mr. and Mrs. AD`, `AD Mr. and Mrs.`, `Mr. & Mrs. AD`, `Dame and Sir AD`, `Secretary of State AD`) under every name and order, each from suffix to title; no ambiguity kind or `initials()` moves, and no corpus name moves (snapshot unchanged).
