# N4 — the alias marker

## Standing

- A word the vocabulary reads as an alias marker (the `alias_marker` kind), written as a word of its own with a name word before it, takes the words after it as the nickname and is dropped; the alias runs to the first patronymic particle, marker of either kind or comma, or with none as far as M2's clause would, and a bracketed or quoted clause led by a marker is the nickname without it — 2026-11-20 #1164.

- `@` and `urf` are markers by default; `alias` and `aka` ship written `default = false` and are turned on by a caller's `Lexicon.add(word, kind="alias_marker")` — 2026-11-20 #1164.

- A glued `X@Y`, an e-mail address and an `@handle` stay one word — 2026-11-20 #1164.

Open: whether to report the fork of two or more words with no boundary (`Rahim @ Jaki Osman`, a whole second name or a one-word alias before the family) as an ambiguity, with the one-word reading as the alternative; no kind is added for it now (#1164).

## Log

- 2026-11-20 #1164 — THE ALIAS MARKER TAKES THE WORDS AFTER IT AS THE NICKNAME. Measured first on master e0f698c8 over the local corpora (each occurrence labelled marker, name or unclear by hand): the spaced `X @ Y` is an alias marker in all 109 of its uses and a name in none; `urf` a marker in 3 of 3 (all PSC; the 2024 Lok Sabha candidates' list, no local corpus, writes it 21 times); `alias` a marker in 43 and a name in 97 (a Malay given name and father's name, a Kerala name, a US surname, FEC's `ALIAS, FRED` shape seven times); `aka` a marker in 38 and a name in 90 (an Ivorian, Turkish and Nepali given name and surname). So `@` and `urf` ship on and `alias` and `aka` off, each written with `default = false` so its vetting stays on the word. A narrower default keyed on case was declined: a lower-case `alias` or `aka` in a mixed-case name was a marker in 29 of 29, but it misses every list written in capitals, where most markers stand.
  THE MECHANISM is M2's take, shared rather than copied: `_group._clause_take` asks M2's three questions (where a marker counts, what it takes, the bound trailing run) for either marker, `_maiden_take` and `_alias_takes` calling it with their own role. What differs is where the alias stops: the measured scopes of the 109 spaced uses were 23 inside the given part ended by a patronymic particle (`X @ Y bin Z`), 31 after the father's name to the end, 26 one word to the end, 1 one word then a comma, and 29 of two or more words with no patronymic particle. M2's clause runs to the end, which would have read all 23 given-part aliases wrong, so the alias also stops at a patronymic particle, at another marker of either kind and at a comma. And it counts in the given part after a family comma too, where FEC writes `FAMILY, GIVEN AKA OTHER`, which M2's marker never reaches.
  DECLINED: taking one word after the marker, right on about 70 of the 109 and wrong on every two-word alias in the given part (4), the two- and three-word aliases after the father's name (10), the whole second names and bynames (about 26) and the Indian whole-name aliases (about 9); taking to the end as M2 does (the 23 given-part aliases); pairing word counts on either side of the marker, which no pattern supports (`Lau Chi Keong @ Thomas Lau`); reading `@Word` after a name word as a marker, which is a handle in 13 of 16 such uses; and splitting a glued `X@Y` at tokenize, an alias in 15 uses and no name in 8 (an e-mail address that lost its dot, a team code).
  THE FORK: with two or more words after the marker and no boundary among them, the alias takes them all: 17 of the spaced uses are a whole second name (`Tan Wei Keong @ Daniel Tan`) and 10 a one-word alias before the family name, and in the Indian `alias` uses about 9 against 2. The one-word reading loses its family name to the nickname (N4's Accepted). Reporting it is left Open.
  ORDER: the alias takes run before the maiden take, the alias stopping at a maiden marker, so `Jane Smith @ Janey née Jones` and `Jane Smith née Jones @ JJ` both read nickname and maiden apart.
