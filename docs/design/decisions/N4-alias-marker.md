# N4 — the alias marker

## Standing

- A word the vocabulary reads as an alias marker (the `alias_marker` kind), written as a word of its own with a name word before it, takes the words after it as the nickname and is dropped; the alias runs to the first patronymic particle or marker of either kind in its part, or with none to the end of the part as far as M2's clause would, and a bracketed or quoted clause led by a marker is the nickname without it — 2026-11-20 #1164.

- `@` and `urf` are markers by default; `alias` and `aka` ship written `default = false` and are turned on by a caller's `Lexicon.add(word, kind="alias_marker")` — 2026-11-20 #1164.

- A glued `X@Y`, an e-mail address and an `@handle` stay one word — 2026-11-20 #1164.

- One name word beside an alias keeps O5's reading, and N3 counts a delimited nickname only; a clause in a maiden pair led by an alias marker is the nickname — 2026-11-20 #1164, THE NEIGHBOURING RULES.

Open: reporting the fork as an ambiguity, left open by the decision on #1164; it needs an issue of its own.

## Log

- 2026-11-20 #1164 — THE ALIAS MARKER TAKES THE WORDS AFTER IT AS THE NICKNAME. Measured first on master e0f698c8 over the local corpora (each occurrence labelled marker, name or unclear by hand): the spaced `X @ Y` is an alias marker in all 109 of its uses and a name in none; `urf` a marker in 3 of 3 (all PSC; the 2024 Lok Sabha candidates' list, no local corpus, writes it 21 times); `alias` a marker in 43 and a name in 97 (a Malay given name and father's name, a Kerala name, a US surname, FEC's `ALIAS, FRED` shape seven times); `aka` a marker in 38 and a name in 90 (an Ivorian, Turkish and Nepali given name and surname). So `@` and `urf` ship on and `alias` and `aka` off, each written with `default = false` so its vetting stays on the word. A narrower default keyed on case was declined: a lower-case `alias` or `aka` in a mixed-case name was a marker in 29 of 29, but it misses every list written in capitals, where most markers stand.
  HOW IT WAS COUNTED: every whitespace word of every local corpus's `corpus.jsonl` (all 18, under the tools' cache) equal to `@`, `urf`, `alias`, `aka`, `a.k.a.` or `a/k/a` after stripping brackets and commas, case-folded, and every word holding `@`, sorted by how the `@` is written; each match labelled marker, name or unclear by hand, and the labels not kept, so the figures are a dated hand judgment over that extraction. The Lok Sabha figures are #1164's own comment, from a list no local corpus holds.
  THE MECHANISM is M2's take, shared rather than copied: `_group._clause_take` asks M2's three questions (where a marker counts, what it takes, the bound trailing run) for either marker, `_maiden_take` and `_alias_takes` calling it with their own role. What differs is where the alias stops: the scopes labelled over the spaced uses were 23 inside the given part ended by a patronymic particle (`X @ Y bin Z`), 31 after the father's name to the end, 26 one word to the end, 1 one word then a comma, and 29 of two or more words with no patronymic particle (110 labels for 109 uses: one label too many, which the unkept labels cannot settle). M2's clause runs to the end, which would have read all 23 given-part aliases wrong, so the alias also stops at a patronymic particle, at another marker of either kind and at a comma. And it counts in the given part after a family comma too, where FEC writes `FAMILY, GIVEN AKA OTHER`, which M2's marker never reaches.
  DECLINED: taking one word after the marker, right on the one-word aliases and wrong on every longer one: the two-word aliases in the given part (4), the two- and three-word aliases after the father's name (10) and the whole second names of the fork below (17), and in the Indian `alias` uses the whole-name aliases (about 9); taking to the end as M2 does (the 23 given-part aliases); pairing word counts on either side of the marker, which no pattern supports (`Lau Chi Keong @ Thomas Lau`); reading `@Word` after a name word as a marker, which is a handle in 13 of 16 such uses; and splitting a glued `X@Y` at tokenize, an alias in 15 uses and no name in 8 (an e-mail address that lost its dot, a team code).
  THE FORK: with two or more words after the marker and no boundary among them, the alias takes them all: 17 of the spaced uses are a whole second name (`Tan Wei Keong @ Daniel Tan`) and 10 a one-word alias before the family name, and in the Indian `alias` uses about 9 against 2. The one-word reading loses its family name to the nickname (N4's Accepted). Reporting it is left Open.
  THE NEIGHBOURING RULES: N3 reads a lone word beside a delimited nickname as the family name, and an alias leaves it to O5 instead (`Osman @ Jaki` given 'Osman', reported; `Osman (Jaki)` family 'Osman'): N3's premise, a person set down by surname, does not hold of the Malay and Indian names that write an alias marker, which mostly hold no family name. And a clause led by an alias marker in a pair the caller declared maiden reads as the nickname, the marker saying which field it means, as M3's marker overrides a nickname pair.
  THE GIVEN PART AFTER A FAMILY COMMA (code review): the alias first stopped there at the first suffix word, so a trailing title, a bare salutation (H8) and a credential its comma-free twin reads out stayed in the nickname (`Smith, John @ Jack Prof.`, `Doe, Jane @ Janey Mrs`). It now reads the end of the part by the clause-free view as the part holding the family name does, with H8's salutations, every word after the alias's first offered to it. Three shapes the given part's own walk reads as the suffix stay in the nickname, a one-letter numeral, an unlisted dotted run and a credential in a one-case name: the disagreement between `tail_reading` and that walk the root AGENTS.md's gotcha records for every group-side predictor of the given part, accepted here as there rather than met by a second predictor (N4's Accepted). And a maiden clause written before the alias is left out of the alias's view, so `Jane née Jones @ JJ V` and `Jane @ JJ née Jones V` both keep `V` in the maiden name, as `Jane née Jones V` does.
  ORDER: the alias takes run before the maiden take, the alias stopping at a maiden marker, so `Jane Smith @ Janey née Jones` and `Jane Smith née Jones @ JJ` both read nickname and maiden apart.
